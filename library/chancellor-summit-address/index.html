Chancellor Christian's Address to the 7th Annual CPL Summit

Chancellor Sonya Christian of the California Community Colleges opened the 7th Annual CPL Summit with three ideas for credit for prior learning. The address runs five minutes. Captions play by default, and the full transcript follows the video.

Transcript

Chancellor Sonya Christian speaks to the camera throughout; the video carries no slides or on-screen text.

0:00 Good morning, California. It is such a great day to be a community college champion. I want to share three ideas with you today. Three ideas that can fundamentally change higher education as we know it. Three ideas that can serve every working Californian.

0:19 Idea one: recognize what people know. Imagine a veteran who has spent eight years operating sophisticated technology, leading teams and solving complex problems, and then walks into a community college and is told, "Welcome, start from the beginning." Really? Or an electrician with 15 years of experience. Why should they have to prove their knowledge by sitting through courses covering what they already know? We at the California Community Colleges must recognize what people know.

0:54 Today, more than 55,000 students, including nearly 30,000 veterans, have been documented in our CPL infrastructure with faculty-approved credit opportunities. So we are already recognizing what people know. And as AI transforms jobs and careers, people will continually acquire new skills in new settings. Our colleges must be ready to recognize that learning wherever it happens.

1:26 Idea number two: give students back their time. Time is perhaps the most precious resource our students have. For a working parent, one less course means more evenings with your children. For a veteran, one less semester means an earlier start to a new career.

1:46 Our CPL Initiative projects approximately $390 million in immediate student savings and over $1.5 billion in economic impact over 20 years. Those are impressive numbers, but behind every dollar saved is something we cannot put a price on: a student's time. So let's give students back their time. And when we shorten the distance between what someone already knows and the credential they need, we accelerate economic mobility for individuals, for families, and for communities.

2:26 Idea number three: make CPL a standard part of the student experience across all 116 colleges. You see, now comes our next big challenge. How do we take CPL to scale? Imagine a student applying to college and being welcomed with these words: "Congratulations! Your prior learning has already earned you 15 college credits toward a program of study. Complete just one more course, and you qualify for a Certificate of Achievement, and you are already on your way to an Associate's degree."

3:08 Imagine technology identifying those opportunities, and our faculty validating the learning, and our counselors guiding students forward. Now imagine making this the standard student experience across all of our colleges. California has invested another $42 million in the 2026-27 budget to help build this future, this dream. We have the resources, we have the faculty expertise, and we have the momentum. Now we need the systems and the commitment to bring this opportunity to every student. So let's just do it. Make CPL a standard part of the student experience across all 116 colleges. Our goal is to reach 250,000 Californians through CPL by 2030.

4:11 So as you gather for this seventh Summit, I hope these three ideas stay with you. Recognize what people know, give students back their time, and make CPL a standard part of the student experience. To our faculty, counselors, classified professionals, college leaders, and partners who have brought us this far, a big thank you. We have a remarkable opportunity to change how California values learning and values experience. After all, the California Community Colleges, we take care of our students, we take care of our communities, and we take care of our planet. Our time is now.
