CPL Initiative · Preliminary scan for the ELL Healthcare Pathways grantees · November 9, 2026

Where credit for ELL Healthcare Pathways training could start

The English Language Learner Healthcare Pathways Program funds California Adult Education Program consortia to build short, accelerated healthcare training for adult English learners. It is a $130 million one-time appropriation in the Budget Act of 2022-23, awarded by the California Community Colleges Chancellor's Office with the Department of Education, the Labor and Workforce Development Agency and the Health and Human Services Agency. Round Three funds 20 consortia from July 1, 2026 through June 30, 2029, each building a Center for Innovation and Impact in Health Careers.

Every Round Three consortium pairs a K-12 adult school with a community college district, so the college that could award credit already sits at the table. We read the eight priority occupations against the MAP platform, where California's community colleges record the credit they award for prior learning, and against each member college's courses and programs. After the Overview, a tab per region holds a card per consortium, ordered by fit. Each card names the credit that exists, the courses to examine and the people at each college who can carry the conversation forward.

The first meeting

Exploring credit for prior learning with the grantees

A grantee's completer holds a credential a college can read: a state nursing assistant certificate, an EMT card, a medical assistant or phlebotomy certification. Credit for prior learning turns that credential into units toward a certificate or degree, so the short pathway becomes the first step of a longer one. That speaks to an outcome Round Three grantees report: participants who move into a credit college course, a credential program or a degree program.

Before any match

What we still need to confirm

Which pathways each Center runs. The summary documents pathways for 7 of the 20 consortia and says its entries may not include every pathway; the other cards read all eight occupations.

Which credential a completer holds at exit, since the credential is what a college articulates. A state nursing assistant certificate, a county EMT certification, a national medical assistant or phlebotomy certification and a pharmacy technician license each map to a different exhibit.

Whether the training is already a college course. Where a member college's noncredit or continuing education division teaches the pathway, the student has a college record, and the question becomes how that noncredit course counts toward the credit program.

Each card's examples are candidates for faculty review. The faculty who teach the course decide whether the training meets its outcomes, and their review settles the units a student would receive.

The people on each card. They are the CPL contacts each college records in MAP; each consortium's project lead is not in the award memo, so the first meeting adds the grantee side.

Practical next steps

  1. Send each consortium its card, so the first conversation is between its project lead and the CPL Coordinators the card names.
  2. Ask each Center which of the eight occupations it runs and which credential its completers hold, and add both to its card.
  3. Where a member college already awards the credit, ask the consortium's other colleges to adopt it: Request Adopt on an example row opens the exhibit in MAP, and Request Quick Adopt there needs no MAP sign-in.
  4. Pick one occupation most Centers share, nursing assistant or medical assistant, and build one credit recommendation with faculty from several member colleges, so every consortium can adopt it.
  5. Record every confirmed match in MAP so the next cohort, and the next consortium, finds it there.

This is a preliminary scan, not an articulation. Program facts, the Round Two and Round Three consortia and the pathways documented come from the ELL Healthcare Pathways Rounds 2 and 3 summary provided for this meeting; each Round Three consortium's region, fiscal agent and award come from the Chancellor's Office memo WED 26-42 (May 19, 2026), and the eight priority occupations from the Round Three bidder's conference (January 15, 2026). Each card names the CPL Coordinator its college records in MAP, or where it records none the first Faculty Leads listed there, read November 9, 2026.
