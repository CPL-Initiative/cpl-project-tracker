CPL Initiative · Preliminary scan for EDD's ELL IET Fund grantees · October 26, 2026

Where credit for ELL IET training could start

On July 3, 2023 the Employment Development Department awarded $17.97 million to eight organizations under the English Language Learner Integrated Education and Training Fund. Each project trains English learners toward a credential and a job. We read the eight project summaries against the MAP platform, where California's community colleges record the credit they award for prior learning, and against the state's program inventory, to see where a project's completers could already turn their training into college credit, and where a first articulation would start.

A card per project, ordered by fit: credit that already exists at a college in the project's own counties or at a partner college comes first.

$17.97 millionAwarded July 3, 2023 to eight organizations; funding decisions are final
8 projectsAdult schools, workforce boards, a school district, a consortium and two nonprofits, across 20 counties
3,032 participantsThe seven projects that state an enrollment target; Corona-Norco states regional demand instead
16 credential familiesFrom nursing assistant to wildland fire, matched against MAP and COCI

Before any match

What we still need to confirm

Which credential each project's completers actually hold at exit: the summaries name training programs and industry credentials, and the credential is what a college articulates. The state nursing assistant certificate, a Clinical Medical Assistant certification, a Child Development Permit or an NWCG wildland fire card each map to a different exhibit.

Whether a project's adult school or ROP partner holds a credit-by-exam or articulation agreement with its nearest college already; several high school and ROP articulations in MAP began that way.

Each card's examples are candidates for faculty review. A row pairs the training with a course the college already teaches, and the faculty who teach that course decide whether the training meets its outcomes; their review also settles the units a student would receive and whether the award is credit or noncredit.

Delivery: our data does not record whether a matched course runs online or on the CVC Exchange, so a learner far from the college would need that confirmed.

The people on each card start the conversation. The project contacts come from the July 2023 notice, so the first call confirms who leads it on each side today.

Practical next steps

  1. Send each project its card, so the first conversation is between the people it names: the project's contact, each college's CPL Coordinator and the faculty who teach the matching course.
  2. For the healthcare projects, confirm the exit credential (CNA, HHA, LVN, CMA) and compare it with the credit recommendations College of the Desert, Chaffey College and Los Angeles Pierce College already hold.
  3. For the early care apprenticeships, ask ECEPTS which Child Development Permit level each cohort reaches, then read it against the Child Development Associate exhibit the CCC Collaborative built.
  4. For Fresno, Kern and the north valley, start with the exhibits Bakersfield College, Fresno City College and Lemoore College already articulate, and invite their CPL coordinators to a shared call with the workforce boards.
  5. Record every confirmed match in MAP so the next grantee cohort finds it there.
