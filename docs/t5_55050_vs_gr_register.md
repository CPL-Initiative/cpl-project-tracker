## 2. ⚠️ The one real conflict — row #9

**Row #9 asks to strike the transcript annotation. The draft restores it.**

That is not an oversight on either side. The row was written when reciprocity
depended on colleges *voluntarily* honoring each other, and de-flagging was, as
the row says, *"the quiet lever that makes reciprocity work."* **The statute
inverted that logic**, and two provisions do it:

1. **§78093.2(b)(2)** obliges a campus to accept *"**transcribed credit for prior
   learning** from other campuses."* The duty is keyed to credit that is
   identifiable **as** CPL. Strike the annotation and the receiving college
   cannot tell which credit the duty applies to — de-flagging stops making
   reciprocity automatic and starts making it **unenforceable**.
2. **§88782(a)(1)** — the Career Passport displays *"preparation for employment,
   academic records, and **credit for prior learning**."* To display CPL
   *specifically*, something must identify it. The academic record is what
   populates the Passport.

⭐ **The register already contains the counter-argument, in row #10.** The sweep's
own entry for #10 reads: *"(b)(2) requires accepting **transcribed** CPL — which
presupposes transcription. If a campus does not transcribe, the receiving campus
has nothing to accept."* **#9 and #10 now pull against each other**, and the
statute resolves it toward #10.

⚠️ **The concern behind #9 is real and does not go away.** A source-flag that
invites adverse treatment is a genuine equity problem. What changes is the
**remedy**: not *don't record it*, but *record it and prohibit its adverse use*.

> **Recommendation — rewrite #9, keep the draft.** New shape: *"Prohibit adverse
> use of the CPL notation"* — the credit is recorded and identifiable for
> portability and the Career Passport, and districts may not treat it as inferior
> for transfer, major preparation, honors, or financial aid. If the flag itself
> must go, it belongs off the **student-facing transcript** while staying in the
> record the receiving college and the Passport read.

**This is Sam's call — it reverses a position he has held.** It is also the single
highest-value item in this comparison, because filing a §55050 package that
restores the annotation while the register asks to strike it puts two CO-facing
documents in contradiction.

## 3. ⭐ §75013 changes three rows, and the biggest is #7

§75013 is Sec. 14 of the same chapter that added Article 9. The sweep never saw it.

### #7 — apportionment. A venue now exists that did not.

Row #7 is `blast_rank 1`, `ed_first Yes`, and its consideration ends: *"SB 744
tried the statute route but appears gutted-and-amended — a gap the TBL can fill."*

**§75013(a) is a live, funded, statutory process aimed at exactly this problem:**

> *"The office of the Chancellor … shall develop recommendations for **equating
> enrollment in competency-based education programs to full-time equivalent
> students for the purposes of generating funding**. The chancellor's office shall
> convene a workgroup of experts, including experts in … **state funding models,
> workforce program funding models, cost-based models, alternate funding models,
> such as subscription-based models, and enrollment funding** … These
> recommendations shall apply to competency-based education at community college
> districts **systemwide** … On or before **October 1, 2028** … submit … to the
> **Joint Legislative Budget Committee and the Department of Finance** … These
> recommendations **shall be used to inform future funding decisions**."*

⭐ **Row #7's named obstacle is the Department of Finance and the attendance
premise. §75013(a) is a workgroup that must design a non-seat-time FTES
equivalence and report it to the Department of Finance by statute.**

⚠️ **Be precise about what this is and isn't.** §75013(a) is scoped to
**competency-based education**, not to CPL, and it produces *recommendations*,
not an apportionment method. It does **not** replace the statutory fix row #7
describes. What it gives you is a **venue and a deadline** — the one room where a
units-based, non-attendance FTES computation is already being designed, with the
right agency in it.

> **Recommendation — add a near-term pathway to #7**: get CPL, or at least
> CPL-adjacent competency assessment, into the §75013(a) workgroup's scope, and
> get the §58003.2 units engine in front of that workgroup. Cheaper than statute,
> already funded, and it lands at DOF by design. Keep the TBL ask as the durable
> instrument. **Add `EC §75013(a)` to the row's citations.**

### #6 — the "may adopt" problem is largely solved

The sweep flagged #6's weakness precisely: *"⚠️ **'may,' not 'shall'** — the
binding duty still needs Title 5."* **§75013(b) is mandatory**: the CO **shall**
make the recommendations, the amount **shall be the same systemwide**, and
colleges are **strongly encouraged to award consistent with them**, by
**2027-07-01**.

> **Recommendation:** add `EC §75013(b)`; rewrite the summary — it currently says
> *"Currently only a funding condition."* That is no longer true. This row's
> `verified_at` is null and it should be verified.

### #4 — a new, scoped argument for no local caps

The sweep found (d)(1)(A) *"equitably"* and (b)(1) *"all incoming students"*,
calling them *"not dispositive; a usable memo argument."* §75013(b) adds a
sharper one: *"The amount of credit for prior learning recommended … **shall be
the same systemwide for all community colleges**."*

**A local unit cap that truncates such an award defeats the statutory
uniformity.** ⚠️ Scoped honestly: it binds only the recommendations made under
§75013(b) — Calbright-like programs and industry certifications — not all CPL.
Still the strongest argument #4 has.

## 6. What is still uncovered by both

- **§78093.2(c)(1)(C)** — publish CPL opportunities in transfer pathways and, to
  the extent feasible, ADTs. A **system** duty, so correctly not in §55050, and
  still not a register row.
- **§78093.2(d)(2)** — the demonstrate-before-allocation gate, whose three goals
  (access · completion · career attainment) are **not** the three the funding
  model uses. The sweep flagged it; it is still unreconciled.
- **§88782(g)** — the privacy floor. The draft's (b)(4) now obliges districts to
  maintain student CPL records in a statewide system; nothing in the register or
  the draft says what protects them.
