# An incentive teaches where the finish line is

> **One-sentence summary** — colleges stopped at uploading JSTs because
> uploading JSTs is what we rewarded; any new binary we publish will become the
> new stopping point.

## Context

The CPL lifecycle runs: upload → CPL Plan generated → **disposition each credit
recommendation** → **create the articulation** → applied → in review →
transcribed. Statewide, ~81% of listed recommendations sit at *Needs Action*.
Sam's explanation, offered as a guess, turned out to be the mechanism:

> *"They often have the impression that uploading the student is the most
> important step and consider the whole process checkboxed at that point —
> perhaps because our first goal in the Vet Sprint is upload all enrolled vet
> JST, which gives them star status."*

## The claim

**A metric does not merely observe behavior; it defines where the work is
understood to end.** The Veteran Star (≥75% of enrolled veterans with a JST in
MAP) is achievable **without performing the per-CR articulation step even once**
— the JST upload creates the plan, and the DD-214/JST Basic Training rows
auto-apply against an exhibit that is already articulated system-wide. So a
college can max the reward and genuinely believe it is done.

The data carries the fingerprint: applied-CPL students ≈ JSTs uploaded, at a
ratio of **1.00** for the median college. De Anza 974 applied / 979 JSTs. San
Francisco 1,238 / 1,237. Lassen 140 / 140.

Three design consequences:

1. **Whatever you make green becomes the new finish line.** Replacing six
   checkboxes with three achieves nothing if the three are still checkboxes.
2. **Express each step as a fraction, never a check.** *"Articulate — 412 of
   1,847 CRs dispositioned"* cannot be satisfied by doing it once. A tick can.
3. **Name the grain in the label.** If the step is named at the student grain,
   it will be read at the student grain — even when the ask is per credit
   recommendation.

The corollary for the reward already in flight: pair it with what it produced.
*"★ Veteran Star — 979 JSTs uploaded. Those JSTs generated 18,264 credit
recommendations; 0 have an articulation. The star is the starting line."*
Congratulate, then redirect, at the exact point the misunderstanding forms.

## How we got here

Diagnosed while designing the $50k tab rework. The plateau was initially read as
a data-quality or motivation problem; it is neither. It is the incentive working
as designed, against a finish line nobody intended to draw.

## When this applies (and when it doesn't)

Applies to every published measure that colleges can see and optimise toward —
badges, stars, outcome checkmarks, funding gates. Ask before shipping: *what is
the cheapest way to max this, and is that the behavior we want?*

Does **not** mean avoid binaries entirely. A binary is right where the step
genuinely is done-or-not (a coordinator is listed, or isn't). It is wrong where
the work is continuous and per-item — which is most of the CPL lifecycle.
