# Funding is restricted by its earning rule, not by a label

## The problem

A unified funding pool is the equitable design — one solve, money flowing to
where the teaching is — but it erases the visible boundaries that protected a
minority use. The worked case: folding the noncredit carve-out into the
one-pool implementation-funding model made every institution's award one
combined figure, and Sam's immediate (correct) worry was that *"NC funding
[would be] usurped by the CR program"* — the dominant program absorbs the
whole award because nothing on the face says otherwise.

The reflex fixes are both wrong:

- **Bringing back a second pool** reopens the problem the unification solved —
  a bucket whose size someone must defend every budget cycle, with a threshold
  guarding it and a parity argument beside it.
- **A label alone** ("of which noncredit: $X") is visibility without teeth. A
  college receives one payment; a designation it can ignore protects nothing.

## The method

Decompose the award on its face **and bind each share to its own earning
rule**:

1. **Decompose by the same arithmetic that built the award.** If size set the
   allocation, size splits it: the noncredit share is the award × the
   noncredit fraction of the institution's FTES. No new judgment enters — the
   split is as defensible as the solve.
2. **Restrict each share to the outcomes that justify it.** The noncredit
   share is drawable only against the noncredit measures; the credit program
   cannot reach it by doing more credit work. The restriction is structural —
   it lives in the earn-out computation, not in a memo asking colleges to
   behave.
3. **Then the only real policy question is the interim**: what happens while
   the restricted share's measures have no feed yet? *Hold* (nothing arrives
   unearned — the strongest fence, and it puts the feed on everyone's critical
   path) or *label-and-report* (funding moves, the restriction is reporting).
   That is a one-letter ruling for the decision-maker, not a redesign.

## Why it works

The label answers "whose funding is this?"; the earning rule answers "who can
actually draw it?" — and only the second stops usurpation. Meanwhile the pool
stays whole, so the unification's benefits survive: no bucket to size, no
threshold to defend, and money still follows teaching. The restricted share is
not a sub-pool with its own solver; it is the same award wearing its
obligations on its face.

## Where it's used

The one-pool implementation-funding model (adopted 2026-08-31): every award
shows "incl. NC $Y"; the NC share earns only by noncredit measures; the three
noncredit-only institutions earn wholly by origination with no advances
(N2 b); the interim question for the $1.3M of college NC shares is item F1 on
`docs/visuals/2026-08-31-if-tab-simplified.html`.
