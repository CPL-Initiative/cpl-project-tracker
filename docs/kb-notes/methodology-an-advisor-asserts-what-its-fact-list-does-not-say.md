# An advisor working from a vetted fact list still asserts what the list does not say

> **Check every line an advisor drafts against the line it came from, never against the brief you gave it:** a model handed only verified facts will join two of them, round a range into a category, or promote a draft's claim into a fact, and each result reads naturally.

## Context

The Ironworker pathway film (S330, `prototype/ironworker_video/`) may state only lines the CPL Pathways ladder marks *In our data*. Fable critiqued the storyboard from a brief that listed only those lines and said so. Its critique was sound on structure (the climax, what to cut, the barriers) and still produced four lines the ladder does not support.

## The claim

A fact list constrains what an advisor *starts from*, not what it *writes*. The failures come in recognizable shapes, and each one survives a read of the brief because every noun in it is on the list:

- **The join.** Two true facts become one claim. "Columbus High's pathway maps to WELD 160 and WELD 100" and "Cerritos grants credit for an articulated course by exam" became "Credit by Exam, B or better: 3.5 units", which asserts the route the ladder marks *To confirm*.
- **The category upgrade.** A figure gets a larger noun. "Up to 31.5 of 34-38 units" became "nearly a whole degree"; 34-38 is the A.S. major, and general education sits beside it.
- **The borrowed mechanism.** A word that fits one step is carried to the next. "Credit by Exam" (the high school route) became the apprenticeship's route, where the evidence is MAP's articulated exhibits.
- **The draft promoted.** A sentence from a working document is treated as data. "The A.S. credit carries into the lower division" came from the B.S. map's discussion draft, a source the ladder never labels.

The check that catches all four is line by line: for each on-screen or outward sentence, name the source line it restates, and drop or reword any sentence whose source says less. Reading the advisor's output against the brief does not catch them, because the brief contains every word they use.

## How we got here

S330 asked Fable once, under Sam's scheduled-session terms (a design judgment with nothing to score it against). The four corrections, and what was kept, are in the film's README; the lessons doc S330 section records the run.

## When this applies (and when it doesn't)

It applies to any outward artifact an advisor or a drafting model helps write from verified material: films, letters, explainers, Sierra's answers, decision-sheet proposals. It matters most where a reader takes a figure as settled (a funding number, a units figure, a program's status). It does not mean the advisor's judgment is unreliable: the structural advice in S330 was taken whole. The risk sits in the sentences, not the design.
