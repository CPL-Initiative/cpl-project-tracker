# Reference — CPL at the pre-apprenticeship stage

> **One-sentence summary** — a pre-apprentice can already earn CPL three ways, and the third
> (clearing admission requirements) is the one nobody names, because it speeds *entry* rather than
> banking credit for later.

## Context

The pathway diagrams the CPL Initiative shows to trade audiences start at **pre-apprenticeship**,
but that box is usually treated as scene-setting — the place before the CPL happens. It isn't.
Recorded here because two sessions in a row reasoned about stage 1 from inference when the
authoritative answer was a curator's.

## The claim

### The three mechanisms ⟨Sam Lee, 2026-08-10⟩

A pre-apprentice can earn CPL from:

1. **Noncredit coursework** at a college.
2. **Industry certifications** — welding is the obvious one in the trades.
3. **Clearing admission requirements** for the apprenticeship itself, and for other programs.

### ⭐ (3) is the one that gets missed

At stage 1, CPL is not only credit banked toward a degree the student may not yet be thinking
about. It can be **what gets someone through the door sooner** — satisfying a sponsor's entry
requirements with documented, faculty-evaluated learning.

That reframes the audience. In a room of trade representatives and JATCs, the people who *set*
apprenticeship entry requirements are present, which turns this from an observation into an ask.

### The mechanism is already visible in our own materials

Stage 1 of the Ironworkers pathway reads:

- *"Cerritos College — Noncredit Welding"* → mechanism 1
- *"American River College — Welding Credit Program"* → the credit-bearing twin
- both under *"Welding Apprenticeship Preparation"* → mechanism 2

That box **is** pre-apprenticeship CPL. It simply isn't labeled as such. Pointing at an existing
slide beats adding a new one.

### ⚠ The high-school route, and the catch that decides its value

High-school CTE articulation is also CPL — specifically **credit by exam** under Title 5 §55050
(1,843 credit-by-exam recommendations live in MAP as of 2026-08-10). About **48 high schools**
already appear in MAP as learning partners; Garden Grove High School, on the Carpentry pathway, is
one of them.

But:

> **CSU does not recognize high-school CTE *articulated* credit for transfer. CSU does recognize
> credit by exam.**

If a college transcripts a pre-apprentice's award as plain articulated credit, that credit **dies
at the CSU door** — and every pathway we show ends at a bachelor's degree. For stage 1 to actually
connect to stage 5, it has to be transcripted as Cx. This is the most useful single thing to raise
with a college partner about pre-apprenticeship. (Full derivation: §8 of the noncredit thinking
document.)

### Where the noncredit route is empty — and why that matters more now

Given mechanism 1, the learning-partner inventory is pointed:

| Partner type | Entries in MAP | Examples |
|---|---:|---|
| High schools | ~48 | Garden Grove, Fontana, Cajon, … |
| ROPs | 2–3 | Baldy View ROP |
| Adult schools | 2 | Fontana Adult School |
| **Standalone noncredit institutions** | **0** | NOCE · SD Continuing Ed · Mt. SAC NC · Calbright |

Noncredit coursework is a *named source* of pre-apprenticeship CPL, and the four institutions whose
entire business is noncredit appear on **zero** of MAP's credential titles. The gap is not policy,
permission or infrastructure — it is data entry, which makes it among the cheapest high-value fixes
on the board.

### Supporting evidence to have on hand

- **Rocio Garcia** — started in adult school, received 16 units for her California Registered Dental
  Assistant certification, finished her bachelor's a semester early, and is now a full-time
  tenure-track professor at West Los Angeles College. *Cleared for use by name ⟨Sam, 2026-08-05⟩.*
- **⚠ The retention statistic needs verification before use.** "44% of California apprentices
  withdraw before completion (DAS, 2000–2026)" is a strong argument for counting partial progress,
  and pre-apprenticeship is the earliest place to catch someone. But it is **not** from our
  dashboard — it reaches us via a CTE policy source citing DAS. Confirm with DAS, or attribute it
  to them out loud, before asserting it in a room that contains DAS.

## How we got here

Session 134 (SkyDeck), 2026-08-10, preparing the CAC deck. Sam supplied the three mechanisms
directly, correcting a draft that had led with credit-by-exam as *the* instrument — one route among
several, and not the primary one for the trades. Curator knowledge outranked the inference, which
is the standing rule in CLAUDE.md's "Working with the MAP team."

## When this applies (and when it doesn't)

**Applies** to pathway design, trade/employer-facing messaging, learning-partner recruitment, and
any argument about where CPL should be introduced in a career pathway.

**Does not apply** as a statement of what colleges *currently do* — mechanism 3 in particular
describes what CPL can support, not an inventory of sponsors who accept it today. No such inventory
exists yet; building one would be the natural follow-on.
