# Reference — CCCCO HUMANS principles for responsible AI

> **One-sentence summary** — HUMANS is the California Community Colleges
> Chancellor's Office framework for human-centered, equity-driven AI: technology
> supports human expertise, relationships, and judgment rather than replacing
> them. Source: [ai.cccco.edu](https://ai.cccco.edu).

## Context

The CPL Initiative / MAP embeds AI assistance "from the outset under HUMANS
principles" — language we use on the public CPL Fact Sheet and elsewhere. This
note captures what those principles actually are so the framing is accurate and
consistent wherever we cite it. It is **external CCCCO policy** (public), not a
MAP-internal invention.

## The claim

### The six HUMANS principles

1. **Human agency and accountability** — people remain in control of decisions
   and outcomes; AI supports human judgment, it does not replace it.
2. **Transparency and explainability** — AI systems and their outputs are clear,
   understandable, and open to scrutiny.
3. **Privacy and data protection** — personal and institutional data are handled
   securely and ethically.
4. **Bias mitigation and harm reduction** — AI is designed to avoid bias and
   minimize potential harm.
5. **Inclusive design and accessibility** — tools are built to be usable by all,
   including people with disabilities and from underrepresented groups.
6. **Evaluation of vendors and partners** — partnerships are assessed for
   alignment with system values and their impact on equity and quality.

(Mnemonic: the six map loosely to **H**uman agency, **U**nderstandable/transparent,
**M**anaged privacy, **A**nti-bias, **N**o-barrier/inclusive, **S**crutinized
partners — but cite the full names above, not the acronym gloss.)

### Stewardship and purpose

Developed and stewarded by the Chancellor's Office **AI Council** (faculty,
administrators, practitioners, and experts), which: informs the CO on emerging AI
trends and risks; defines systemwide principles and frameworks for responsible
use; keeps equity, accessibility, and ethics central; and supports alignment
across teaching, student support, and operations.

Applied across **policy and governance** (ethical standards in AI decisions),
**pilots and partnerships** (human-centered + equity goals), and **professional
learning** (AI literacy and responsible-use skills).

### Why they matter

By anchoring AI work in human agency, transparency, privacy, equity, and
inclusion, HUMANS helps the colleges enhance student success and access,
strengthen institutional trust, and keep the human connection at the heart of
learning — ensuring technology serves people, not the other way around.

## When this applies (and when it doesn't)

- **Use it** whenever we describe MAP/CPL AI work to an external audience (the
  Fact Sheet, briefings, reports) — it's the accurate, on-record framing, and it
  is the standard the colleges hold AI tools to.
- **Don't** paraphrase a principle into something stronger/narrower than the six
  above, and don't imply MAP authored the framework — it's CCCCO/AI-Council
  policy that MAP *follows*.
