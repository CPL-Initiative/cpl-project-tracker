# A ruling names a record; the store may count another

Open Asks Sheet 99 card 3 asked Sam to settle the reference's one collision: AUTB sat on Auto Body
Technology and on Agricultural Business and Related Services, "whose one identity is AUTB M1006
Supervision and Management in Agriculture." He ruled: Agricultural Business takes AGAB; its one
identity is re-keyed under the playbook.

Measured before the move, the seed's count of one came from a different record. AUTB M1071 Import
Body Customizing carries the discipline Agricultural Business in its record, so the seed counted it;
AUTB M1006's record said Auto Body Technology, so the seed did not count the course the card named.
Both carry a discipline row Sam himself wrote on 2026-07-13, three seconds apart.

## The method

1. Before a re-key, read the identity the ruling names and the identities the store counts under the
   discipline, and say which is which. A card quotes lane prose; lane prose quotes a count; the count
   came from a field the card did not name.
2. Move the record the ruling names, with its evidence pinned as gates (the title, the human's own
   row on it, its TOP, its key). The dry run refuses a catalog that drifted since the ruling.
3. A second record whose evidence disagrees with a human-sourced row is a card for the human, never a
   session's correction (Rule 8). The view then tells the truth in the meantime: one row sits off its
   code, and the card says why.
4. Teach the checks the ruled shape before the move lands, or the ruling replaces the collision it
   closed with a false one: a family code one discipline takes while the umbrellas declare it is
   shared by design; two disciplines canonical on one code is still the collision.

## How it ended

Sam answered Sheet 101 the same evening (22:38Z, his own call): Auto Body. The second record moved
by one discipline field under his own email, with no re-key, and the reference reads no row off its
code. The card cost one sheet and an hour; a session's correction would have cost his trust in the
rows he wrote.
