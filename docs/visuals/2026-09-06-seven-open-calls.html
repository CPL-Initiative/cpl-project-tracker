Should the daily run rebuild the map’s payloads?

your call
What it is
SkyView reads two generated files. One is rebuilt by hand and one has not been rebuilt since August, and nothing rebuilds either on a schedule — the daily workflow builds only the course-description shards.
Measured this morning
FileBuiltWhat it feeds
ccr_universe.json2026-09-05 15:22the map, and every count on the workspace
ccr_atlas_data.json2026-08-24 15:34the Decisions column and the decision packs

⚠️ I told you last night this made the map and the discipline tables disagree by 43 on Health. That was wrong, and reading the code this morning is what corrected it. The workspace builds its identity and stand-alone counts from the universe (disciplineRows() reads I.n); the stale file supplies only the Decisions column, the work-surface offer, and a provenance tooltip. The 117-discipline gap between the two files is real but nothing renders it side by side.

What the staleness does cost, measured through the alias chain against the live catalog: the five embedded decision packs carry 593 identity ids, of which 504 (85%) resolve to something live and 89 (15%) resolve to nothing.

Proposed
Split the answer. Rebuild ccr_atlas_data.json in the daily run — it is derived, it takes under a second, and 15% of what it currently offers a curator is stale. Four small builds

Each is an afternoon at most. I left them because they change how the surface behaves, not whether it works.

2

Make the search chips navigable

build
What it is
The chips above the map name what you have picked. They sit where a trail of breadcrumbs would sit and they look like one, but the only working control inside a chip is its ×.
Measured
One chip renders one button, and that button removes the chip. The chip carries a full-title tooltip (your ruling of 5 September) but no click target. So the only way back to a pick you have navigated away from is to search for it again.
Proposed
Make the chip’s label a button that re-centres on that pick and opens its panel. The × keeps its own job. Nothing is removed.
Your reply

3

Make the whole panel row clickable

build
What it is
In a discipline panel each course is a bordered row with a title, a code, member counts and a chip. Only the title opens it.
Measured
14 rows, 14 buttons — exactly one hit target per row, and it is the title. The rest of the row, which is most of its area, does nothing.
Proposed
Make the row itself the target, keeping the title as the accessible name so the keyboard path is unchanged. ⚠️ One caveat worth your ruling: the row also carries the identity-system chip, which now has its own tooltip — a whole-row click would swallow a hover a reader may want. I would keep the chip inert and let the rest of the row open the course.
Your reply

4

Let a carried course be dropped inside the panel

build — nearly free now
What it is
Pick up a course with Drag… and the hint says to drop it on the identity it belongs to “or click that identity”. Until last night the panel never showed a draggable course and a droppable identity at the same time, so every move had to go across the canvas.
Measured
⚠️ This changed under me. The Similar courses list I built last night put destinations in the panel: an identity now shows 24 draggable courses and 24 identity links side by side. But every one of those links calls goNode() — it navigates. Clicking one while carrying a course abandons the carry instead of completing it. The destinations became visible; they still do not accept a drop.
Proposed
While a course is carried, a click on a similar-course link applies the move instead of navigating, and the row says so. This is the smallest of the four now that the list exists.

5

Say what Recenter re-centres on

wording
What it is
With one pick the button reads Recenter; with more it reads Fit all. It acts on the chips, not on what the panel is showing.
Measured
With two picks and the panel open on Introduction to Welding, the button reads “Fit all”, titled “Fit every pick into view”. That is accurate. The ambiguity is in the one-pick case: “Recenter the map on this pick” is honest, but after drilling two levels past the pick, “this” is not what the reader is looking at.
Proposed
Name the target in the button: Recenter on Welding. It removes the ambiguity without adding a control, and it fits the glyph rule — a word doing the work. Say the word if you would rather it followed the panel instead.

Two rulings, and a correction

6

Should Enter keep the suggestion list open?

your call
What it is
Typing in the search opens a list. Pressing Enter with nothing highlighted closes it and flies the map to the term instead.
Measured
The form’s submit handler takes a highlighted suggestion if there is one, and otherwise closes the list and runs a map search. That is deliberate — one box, two behaviours, chosen by what is on screen. It is also what made you say the intro course “did not show” before you found the real defect: Enter had dismissed the answer.
Proposed
Leave Enter as it is, but stop it hiding the answer: run the map search and keep the list open, with the first row highlighted so the next Enter opens it. Nothing is taken away and the list stops vanishing at the moment you look at it.
Your reply

7

correction
What it is
The observation log reported that reaching the map by keyboard took 15 Tab presses past 217 controls, and I passed that on to you.
Measured
⚠️ Wrong, and mine to correct. The page holds 205 focusable elements and the canvas sits at index 39 — it also already carries tabindex="0", so it is reachable. 39 is still a long way in for the main thing on the page, but it is not 217 and it is not unreachable.

Add a skip link — Skip to the map — as the first focusable element, which is what the presentation rules already ask for and costs one line. That makes the canvas the first stop for anyone who wants it and leaves the tab order alone for everyone else.
