The three answers

A college has finished nothing here. The question is only what it records in the meantime.

A Mark it Not Applicable anyway Rule this out

Records
“We looked; no credit applies.”
Cost
States a refusal ACE never made. An N/A row leaves the queue and nobody revisits it — the one irreversible choice of the three. It also lifts every college’s disposition rate without awarding a single unit.
Student
The door closes silently, and no one ever files a complaint about a door they were told wasn’t there.

B Leave it at Needs Action Right for now

Records
“Not decided yet” — which is true today.
Cost
5,311 rows sit in the backlog indefinitely, indistinguishable from work nobody has opened. A college that has genuinely decided it won’t assess these has no way to say so.
Student
Nothing happens, but nothing false is on the record.

C Offer it as Credit by Exam ✓ The ruling

Records
What is actually true: this is a course you can challenge by exam. Distinct from both “we refused” and “we haven’t looked.”
Cost
None to build. This was framed as needing a new state in MAP; it doesn’t. Credit by Exam is an existing CPL mechanism and the most-used one in the whole curated corpus — 798 credentials carry it, ahead of Industry Certification’s 671. The work is presenting it, not building it.
Exception
One, and it is per-college policy rather than per-row judgement: a college that does not permit Credit by Exam for that particular course closes it on that basis.
Student
The door is not just left open — someone points at it. You can sit an exam for this.

Why C wins on its own terms. A is the only one of the three that puts something false on the record, and the only one that can’t be walked back — an N/A row is never revisited. B is honest but inert. C is the only one that produces a student outcome.

✓ And it turned out to cost nothing to build, because the mechanism already existed. The rows were never dead ends — measured 2026-08-20, all 5,311 carry an empty CPL type. They were untyped, not refused, and an untyped row reads exactly like a closed one.

The question that dissolved

Before the ruling this looked like it needed two answers. The individualized-assessment sentence is 75% of the class; swimming takes it to 95% — but only if swimming rules the same way, and “additional swimming credit on institutional evaluation” is a legacy military-PE recommendation at 86 colleges. Whether a college would grant that today is a different question from whether to assess someone’s occupational training.

One rule with an exception covers both. Offering the row as Credit by Exam doesn’t require anyone to decide in advance whether swimming deserves credit — the college’s own Cx policy for that course decides it, which is where that judgement belonged all along. The 95% question never needed answering.

What is already moving, and what is held

✓ Priority 1 is cleared to send — 12,283 rows at ~100 colleges where ACE genuinely recommends no credit, or the recommendation’s own validity window has closed. One instruction, the same one everywhere. It was not safe to send before these 5,311 were separated out.

✓ Priority 5 is live and student-facing — the class now reads “Credit by Exam opportunities — present, do not close”, one rule, owned by college CPL staff. It stopped being a clean-up item and became the only class in the worklist that can still turn into credit for someone.

⚠ Its rank still says 5, and that is now understated. Priority 5 meant readiness — nobody had ruled. That reason is gone, and this is the highest-value class in the list. The number is left alone only because renumbering would move every other class Sam’s team already refers to by number.
