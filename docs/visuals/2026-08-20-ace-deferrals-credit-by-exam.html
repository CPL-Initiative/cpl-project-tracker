The three answers

A college has finished nothing here. The question is only what it records in the meantime.

A Mark it Not Applicable anyway Rule this out

Records
“We looked; no credit applies.”
Cost
States a refusal ACE never made. An N/A row leaves the queue and nobody revisits it — the one irreversible choice of the three. It also lifts every college’s disposition rate without awarding a single unit.
Student
The door closes silently, and no one ever files a complaint about a door they were told wasn’t there.

B Leave it at Needs Action Right for now

Records
“Not decided yet” — which is true today.
Cost
5,311 rows sit in the backlog indefinitely, indistinguishable from work nobody has opened. A college that has genuinely decided it won’t assess these has no way to say so.
Student
Nothing happens, but nothing false is on the record.

C Offer it as Credit by Exam ✓ The ruling

Records
What is actually true: this is a course you can challenge by exam. Distinct from both “we refused” and “we haven’t looked.”
Cost
None to build. This was framed as needing a new state in MAP; it doesn’t. Credit by Exam is an existing CPL mechanism and the most-used one in the whole curated corpus — 798 credentials carry it, ahead of Industry Certification’s 671. The work is presenting it, not building it.
Exception
One, and it is per-college policy rather than per-row judgement: a college that does not permit Credit by Exam for that particular course closes it on that basis.
Student
The door is not just left open — someone points at it. You can sit an exam for this.

Why C wins on its own terms. A is the only one of the three that puts something false on the record, and the only one that can’t be walked back — an N/A row is never revisited. B is honest but inert. C is the only one that produces a student outcome.

✓ And it costs nothing to build, because the mechanism already existed. The rows were never dead ends — measured 2026-08-20, all 5,311 carry an empty CPL type. They were untyped, not refused, and an untyped row reads exactly like a closed one.

⚠ Its reach is a quarter of the class, not all of it — see below. The ruling is right; what it can be applied to today is smaller than this page first implied.

The alignment tag, and why it is smaller than it looked

Sam asked for an indicator of where the training might align with a course. Peer precedent makes that a fact rather than a guess — but the first version of it was wrong.

⚠ A two-college floor is not enough. Requiring that two or more colleges named the same course produced a top tier where 11 of 14 exhibits pointed at MAG-51 Elements of Supervision — offered for Infantryman, Combat Medic, Cook, Truck Driver and HR Specialist alike.

Counting agreeing colleges cannot tell corroboration from a blanket mapping. Three colleges that map any military service to a supervision course produce the same count as three colleges independently reaching the same conclusion.
