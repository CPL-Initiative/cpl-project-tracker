What to build

1 · The description: keep quoting one college, or write a consolidation?

What this is
You asked me to read the batched local descriptions and write one that captures the essence of all of them without repetition. That reverses your Session 235 ruling, which is in the code as an invariant: “the representative description is CHOSEN, not written — composing new prose out of several catalogs would read as authoritative while belonging to nobody.” Today the card quotes the medoid — the one description most typical of the rest — and attributes it to the college and course code that wrote it.
What the descriptions actually look like
Measured from unified_courses_member_desc.js today: 46,317 identities carry a description; 14,902 of them (32%) carry two or more, median 3 apiece, mean 6.4, one carries 2,024. On a 1,200-identity sample of the multi-description set, the medoid’s mean agreement with the others is median 0.34 — and 33% score under 0.25, meaning the quoted description shares less than a quarter of its content words with the rest. At the other end, the top decile scores 1.00: the colleges wrote the same text and choosing is already the right answer.
And the boilerplate is a separate problem
Literal boilerplate is rarer than it looks — only 3.8% of the 127,266 description rows are text reused across more than one identity. But the single most-reused string, on 48 identities, is “This text field is blank due to the March 2012 data migration and CCC Curriculum Inventory Version 1 protocol…” — a placeholder pretending to be a description. “Experimental course.” sits on 25 more. That is almost certainly what you saw, and consolidating it would launder it into prose rather than remove it.
What I propose
Two mechanisms, not one. (a) Detect placeholder text and drop it from the medoid pool entirely, so a course with one real description and three migration stubs quotes the real one — and a course with nothing but stubs says it has no description rather than printing the stub. (b) Keep the quoted, attributed description always, and add a clearly-labelled MAP-Generated block beneath it — “What these 14 colleges agree this course covers” — only where agreement is low. Nothing composed is ever mistaken for a college’s own words, and your Session-235 reason survives intact.
Why not simply replace it
Your original reason still holds where the colleges agree, which is most of the top half of the distribution: there, a synthesis would restate one college’s sentence in MAP’s voice and lose the attribution for nothing. Replacing everywhere also removes the only line on the card a faculty member can check against their own catalog. If you want the replacement anyway, say edit: replace it and I will — it is your call to reverse, and I would rather you reverse it on purpose than have me quietly keep half of it.

2 · Where should exhibit names appear? They are already on the card.

What this is
You asked me to add the names of articulated exhibits to the description card. They are already there, in the CPL layer of the outline card and in the inspector panel — each credential lists its exhibits as MAP exhibit: Fire Prevention FIRE 002 Cx, with the CPL type, the credit recommendation and the college holding it. So either you were looking at a surface that does not carry them, or you want them somewhere else.
Where they are not
Three places name a course and show no exhibit: the Courses face (the default — the CPL layer sits below the description and is easy to miss), the hover quick-look, and the collapsed CPL layer, whose summary chip says only “1 credential”.
What I propose
Put the exhibit count and the first exhibit’s name in the collapsed CPL layer’s summary, so the card names an exhibit without being opened — the smallest change that answers the ask on the surface you were looking at. Reply edit: with the surface you meant if it was the hover or the Courses face instead.
Why the smallest one
The hover quick-look is deliberately thin — it is a glance, and the lane invariant is that hover is a quick look and the click opens the detail. Widening it is a bigger call than this ask implies, so I would rather you name it than assume it.

3 · What should CPL mode show? Right now it hides where the credit comes from.

What this is
You hit this from the Fontana High School surprise. The data was right: FIRE 110 X is reached by a credential called Fire Prevention whose issuing agency is Fontana High School — a high-school fire academy, Credit By Exam, 3 hours, held by Copper Mountain, reviewed by you on 8 July. Nothing is wrong with the record. What surprised you is that CPL face labels a point with the credential name and nothing else, so a high-school articulation looks identical to an ACE or a state certification until you open the card.
What is actually in there
Measured from prototype/ccr_cpl.json: 3,124 exhibit rows across six CPL types — Industry Certification 1,558 · Credit By Exam 862 · Standardized Assessment 373 · Portfolio Review 213 · Military 66 · Other 52 — issued by 135 distinct agencies. The largest single issuer is California Community Colleges at 924 rows, then the College Board at 370 and California State Fire Training at 179. All of that currently renders as one undifferentiated field of points.
What I propose
Make CPL face answer where does our CPL actually come from: color the points by CPL type, put the six types in a Show-style filter row like the existing switches, and add the issuing agency to the point’s label. Fontana High School then reads as one Credit-By-Exam point among 862, which is what it is.
Why type and not agency
135 agencies cannot be a color scale and would need a legend nobody reads; six types can, and type is the distinction that actually changes what a college does about it. The agency stays as a word on the label and in the card, where it is precise. If you would rather group by something else — military vs civilian, statewide vs local — say so; the payload carries both.

4 · The live-session banner works. Its row expired two days ago.

What this is
You reported not seeing the banner announcing a live Claude Code session. It is not a code fault. Read from the live table today: cobi_live_session row 1 is active with a valid claude.ai URL, and expires_at is 8 September 20:29 UTC — two days ago. The renderer fails closed on an expired row, exactly as designed, and shows nothing.
The part worth fixing
You set that row, saw nothing, and concluded the feature had never been built. An expired row and an unbuilt feature are indistinguishable to the person who set it — which is the same failure shape as the two bugs fixed this morning, where a control did its job silently and read as broken.
What I propose
Add a curator-only line, visible only to a signed-in Team member, that says “your banner row expired 2 days ago” where the banner would have been. Nothing changes for anyone else, and the public still sees nothing, because the row stays unreadable to them under RLS. Refreshing the row itself is yours — I can draft the SQL but I will not write to that table without you saying so, since it points the whole team at a session URL.
Why not just extend the expiry
The expiry is the safety mechanism: it guarantees a stale link stops advertising itself. Lengthening it trades that away to paper over a diagnosis problem, and the diagnosis is what actually cost you the time.
