The Subject–Discipline Edge: Nine and Five

Two things came out of re-measuring the edge you ruled on this morning. Nine subjects disagree with the map, not four — and until today none of the nine printed anything. That is fixed. What is left is which of the nine are corrections, and that is yours. Second, the 93 identities the fill could not reach are not mechanically fillable: 37 of their 50 subject codes have no other identity to corroborate against, and five carry enough weight to be worth a map entry.

What changed since this morning

Your five rulings went in and the fill ran: 326 blanks → 93. Re-measuring the result turned up two things worth your time.

The nine, and why you never saw them

The subject table was built to say so when the map overrules the identities. It built that sentence and then attached it to one of the four ways the function can return — the branch for a subject that is its discipline’s Common SUBJ. A subject whose identities sit somewhere else is, by construction, usually not that. So the note was computed correctly and discarded on the way out, every time. Measured on the payload the page itself draws: nine subjects disagree and zero printed. The note now rides every exit, with a guard per exit.

The 93, and why they are not a data-entry job

Fifteen are present in the C-ID/CCN reference carrying a null discipline; the other 78 have a SUBJ4 the map does not hold. They span 50 subject codes. I looked for a second, independent signal before proposing any value, and for most of them there is none:

HUMN is why the middle row matters. Its one voting identity is Music for Video Games and Film, filed under Music — and its three blanks are Honors Introduction to Popular Culture, Honors Popular Culture & United States History and Popular Culture & United States History. Taking the unanimous reading would have filed three humanities courses under Music.

The calls

1 · BSOT → Office Technologies

What this is
Three blanks — MS Excel III, MS Access III, Business Information Professional. 369 other identities carry the BSOT prefix and 99% of them are already filed under Office Technologies.
What I propose
Add "BSOT": "Office Technologies" to subject_discipline_map.json.
Why
Both signals agree and the titles are unambiguous office-application courses. This is the strongest of the five by an order of magnitude.
Your reply

2 · HUMA → Humanities

What this is
One blank, Survey of Film. 112 other HUMA identities, 96% Humanities.
What I propose
Add "HUMA": "Humanities".
Why
The edge files the SUBJECT, not the course. A film survey taught under a humanities subject code belongs to the humanities subject; if the course itself belongs elsewhere that is a course-level move, not an edge.

6 · HOSP — genuinely split, and I am not proposing a value

What this is
Five blanks spanning Hospitality Cost Control, Hospitality Law, Culinary Principles and Introduction to Baking. The eight HOSP identities that do carry a discipline are split four ways: Hotel and Motel Services (2), Travel Services (2), Family and Consumer Studies / Home Economics (2), Culinary Arts / Food Technology (2).
What I propose
Leave HOSP out of the map and put it on the curation worklist. If you want a value now, Hotel and Motel Services is the closest to the code’s name — but two of the five blanks are culinary courses, so the edge would be wrong for them.
Why
The map’s own policy is to skip codes whose sampled titles are mixed. HOSP is exactly that case, and forcing it would put baking under hotel services on every surface at once.
Your reply

7 · The nine disagreements — who decides, and in what order

What this is
Nine subjects where the map overrules what the courses actually say. Five are large and near-unanimous (ATHL, THTR, ESCI, ELEC, MUSC); ETHN is 34 rows; PHTO, ESLN and ENVS are small.
What I propose
Rule the five large ones as a batch, since each is a single value in one file: change the map to match the courses (ATHL→Kinesiology, THTR→Drama/Theater Arts, ESCI→Environmental Technologies, ELEC→Electricity, MUSC→Commercial Music) or keep the map and let the row say so, now that it does. I do not have a recommendation here — these are discipline names, and which one is right is faculty vocabulary, not a measurement.
Why
A change to the map re-files rows on every surface at once, so under Rule 10 it lands in one cron window with a receipt. That is cheap for five values and expensive to get wrong, which is why it is a ruling rather than a cleanup.
Your reply

8 · ESLN — a malformed discipline name in the data

What this is
Five ESLN courses sit under a discipline literally named English as a Second Language Noncredit 53412. The trailing number is a TOP code that got into the name.
What I propose
Treat this one as a data defect rather than a vocabulary question: the map’s English as a Second Language is right and the courses’ discipline string should be repaired at its source.
Why
Unlike the other eight, nobody chose this name. It is the only one of the nine where the disagreement is not a judgment about faculty vocabulary.
Your reply

9 · The 37 codes with nothing to corroborate against

What this is
Thirty-seven subject codes — 63 of the 93 rows — where no other identity carries the prefix. WWTR (water treatment), TOUR, LIBS, MCOM, AG-PS, AG-EH and thirty-one more, most of them one or two rows.
What I propose
Leave them blank and say so on the surface, rather than inferring a discipline from a title. Put them on the CPL clean-up worklist as a curator pass — a person who knows the field can read thirty-seven codes in one sitting; a session cannot check its own guess against anything.
Why
Every filling signal we hold has already been tried on these. A discipline invented from one course title would be indistinguishable, on every surface, from one a curator chose — and HUMN shows what that costs when the guess is wrong.
Your reply
