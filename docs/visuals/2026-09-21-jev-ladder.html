1 · Which center gets the next sitting of verdicts

reference: ladder-01-first-verdict-set

What this is
Jev has a calibrated gate on exactly one center. Your 51 CCRR verdicts set it at 0.85, where Jev folded 25 of 25 correctly. The CER, the CSR and the CCR carry no human verdict at all. Measured today against the articulation corpus, all four centers ride the same 9,413 rows across the same 82 colleges — they consolidate different columns of one table — so collapse value, the rule this lane ranks by, is a constant between them and orders nothing. What separates them is what one sitting of verdicts buys: your 51 CCRR verdicts settled 1,459 rows, about 29 rows each, while the CER’s entire judgment backlog of 59 findings reaches 71 rows across 20 colleges, about 1.2 rows each. The CCR carries 134,485 member rows over 16,478 course identities, fourteen times the whole articulated corpus.
Why
A gate is calibrated once and then runs unattended. Spending a sitting where the corpus is largest buys the most unattended running afterward, and the CCR is larger than the other three together by an order of magnitude.
What I propose
Give the CCR the next sitting of verdicts, and calibrate its gate on them. It might be wrong if the CCR’s questions turn out too varied for one gate to cover, in which case a sitting spent there calibrates nothing and the CCRR head harvest in item 5 was the better buy.

2 · The CCR has nothing to hand Jev yet

reference: ladder-02-ccr-trust-card-adapter

What this is
The adjudication module wires the CSR, the CER and the CCRR. The CCR sits outside it for a structural reason: its method half, kb/_row_audit.py, emits Trust Cards — a score per row across eight rules — where the other three emit findings, which are discrete questions. Jev answers questions.
Why
Item 1 sends a sitting to the CCR, and a sitting needs questions to answer. The adapter is the step between the two.
What I propose
Build the adapter that turns a Trust Card into a question, and run it before the CCR sitting. It might be wrong if the Trust Card rules carry no question a curator would recognize as a question, which the adapter would surface in its first batch rather than after a sitting is spent.

3 · Each center calibrates its own gate

reference: ladder-03-per-center-gate

What this is
The 0.85 gate was measured on one question put to the CCRR: do these two recommendations describe the same course content. The CER asks whether two credential titles name one credential, the CSR whether a four-letter subject code is wrong, the CCR whether two local courses are one course. Nothing measured says a probability carries the same meaning across those four questions.
Why
The battery already showed what happens when a general prior meets a domain that has overruled it: on units Jev scored 0.281, below chance, because this lane rules that units are not identity and Jev assumes they are. A gate carries that kind of error with it.
What I propose
Calibrate a gate per center against that center’s own verdicts, and let none of them inherit 0.85. Until a center has verdicts, treat its Jev output as a ranked worklist and never as a suggestion to act on.

4 · Three centers have nowhere to record a verdict

reference: ladder-04-decisions-store-per-center

What this is
The CCRR writes to cr_reference_decisions, which carries a note column for the reason behind each call. The CER, the CSR and the CCR have no decisions table. Measured today: those two are the only decision or curation tables in the database.
Why
A center with nowhere to record a verdict asks you the same question again next run, and the reason behind the first answer is lost. The reference already states that a center needs a decisions store with a reason column before its first sheet.
What I propose
Create one decisions table per center on the CCRR’s shape, reason column included, and route each through Governance before it ships — a first writer to a shared table is a decision-rights change under Rule 10, rather than a code detail.

reference: ladder-06-band-to-curator

What this is
The pre-registered battery of six variables ran on those 26 pairs and nothing passed. The primary came back at AUC 0.378, below chance: where Jev found a reason to hold two recommendations apart, you were more likely to fold them. Six phrasings have now been spent on the same two strings. The band carries 838 articulation rows.
Why
The band is where curator judgment is the product rather than a cost. A seventh phrasing against the same evidence is the move the battery was run to rule out.
What I propose
Put the 26 on a sheet as your work, and stop asking Jev about them. It might be wrong if a genuinely different kind of evidence reaches them — member course titles, the units spread, what other colleges did with the same pair — which item 7 tests on one variable before anyone builds it.

7 · The one lead from the battery gets a single re-test

reference: ladder-07-level-retest

What this is
Of the six variables, level pointed the right way with the largest effect, at raw p 0.023. Corrected for having asked six questions at once it reads 0.138. Six questions yield about one good-looking result by luck, and the rule that says so was fixed in the source before any answer existed.
Why
A lead is worth one honest shot. Re-analyzing the rows that produced it turns a lucky draw into a finding, which is the failure the pre-registration was written to prevent.
What I propose
Give level one targeted re-test on a batch it did not pick, pre-registered as a single hypothesis, and drop it if it fails. Leave the original 26 rows out of that analysis entirely.

9 · Hold the CSR out of the ladder while it asks identity questions

reference: ladder-09-csr-rule-7

What this is
123 of the CSR’s 143 judgment findings are one rule, cs9_anchor_subj_diverge: it asks whether a discipline’s canonical four-letter code should change to match its anchor course. A change there is a re-mint, governed by Rule 7 and its mandatory playbook. Rule 7 also holds that an unreliable signal never gates identity.
Why
A fold in the CCRR collapses two wordings and a curator can pull them apart again. A subject code change re-keys stored identifiers, and the alias chain exists because that is expensive to undo.
What I propose
Let Jev rank the 123 as a worklist for a human and keep it away from the verdict, so no re-mint is ever proposed by a model. Hold the CSR below the CCR and the CCRR in the ladder until the M-ID layer is declared faculty-published and Rule 7 re-locks.
