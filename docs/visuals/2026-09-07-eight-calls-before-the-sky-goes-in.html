What the port does whatever you answer

Some of the port is engineering rather than a call. It is listed here so the eight items are the only open ground.

  • The Sky reads the live map — the same identities, the same college courses, the same articulations — and the placement on the sphere becomes a file the daily run rebuilds and checks, so the sky never lags the map.
  • It is drawn on the map’s own canvas, with no outside library. A full redraw of all 49,896 points measured 18 to 35 milliseconds in software rendering, which is a turning sky at 30 frames a second or better on a real graphics card, without a third-party script on the public page.
  • Every rule the flat map earned is re-earned on the sphere, each with a test: a drop lands on a circle even when a star sits over it; names drop rather than stack; Tab, Enter and Escape walk it; text keeps its size under zoom; a reader who asked for reduced motion gets a still sky; the accessibility run adds the route.
  • The Courses | CPL face and the Articulations light come along as they are. They are the same data.
  • A move is one course at a time, staged as a row for review, never written from the page.

4

Whether the sky moves on its own, and when it stops

your call
What it is
The prototype turns by itself when it opens: one full turn in about 140 seconds, slower as you zoom in so the sky drifts at one pace, and the stars twinkle only while it turns — an eleven-second breath, never more than a quarter of a star’s light. A drag stops it. It never turns for a reader whose system asks for reduced motion; that is already built. The map today moves only when a reader moves it. A curation surface that moves by itself needs a rule for when it may.
Measured
At the opening zoom the sky drifts about 2.6 degrees a second — on a 960-pixel window 150 degrees across, about 16 pixels a second — so a course carried for three seconds sees its destination move 50 pixels under the hand. A full redraw costs 18 to 35 milliseconds in software rendering, which is why the flat map redraws only on a change and a turning sky can afford to redraw every frame.

“slow the rotation” · “Rotation speed is nicer now” · “slow rotation proportionally on zoom” · “make twinkle a bit gentler/slower” · “rotation should be left to right” — you, 7 September 2026; all built, all kept as built

What I propose
The Sky turns when it opens and stops at the first touch — a drag, a click, a key, a search, a carry — and stays still until Rotate is pressed again. The twinkle runs only while it turns. Neither runs for a reader who asked for reduced motion. The Globe the same. A carried course is never dropped on a moving target.
Why
The turn is what makes it a sky while you are looking and a hazard while you are working, and the first touch is the line between the two. Rotate stays one word in the row, pressed at opening, so a reader can see that it is on and turn it back on when they are done.
