1 · How a course’s level is read

reference: in use · kb/_esl_package_dryrun.py classify() · kb/reference/esl_level_sets.json

What this is
The fold reads each title in a fixed order and stops at the first answer: a purpose word (citizenship, vocational, culture and media), then a level word (Beginning, Intermediate, Advanced, their synonyms, and pairs such as Beginning-Intermediate), then the course’s rung on its own college’s ladder, and Beginning when none of these speaks. Your per-ladder sets of 2026-08-24 decide a rung’s band, with the two-rung row and the noncredit shift one band down. Checked against the colleges’ catalog descriptions, a level word was wrong 6.2% of the time, the old fixed rung reading 49.2%, and the Beginning default 76.7%.
Why
Every other rule sits inside this order, so a change here moves more courses than any other card. The 92 newer identities (item 5) show three misreads: Careers, plural, misses the vocational word career, so ESL for Healthcare Careers falls to Beginning; High-Interm is not read as Intermediate; and Part 1 of a two-part course reads as rung 1.
What I propose
Keep the order and fix the three misreads: plurals of the purpose words, Interm as Intermediate, and Part N no longer read as a rung. It might be wrong if a college numbers its ladder in parts, in which case Part 2 is a real rung and the third fix drops it.

3 · Purpose before level, and the healthcare courses

reference: in use · classify() carve-outs · kb/_esl_package_apply.py Enrichment and Healthcare

What this is
Citizenship courses go to Civic ESL, vocational and workplace courses to Vocational ESL, and courses framed around culture, film, music or leisure to Enrichment ESL, before any level is read; Vocational ESL then sends its health courses to Vocational — Healthcare. A re-level never moves a course out of a purpose group (the ladder pass skipped 181), and 45 stay put although a member’s catalog names a level. Health words are missing from the first step: of the 23 health-titled ESL folds, 5 reached Vocational — Healthcare, and 17 sit in Beginning and 1 in Intermediate (ESL for Healthcare 1, ESL for Medical Terminology, ESL for Patient Care Skills).
Why
A purpose group tells a student and a counselor what a course is for, and a level group tells them where it sits; a course that belongs to both can live in only one. You made Vocational — Healthcare its own comprehensive, and 18 of its courses have not reached it.
What I propose
Keep purpose first, and add health words to it, so the 18 move to Vocational — Healthcare. It might be wrong if some of the 18 are general ESL courses that use health topics for reading practice, which the apply shows by name before it writes.

4 · Transfer composition stays out of the fold

reference: in use · kb/_esl_package_apply.py (transfer-level held back, unruled) · 8 identities

What this is
The fold holds back composition taught for ESL students because it may carry transferable credit, and the code notes that you have not ruled on it. Eight identities sit there. Five are transfer composition: ESL College Composition and Reading (ESOL M1205), Composition, Reading, and Freshman English (M1239), College Composition for ESL (M10KY), Composition for Non-Native Speakers (M10PO) and Freshman Composition for Non-Native Speakers (M10PN). Three are not: two Preparation for English Composition for Multilingual Students (M10AD credit, M91BQ noncredit) and a noncredit Composition for ESL Students (M9192).
Why
A transfer course and the course before it serve different students, and a noncredit course carries no transferable credit. Held apart, the three appear under no level group, so a counselor looking under Advanced does not find them.
What I propose
Fold the three into Advanced ESL and keep the five apart as transfer composition. It might be wrong if a college runs the preparation course as a co-requisite of transfer composition, or your noncredit rule puts the two noncredit courses one band lower, at Intermediate.
