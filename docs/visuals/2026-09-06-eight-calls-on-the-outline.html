When the three sources disagree about a certification, which one wins?

your call
What it is
You ruled that agency skill statements come from published agency standards and ACE exhibits and the MAP team — “All three” — and then raised the question yourself: what happens when they disagree about the same certification? Until that has an answer, the outline’s skills layer has no authority to draw on and stays imputed from catalog text.
Measured today
What we holdCount
Credentials classified, all disciplines1,987
Welding credentials among them64
Published welding credit recommendations57
Carrying any skill statement0

The files carry agency, title and hours. Not one carries what a holder can do. So this is not a merge problem yet — it is a sourcing problem, and the reconciliation rule decides what we go and fetch.

What I propose
A precedence order, not a merge: the published agency standard is the text of record; an ACE exhibit fills a gap the standard leaves and never overrides it; the MAP team overrides either, attributed and dated, because a curator knows things a document does not. Where the standard and ACE genuinely conflict on the same skill, the outline shows both, side by side, each named, rather than picking — a faculty reader judging sufficiency is better served by seeing the disagreement than by a silent winner.
Why
It matches how you already treat curator knowledge everywhere else in MAP (attributed, first-class, outranking inference), and it keeps the one case that actually matters — two authorities disagreeing about what a certification proves — visible to the person making the call. Pilot it on an AWS welding certification as you suggested; one worked example will show whether “show both” is generous or noisy.

3

How far should the text zoom go?

your call
What it is
You want a control on the SkyView toolbar that sizes label text up or down, and you were explicit that it must not change today’s behavior where text does not zoom with the map — “it’s important to keep with all we have going on.”
What that constrains
Label size is already independent of the map’s zoom, so this is a second axis rather than a change to the first. The risk is not the control, it is the range: labels are placed by a collision-avoidance pass, and past a certain size the map stops being able to place them and starts dropping them silently.
What I propose
Three steps and a reset — Smaller · Normal · Larger at roughly 0.85×, 1×, 1.25× — remembered per browser like the dark canvas is. Words, not a slider and not a pair of glyphs.
Why
A slider invites a size the label placer cannot honor, and the failure is invisible: labels stop appearing rather than overlapping. Three named steps stay inside what the placer can do, and “Larger” is a promise we can keep at every zoom level. If 1.25× turns out too timid on your screen, that is a number to change, not a design.

Is “most colleges / some colleges / one college” the right way to say how sure we are?

your call
What it is
Each skill on the outline carries a confidence chip. Because we hold no agency skill statements, confidence means one thing only: how many of the colleges teaching this course name that skill in their own catalog. The words on screen are most colleges, some colleges and one college.
Measured today
Across the whole referenceCountShare
College courses carrying a catalog description127,27494.6%
Course identities with two or more14,90230.0%

So on seven identities in ten there is only one catalog to read, and every skill is a “one college” skill. That is honest, and it may still read as faint praise for a course that is simply taught at one place.

What I propose
Keep the three words, and where a course has only one college carrying it, say “the only college teaching it” instead of “one college” — the same fact, without implying a thin result.
Why
“One college” on a course taught at twenty means the skill is poorly corroborated. On a course taught at one, it means the evidence is complete. Same words, opposite meanings, and a faculty reader has no way to tell which they are looking at.

6

Should the daily run rebuild the map’s layout too?

your call
What it is
You ruled last week that the daily run rebuilds SkyView’s decision payload and leaves the layout hand-built. That is working. The open half is whether the layout — where every island and point sits — should be rebuilt nightly as well.
What it costs either way
The layout takes about twenty seconds to build, so runtime is not the argument. The argument is that a nightly rebuild moves the map under people: an island a curator has learned the position of drifts when the corpus changes, and the drag positions they have set are computed against it.
What I propose
No — keep it hand-built, and rebuild it deliberately when the corpus has moved enough to be worth relearning the map. Revisit once the curation queue is being worked daily rather than in bursts.
Why
Stability is the whole value of a map. The decision payload going stale silently cost us dead decisions, which is why it now rebuilds nightly; the layout going stale costs nothing but a slightly out-of-date arrangement, and rebuilding it costs the reader their bearings.
