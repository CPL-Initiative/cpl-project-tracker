The questions

1 · What does ENVS mean — science or technology?

What this is
One identity: Introduction to Environmental Science, filed under Biological Sciences, against a map that says Environmental Technologies. ⚠️ There is no “Environmental Science” MQ discipline — the list has only Environmental Technologies, so an environmental science course has nowhere of its own to sit.
What I propose
Tell me which the code is for. If ENVS is a science subject, the map entry is what should change and Biological Sciences is where an introductory survey belongs. If it is a technology subject, the map is right and the one course is mis-filed.
Why
It is one row today, so it is cheap either way — but the answer decides where every future course arriving under ENVS lands, and a science survey and a technology program are taught by different faculty under different minimum qualifications.
Your reply

2 · African American Studies and Asian American Studies have no courses at all

What this is
Both are MQ disciplines. Neither has a single course anywhere on the map, and neither has a canonical subject code. Their three siblings all do: Ethnic Studies 534 courses, Chicano Studies 35, Native American/American Indian Studies 33.
What I propose
I am not proposing anything — I cannot tell from the data whether this is a real absence or a filing gap. If colleges do teach these and we are filing them under Ethnic Studies, that is worth knowing. If the courses genuinely are not in MAP’s corpus, saying so here stops the next person looking.
Why
A discipline with zero courses looks identical whether nobody teaches it or we are putting its courses somewhere else. Only someone who knows the colleges can tell those apart.

3 · The Title 5 rename — confirm the shape before I build it

What this is
You ruled this morning that ESLN’s discipline name is a data defect to repair at source. It is, and it is eight names, not one: 53412 and 53414 are Title 5 section numbers the 19th-edition index prints beside the titles. 1,183 occurrences across 33 files, including the minted-course record and four alias maps.
What I propose
Strip the section number from the title and keep it in a field of its own rather than deleting it — then land the rename the way a re-mint lands: a dry run, an alias map, the promotions re-keyed, one cron window, a receipt.
Why
The number is real information — it says which regulation sets the minimum qualifications for that discipline — and it is only in the wrong place. A name that 33 files key on cannot change by find-and-replace, so this wants its own PR rather than riding along in another.
Your reply

4 · Who does the curator pass, and how big a first bite

What this is
The unfillable blanks are a named list now: 86 identities across 45 subject codes, each with what corroboration exists, rebuilt nightly. HOSP’s five and PHTO’s twelve belong in the same pass. Nobody is named to do it and no size is set.
What I propose
Name an owner. My suggestion for the first bite is the eight codes carrying three or more identities — roughly half the rows for a fraction of the reading — and to leave the thirty-odd single-course codes until someone is working in that discipline anyway.
Why
A list with no owner ages. And the ordering matters more than the effort here: the long tail is single courses at single colleges, which is exactly where a curator’s knowledge is worth most and a session’s guess is worth least.
Your reply

5 · Write down when a code is shared and when it is split

What this is
Three mechanisms exist and nothing says which to reach for: an umbrella (one discipline spans several codes — Foreign Languages, Kinesiology, the two Agriculture disciplines), a fan-in (two disciplines share one code, both names kept — FTVE), and a plain correction (the map disagrees with the CSR — the five you ruled this morning, and items 6 and 7 below).
What I propose
Record the test in the governance register beside DR-25: does the MQ list already carry the distinction? If no, it is an umbrella and we mint codes. If yes and the courses genuinely split, it is a fan-in. If yes and they do not split, it is a correction.
Why
That one question sorts every case on this sheet in a single pass, and it is the question I did not have written down — which is why ETHN and PHTO spent a day on the hard list.

6 · ETHN → Chicano Studies

What this is
The map sends ETHN to Ethnic Studies. So does ETHS, which is Ethnic Studies’ own canonical code with 534 courses. The CSR records ETHN as Chicano Studies’ canonical code, and 34 of its 35 courses are filed there — The Mexican-American in the History of the U.S. and the like.
What I propose
Change the map: ETHN → Chicano Studies.
Why
The map is the only store that disagrees, and it currently points two codes at one discipline while the discipline those courses belong to has its own code sitting unused. Same shape as the five you ruled this morning.

What this is
ETHA carries 33 courses, all under Native American/American Indian Studies, and the CSR records ETHA as that discipline’s canonical code. The subject map has no entry for it at all.
What I propose
Add "ETHA": "Native American/American Indian Studies".

8 · PHTO — leave the map, fix the courses

What this is
Twelve identities, every title read. Color Photo Lab 1 and 2 under Art; Digital Photo Lab 1, 2 and Digital Photos and the Computer under Multimedia; Introduction to Lighting, Portraiture on Location and Black and White Photo Lab 1 under Photography; Digital Photo Editing under Communication Studies; Phonetography under none. Two are plainly wrong: Individual Projects B under Machine Tool Technology and Materials and Processes Lab under Engineering Technology. They share one college’s numbering.
What I propose
Leave the map — PHTO → Photography is right — and send the twelve to the curator pass in item 4 as a per-course fix.
Why
The row reads as a disagreement only because the modal discipline among twelve scattered courses is 3 of 12, which is a majority of nothing. The titles are unanimous where the disciplines are not, and here the titles are the better evidence.
