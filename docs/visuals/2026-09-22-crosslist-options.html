1 · The frame: four kinds, not one population

reference: crosslist-frame

What this is
Of 16,480 unified course rows, 1,210 have member colleges whose subject codes resolve to more than one MQ discipline. Sorting them by why they disagree: 400 rest partly on a subject code of one or two letters, 107 name one discipline twice, 168 pair a specialization with its parent, and 535 remain as candidate dual homes. The largest single pair in the data is Kinesiology with Physical Education, at 213 rows.
Why
Cross-listing and nesting each answer part of this. Ruling once over all 1,210 would record Advanced Golf as an Ethnic Studies course with a second home, rather than as the mapping error it is.
What I propose
Adopt the four-kind sort as the frame, and rule each kind separately below. It might be wrong if you would rather take one blanket decision now and clean up afterward, which is faster and costs a re-mint later.

2 · A one or two letter subject code never decides a discipline

reference: crosslist-short-codes

What this is
25 mapped subject codes are one or two characters, and they are a signal on 400 of the 1,210. ES alone appears on 82 rows and maps to Ethnic Studies — but at many colleges ES means Exercise Science. Of 226 M-IDs minted with an ETHS prefix, 31 are physical activity courses: Adapted Physical Exercise, Advanced Fencing, Care and Prevention of Athletic and Recreational Injuries, Intercollegiate Track. Both meanings are genuinely present — Introduction to Racial and Ethnic Groups carries ES too.
Why
The map's own policy says short ambiguous codes stay unmapped. These slipped through, and a two-letter code cannot be resolved without reading the title. This is the same shape as the TOP ruling in Rule 7: a signal worth displaying, and not worth gating on.
What I propose
Hold a short-code discipline out of the canonical fold until a second signal agrees. It still displays. It might be wrong if you read the 25 codes as reliable enough to keep counting, in which case the ETHS identities below are the price.

4 · One discipline carrying two official names

reference: crosslist-same-name

What this is
107 rows pair two names the CO's own MQ list carries separately: Kinesiology with Physical Education (213 rows); Drama/Theater Arts with Theater Arts (40 rows). Kinesiology and Physical Education is the largest pair in the whole dataset. Intercollegiate Baseball resolves to both.
Why
Neither cross-listing nor nesting fits, because there is one discipline here and two words for it. The CO's vocabulary carries the duplication, so we resolve it on our side rather than asserting the list is wrong.
What I propose
Name one preferred form per pair and treat the other as an accepted alias for counting, keeping both for display and search. Use the picker to say which form wins. It might be wrong if the colleges mean something different by each, in which case these are dual homes and belong in the cross-list.

5 · A specialization and the discipline it sits inside

reference: crosslist-specialization

What this is
168 rows pair a narrower discipline with its parent: Art History and Commercial Art inside Art, Chicano Studies inside Ethnic Studies, Licensed Vocational Nursing inside Nursing, Commercial Music inside Music, Legal Assisting inside Law. Worked rows: The Music of Multicultural America (Commercial Music and Music); Arts of Africa, Oceania, and Indigenous North America (Art and Art History).
Why
This is the part of the question where nesting is the right answer. The relationship holds for every course in the discipline, so recording it once on the vocabulary beats recording it on each of the rows.
What I propose
Nest the vocabulary, not the courses. Give the discipline list a parent field; a course keeps its specialization and the counts roll up. It might be wrong if you want a course to appear under the parent by name, which a roll-up does not do on its own.

6 · Cross-list the courses that genuinely have two homes

reference: crosslist-dual-homes

What this is
535 rows remain once the other three kinds are set aside: Creative Darkroom: Alternative Processes (Art and Photography); Computer Network Fundamentals (Computer Information Systems and Computer Science); Beginning Advanced Ballet. (Dance and Theater Arts). The field already exists and already works — 9 rows carry xdisc today, and Work Experience Education carries 105 disciplines because it genuinely is taught in all of them.
Why
The MQ discipline list decides who may teach a course rather than what the course is. A course taught by an Art-qualified instructor at one college and a Digital Media one at another is already cross-listed in the field; a single parent is the part that disagrees with practice.
What I propose
Cross-list them through xdisc. It might be wrong if you would rather force one home per course and accept that some colleges will not find their own course where they look for it.

7 · One discipline stays primary, for counting

reference: crosslist-primary

What this is
The disc field is read by the Unified Courses tab and ten scripts, among them the SkyView builders, the discipline blanks worklist, the ESL relevel dry-run and the merge candidate queue. A course carrying two disciplines with no primary makes every discipline count ambiguous at once.
Why
This is the shape Rule 7 already uses for TOP: gate identity on one signal, keep the second for display. It lets a cross-list ship without touching a single counting surface.
What I propose
Keep disc as the one primary; xdisc serves display and search. It might be wrong if you want a college to see its own discipline's number include a course whose primary sits elsewhere.

8 · A cross-list never triggers a re-mint

reference: crosslist-remint-boundary

What this is
An M-ID's prefix follows its primary discipline's canonical SUBJ4. Of the 1,210, 59 already carry a C-ID rather than an M-ID, so an approved identifier is attached and a renumbering would reach outside our own staging layer.
Why
Cross-listing is reversible and a re-nest is a re-mint. Keeping the two apart is what makes the cross-list pass cheap enough to run across hundreds of rows.
What I propose
Adding xdisc leaves the identifier alone. Only a change of primary enters the re-mint playbook. It might be wrong if you want the prefix to reflect every home a course has, which no four-letter prefix can do.

9 · The rows whose recorded discipline matches no member at all

reference: crosslist-orphan-parents

What this is
113 of the 1,210 carry a discipline that appears nowhere among their members' mapped disciplines. The parent and every college under it disagree. Hydraulics (Fluid Power) sits under Agriculture while its members read Automotive Technology and Fire Technology.
Why
These are neither dual homes nor specializations. Sending them through a cross-list pass would record the disagreement as a second home and settle nothing.
What I propose
