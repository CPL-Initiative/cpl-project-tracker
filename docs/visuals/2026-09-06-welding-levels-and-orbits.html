Two identities, one title, no relationship between them

What it is
Two separate clustered identities carry a byte-identical title. Neither orbits the other; neither references the other. A curator searching the phrase gets both, with no way on the screen to tell why there are two.

Two groups, four identities, across the whole Welding discipline. Both are free-standing — the o field is absent on all four.

Proposed
Queue both as merge candidates for a faculty reviewer, and add an exact-title collision rule to the auditor. An identical title inside one discipline is not a judgment call about scope — it is either a merge or evidence that two genuinely different courses share a name, and a curator should be told which. It is cheap to detect and there are only two of them here, so the rule earns its place by catching the next one automatically.
What it is not
Not a session decision. Rule 7 and the re-mint playbook put the merge with a faculty reviewer; this only puts it in front of one.

2

The map suggests a home without looking at the level

your call
What it is
A stand-alone course is parked in orbit around the identity it is most aligned to. That placement is a suggestion, never a curation decision — nothing is written and the point stays hollow. But it is the suggestion the reader sees, and it is one drag from being accepted. Proficiency level is not one of the signals that produces it. The scorer weighs the local subject code, Dice overlap of title words, and SUBJ4.

The clearest single case is in Welding. Introduction to Welding (WELD M1109) carries 45 orbiting stand-alones, and six of them are Advanced:

The mechanism is plain: the shared word is welding, and one level word cannot outweigh the rest of the title. This is the same failure I fixed in the Similar courses ladder this morning by stripping level words before scoring — the orbit scorer needs the opposite treatment, where a level mismatch counts against the match rather than being invisible to it.

Proposed
Make a level mismatch a penalty in the orbit score, and show it on the point. Not a veto — a course with no level stated must still be placeable, and two rungs apart should cost more than one. The reader should be able to see that the map knows: a hollow point whose level disagrees with its host says so in the inspector, the way the other why-bits already do.
The cheaper half, if you prefer it
Say nothing in the scorer and only display the mismatch. That is the "gate identity, keep display" shape you have ruled for TOP codes, and it changes no placement — but it also leaves 559 two-rung suggestions on the screen.

4

Level is an axis we ruled we would carry, and we hold it for 22% of the state

needs you
What it is
You ruled for the course outline that a proficiency level belongs on the course and on each skill — two different axes, carry both, derive neither. Today the only place a level exists is the words in the title, and everything that reads one reads it from there: the Similar courses ladder, and any level rule we add to the orbit scorer under call 2.
Measured
ScopePointsState a levelShare
Statewide49,89610,95422%
Welding, all points51217935%
Welding, clustered identities only1708148%

So a level rule is silent on roughly four points in five, and reading a level out of a title is derivation — the thing the ruling says not to do. It works because the title is the only holder we have, not because it is right.

Proposed
Give the course outline a real level field, and let the title be a seed rather than the source. The outline is the next thing to be built and is the natural home; a curator confirms or sets the level once, it is stored, and the ladder and the orbit scorer both read the stored value and fall back to the title only where nothing is set.
What I need from you
Whether the level vocabulary is exactly Beginning / Intermediate / Advanced, or whether the four-rung local pattern in this discipline — Advanced Welding Level 1 through Level 4 — has to survive as something more than a title. That decides whether the field is a three-value enumeration or a level plus an ordinal.
