How to read a row

  • The pair is each reference's own definition of complete, and the word beside it says the test behind the count: a machine check, a curator's confirmation, the auditor's score, an assignment by rung. The completes differ in kind, so a percentage stays inside its row and no column sorts on it.
  • Every known issue names its owner: a procedure, a college, a curator, or Sam. A count opens the list it totals in the reference's tab.
  • Two stamps, apart: the machine stamp is the last run of the thing that writes the data, read against its declared cadence; the session stamp is the last time a session moved the lane. A stamp past its cadence reads stale; a reference with no run log reads could not be read, never current.
  • A call waiting on Sam is read from the decision sheet's own store, so an answered card leaves the row the moment he answers.

What this asks of Sam

  1. Is this the row? Five cells after the pair: where it stands, known issues by owner, settled procedures with the calls under them, and the two stamps.
  2. Is the starting order right: Program Requirements, Exhibit, Course, CR, Subjects? The words on each row move it, and the build keeps what you set.
  3. Each reference's definition of complete, as the row states it, becomes a card on the standing sheet when its Progress view is built.
