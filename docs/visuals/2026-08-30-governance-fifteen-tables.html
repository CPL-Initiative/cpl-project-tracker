The Fifteen Tables

The Governance tab's drift detector, now reading from the dependency map, surfaced fifteen editable tables the register has never heard of. Each needs a register row or a reasoned dismissal — never a bulk wave-through, because the reason is the point. This sheet proposes a disposition for every one, with who writes it today taken from the dependency map rather than guessed.

How to rule: reply in chat by number. yes accepts a proposal as written; or override with row (give it its own new row), fold: DR-xx, cadence, dismiss, or edit: your wording.

Example: 1–6 yes · 7 edit: overrides also need my sign-off · 15 row

A session then writes your rulings and reasons into the surface map and the register, and tightens the detector's noise guard back below thirty.

  • 5 new decision rights (covering 9 tables)

Shared memory

1 cpl_memory New row · DR-19
What it is
The shared memory table — the facts, pitfalls, and decisions every session is required to read before working. What it asserts steers behavior.
Who writes it today
The Memory tab (you and the team, including the Autogenerate drafter), and sessions at every checkpoint through the documented auto-write procedure.
Proposed decision right
What the shared memory asserts as true. Decider: Sam, through the verify / stale / supersede controls. Sessions may only propose; a row becomes trusted through the corroboration gate (a committed source, a second session, or your check), and every write is logged.

Team & RACI cluster — one right, three tables

All three are written from the Team & RACI tab, behind the reviewer sign-in or team phrase.

2 item_raci New row · DR-20
What it is
Who is Responsible, Accountable, Consulted, and Informed for every activity and project. Read by the cards, the master report, and the report generator.
Proposed decision right
Who assigns responsibility. Decider: the Accountable person for the item, with Sam as the default while owners are being filled in. Acceptance: a signed-in team member; assignments name people, not roles.
3 item_updates Same row · DR-20
What it is
Posted status updates per activity and project. These travel farthest of the three: they surface on the card faces, in the master report, and in the Annual Report — outward-facing text.
Proposed decision right
Covered by DR-20, with one added acceptance standard because of the reach: an update is written as something you would be comfortable seeing quoted in the Annual Report, and carries its author.
4 team_members Same row · DR-20
What it is
The team directory — names and contact details (staff information, not student data). Also feeds the nudge-email builder.
Proposed decision right
Covered by DR-20. Acceptance: entries are real staff who know they are listed; removals happen when someone leaves, not by cleanup sweeps.

Program tracking

5 liftoff_state Fold into DR-13
What it is
The Mission Control tracker's task statuses and decision-branch choices for the Lift Off plan — each chosen branch doubles as the decision log.
Why fold
DR-13 already governs the workplan itself (projects, goals, associations, lifecycle). Lift Off is the same family of program-tracking state, written by the same signed-in team. One decision right, one owner, less register sprawl.
6 map_data_quality New cadence · CA-07
What it is
Data-quality flags raised against MAP data from the quality view — a worklist of things someone should fix or dismiss.
Why a cadence, not a decision right
A flag queue's failure mode is not a bad write — it is nobody ever triaging it (the register already caught one cadence that never ran). Model it on the Sierra feedback triage: a named owner and a rhythm, so the register can measure whether triage actually happens.
7 factsheet_overrides New row · DR-21
What it is
Hand overrides on figures shown by the public CPL Fact Sheet — the highest-stakes table in this batch, because it changes numbers journalists and the public read.
Who writes it today
The Fact Sheet's edit layer.
Proposed decision right
Who may make a published figure disagree with the live data, and against what standard. Decider: Sam. Acceptance: every override carries its source and date, and only aggregate figures are ever shown — per the standing disclosure rules.

Intake and reflection

14 cpl_adoption_interest New row · DR-23
What it is
The intake queue where colleges express interest in adopting a pathway, submitted from the CPL Pathways tab — requests from outside the team, waiting on a response.
Proposed decision right
Who owns the intake and its follow-up. An unanswered interest queue is a silent promise to a college. Decider: Sam until delegated. Acceptance: each entry resolves to a real college identity, and the row names a response expectation the register can measure.
15 cpl_reflections Dismiss, with reason
What it is
The team reflections stream, written from the First Light page and read only by the weekly digest that goes back to the same team.
Proposed dismissal
A journaling surface whose audience is its own authors. No third party relies on it, nothing downstream publishes it, and burdening it with decision rights would discourage the writing it exists for. The reason lands in the surface map so the dismissal stays reviewable — and reversible if the digest ever grows an outside audience.
