# Review of Sam's revision, against two statutes we did not have

> **Sam, 2026-08-26:** *"See my revision of your draft and consider the newly
> attached docs… The Career Passport article supports everything we're planning
> and adds new wrinkles. The article focused on Calbright and competency-based
> education does as well."*

He is right on both counts, and one of the two does more than support the
package — **it moves an amendment out of Tier 2 and into Tier 1.**

---

## 1. ⭐ The headline: §75013(b) is a CPL Initiative duty living outside Article 9

`EC §75013` was added by **Stats. 2026, Ch. 79, Sec. 14 — the same bill and the
same chapter as Article 9** (which is Sec. 16). Same act, same effective date,
read together.

> **§75013(b)** — *"**As part of the Credit for Prior Learning Initiative**, the
> chancellor's office, in partnership with the Academic Senate of the California
> Community Colleges, **shall make credit for prior learning recommendations for
> each program** offered by the California Online Community College **and similar
> programs offered by other community colleges, including programs that prepare
> students for industry certifications.** The amount of credit for prior learning
> recommended under this subdivision **for each program or industry certification
> shall be the same systemwide for all community colleges**… On or before **July 1,
> 2027**… **Community colleges are strongly encouraged to award students credit for
> prior learning consistent with these recommendations.**"*

Three consequences, in descending order of value:

### ⭐ 1a. It is the authority for the (c) broadening — which was Tier 2

Sam's (c) edit lets credit attach to *"a local or transfer general education
area, or degree-applicable elective credit"* rather than only to individually
identified catalog courses. In the November package that was **Tier 2, item 9**,
argued on its merits. It now rests on statute:

- §75013(b) requires recommendations **per program or industry certification**,
  at an **amount that is the same systemwide**. A recommendation of the form
  *"this certification is worth N units toward this program"* **cannot be
  implemented by a regulation that permits credit only against individually
  identified catalog courses.**
- §78093.2(b)(2) and (c)(1)(A) already contemplate CPL applying **"for general
  education, transfer, and major preparation."**

**Move it to Tier 1 and cite both.** This is the single biggest change to the
package's shape, and it is why the Calbright article "supports everything."

### ⭐ 1b. It repairs the weaker of our two named stretches

The drafting notes concede that *"shall consider"* in (c) rests on §78093.2(a)(3),
which is permissive (*campuses "may adopt"*). §75013(b) is not permissive: the CO
**shall make** the recommendations, they **shall be** uniform systemwide, and
colleges are **strongly encouraged to award consistent with them**. A Title 5
duty merely to *consider* is materially weaker than the statutory encouragement
to *award* — which is a much easier defense than the one we were prepared to make.

⚠️ **Do not overclaim.** *"Strongly encouraged"* is still not *"shall,"* so
"shall consider" remains a Title 5 layer. But it is now a small step from an
express statutory encouragement rather than a step up from a permissive clause.
Keep naming it; the concession just costs less.

### 1c. It is a fourth source of credit recommendations, and (c) omits it

Our (c) names three: ACE, faculty discipline review groups (§78093.2(a)(3)), and
local discipline faculty experts. §75013(b) creates a **fourth, distinct** source
— the chancellor's office **in partnership with the ASCCC** — that no other
provision covers, with a **July 1, 2027** delivery date.

**Recommended (c) insertion**, after the faculty-discipline-review-groups clause:

> …by the chancellor's office in partnership with the Academic Senate for
> California Community Colleges pursuant to Education Code section 75013(b),

## 2. §88782 — the Career Passport. Confirmed, and load-bearing

**First, it closes an open item.** §88782 **is** the California Career Passport
Program. The adopted NOTE's citation is correct and the November proposal's
§88792(b)(1) was the transposition. ✅ **Drafting note (3) is now false and must
be updated** — see §5.

It is also more than a citation:

| provision | what it gives us |
|---|---|
| **(a)(1)** | The Career Passport displays *"preparation for employment, academic records, and **credit for prior learning**."* CPL is a **named input** to a statewide digital credential. |
| **(b)** | The program is built by leveraging *"existing tools for validating learning in nonacademic contexts, including… providing credit for prior learning and **the California Mapping Articulated Pathways Initiative**."* ⭐ **MAP is named in the Education Code.** |
| **(a)(2)** | Individuals curate *"relevant **validated skills**… for **skills-based and competency-informed hiring**."* Pairs with §78093.2(a)(5) and §75013. |
| **(c)(3)** | Aggregated information must be available *"at **no or low cost** to the individual."* |
| **(g)** | Express FERPA / privacy compliance obligations. |

### ⭐ 2a. §88782(b) is the citation Sam's (b)(4) is missing

His (b)(4) currently names the platform as a bare parenthetical — **"(MAP
platform)"** — with no authority and no definition. A product name in
regulatory text with no statutory anchor is the kind of thing that gets struck
in review, and it goes stale if the platform is ever renamed.

§88782(b) fixes that: the Legislature has already named it. **Recommended
replacement for the parenthetical:**

> …the chancellor's office systemwide credit for prior learning infrastructure
> described in Education Code section 78093.2(a)(2) and referenced in Education
> Code section 88782(b)…

⚠️ **A naming wrinkle worth knowing before you cite it.** The statutory name is
*"the California Mapping Articulated Pathways Initiative"* — the Ed. Code uses
**"Initiative"** for what our own convention calls the **MAP platform**, reserving
"Initiative" for the CPL Initiative. §78093.2(a) and (d)(1)(D) use the same
phrase. The convention still governs our prose; **statutory names get quoted
verbatim.** Flagging it because it will read as an inconsistency to anyone who
has been told the naming rule.

### ⭐ 2b. It is the argument for keeping the (l) you deleted

Your revision deletes adopted (l) — *"The student's academic record shall be
clearly annotated to reflect that credit was earned through an alternative method
of assessment for prior learning"* — and reuses the letter for grading.

**That annotation is the precondition for two things the statutes now require:**

1. **(n) reciprocity.** §78093.2(b)(2) obliges a college to accept CPL
   *"transcribed by another campus."* A receiving college can only identify
   transcribed CPL if the sending college annotated it. Delete the annotation
   duty and the reciprocity clause loses the thing it operates on.
2. **The Career Passport.** §88782(a)(1) displays CPL specifically. What
   populates it is the academic record. An unannotated award is invisible to it.

**Recommendation: restore adopted (l) and put the new grading clause at (o).**
Cheap, and it removes the only place where the package deletes a duty rather
than adding one.

### 2c. It gives the fee amendment a second anchor

Tier 2, Amendment 7 (no fee for alternative methods other than examination)
rested on **ASCCC Resolution 103.04** plus §78093.2(d)(1)(A)'s *"equitably."*
§88782(c)(3) adds *"at no or low cost to the individual."*

⚠️ **Supporting, not dispositive** — (c)(3) governs access to the *Passport*,
not the price of a CPL assessment. But it is a better second leg than the
amendment had, and it makes a fee barrier at the CPL stage harder to defend.

### 2d. If (b)(4) obliges districts to place student records in MAP, cite the privacy floor

Your (b)(4) now requires districts to *"maintain student CPL records"* in the
infrastructure. §88782(g) is the model for what that obligation carries with it
(FERPA, the federal Privacy Act). Worth a clause; at minimum worth a sentence in
the rationale, because a regulation directing student records into a statewide
system will be asked about privacy.

## 2A. §66025.71 — Sam's read is right, with two things worth banking

> **Sam, 2026-08-26:** *"Attached 66025.71 is an older law that should be cited.
> I don't think it has any impact on revisions."*

**Correct — it drives no new amendment.** It is already Authority in the adopted
NOTE and already cited inside (c), and it is JST/military-scoped, so the broader
sources in the amended (c) are carried by their own citations (§78093.2(a)(3),
and now §75013(b)). The structure is sound as drafted. Two observations, though:

### ⭐ 2A-a. It gives the website-publication amendment an older, squarely on-point hook

> **§66025.71(a)(2)** — *"Each community college campus **shall post on its
> internet website** the most recent policy adopted pursuant to this subdivision."*

Amendment 1 adds *"and on the district's or college's public internet website"* to
(b), and we justified it from §78093.2(a)(2) (public visibility). §66025.71(a)(2)
is a **pre-existing duty on districts** to publish CPL policy on the web. It is
narrower — it covers the JST policy specifically — but it establishes that web
publication of CPL policy is already the legislative expectation, not a new
burden this package invents. **Worth a line in the rationale**: the amendment
generalizes an obligation districts have carried since 2019.

### ⚠️ 2A-b. Its GE list is the superseded one — another reason to keep the Cal-GETC sentence

§66025.71(a)(1)(A)(i) still directs credit toward *"California Intersegmental
General Education Transfer Curriculum, California State University General
Education Breadth, or local community college general education requirements."*
That is **verbatim the language the BOG replaced with Cal-GETC on 12 August**.

The regulation is not wrong — Cal-GETC superseded those frameworks by other law —
but §55050 has modernized while a statute it cites as **Authority** still names
the old ones. Two consequences:

- It is a **third reason not to delete the adopted Cal-GETC sentence** (§3d): that
  sentence is the only place §55050 reconciles itself with §66025.71's list.
  Delete it and the section cites an authority whose GE vocabulary it no longer
  reflects anywhere.
- Someone at the CO comparing the two will notice. **Have the answer ready**
  rather than be asked: Cal-GETC replaced IGETC and CSU GE Breadth systemwide;
  the regulation tracks current law and the statute's list is legacy text.

Nothing here needs drafting. It is ammunition and a question to be ready for.

## 3. Recommended revisions to the revision

Ranked by how much trouble each will cause, worst first.

### 🔴 3a. (m) — restore "gender and race/ethnicity"

The revision **deletes** disaggregation by gender and race/ethnicity and replaces
it with CPL type and student population (military, working adult, apprentice or
journeyperson, high school, noncredit, not-for-credit).

**The new categories are excellent. The deletion is the single most dangerous
edit in the package.** Three reasons:

1. It **deletes adopted text** in a filing framed as conformity.
2. **§75013(c)(2)(B), in the same bill, requires "the demographics of enrolled
   students."** Removing demographic reporting from the CPL regulation while its
   sibling section adds it is a contradiction a reader will find.
3. §78093.2(d)(1)(A) conditions the money on increasing access **"equitably for
   all eligible students."** Equity you do not disaggregate is equity you cannot
   evidence — and the allocation depends on evidencing it.

**Recommendation: keep both.** *"…disaggregated by credit for prior learning
type, student population (military, working adult, apprentice or journeyperson,
high school, noncredit, and not-for-credit), gender, and race/ethnicity…"*

⭐ Keep *"were eligible for and received"* — that is a genuinely strong addition.
It makes the gap between credit that exists and credit that is awarded a
**reported measure**, which is the backlog the whole initiative is trying to move.

### 🔴 3b. (b) — the procedures list has become a list of documents

The revision replaces the adopted **procedures** list with the §78093.2(b)(1)
**documents and credentials** list. The sentence now reads:

> *"**Procedures** for students to attain credit for prior learning shall include,
> but not be limited to, Joint Services Transcripts…, industry-recognized
> credentials…, portfolios…, and standardized assessments…"*

Those are not procedures, they are evidence. Two further problems:

- ⚠️ **It duplicates (e) verbatim.** The identical list is already inserted in
  (e), which is where §78093.2(b)(1) puts it — attached to the evaluation duty.
  The same sentence appearing twice in one section invites a drafting objection.
- ⚠️ **It strikes adopted text** the BOG amended three weeks ago (that list is
  where *"credential documentation"* → *"certifications and documented trainings"*
  and *"exams"* → *"examinations"* landed on 12 August).

**Recommendation: restore the adopted procedures list in (b); keep the documents
list in (e) only.**

### 🔴 3c. ⭐ Restoring "evaluation of authenticated competencies" is the competency hook

The struck (b) list contains the **only competency language in §55050**. Striking
it cuts against exactly the direction you flagged:

- **§78093.2(a)(5)** — *"Enable, document, and **validate skills and competencies**
  earned through educational, workplace, military, and other experiences."*
- **§88782(a)(2)** — *"validated skills… for skills-based and **competency-informed**
  hiring."*
- **§75013(a)** — the CO must develop CBE-to-FTES funding recommendations
  *"systemwide"*; **(c)(3)** speaks of *"competency-based direct assessment."*

⭐ **And the BOG has already moved this way**: the 12 August revision changed (f)
from *"mastery of the course **content**"* to *"mastery of the course
**outcomes**."* Outcomes-based mastery is the competency-based framing, and it is
now adopted text. (h) still says *"course content"* — an asymmetry worth noticing,
though I would **not** touch (h) in this package.

**Recommendation: restore "evaluation of authenticated competencies" at minimum.**
It is one phrase, it is adopted text, and it is the only word in the section that
a competency-based college can point at.

### 🟠 3d. (c) — do not delete the Cal-GETC sentence

The revision strikes the adopted *"Upon a student's demonstration of sufficient
mastery… Cal-GETC and local community college general education requirements…
Award of credit may be made to electives…"* sentences.

- That Cal-GETC sentence **is** the 12 August amendment (it replaced the
  IGETC/CSU-GE Breadth text). Deleting a clause the BOG adopted three weeks ago
  is the most likely single trigger for the package being recharacterized as
  reopening the rulemaking rather than conforming to statute.
- It is **not inconsistent** with what you are adding: your first sentence says
  what credit may attach to; the adopted sentence says where it should
  preferentially be applied. They coexist.
- ⚠️ You would also be deleting your own support — the adopted elective sentence
  is part of the authority for *"degree-applicable elective credit."*

**Recommendation: keep both. Add, don't replace.**

### 🟠 3e. (b)(4) — "independent institutions" is a system duty, not a district duty

The revision requires each district's policies to make CPL opportunities
*"to the extent feasible, made available to interested independent institutions
of higher education."* The statute says something narrower:

> **§78093.2(c)(2)** — *"To the extent that resources are available, **the
> California Community Colleges system** may collaborate with available and
> interested independent institutions of higher education."*

Three mismatches: the duty is on **the system**, not districts; it is
**permissive** (*may*); and it is conditioned on **resources being available**,
not on feasibility. A district also has no mechanism to publish to independent
institutions — the CO's infrastructure does that.

**Recommendation: move it to the chancellor's-office side of the clause**, or
drop it from (b)(4) and raise it in the rationale. As drafted it is a **third
stretch**, and unlike the other two it is easy to attack.

⚠️ Relatedly, the revision removes the alternative-compliance path (*"or supply
regular and frequent updates of required data to that infrastructure"*), making
use of the platform mandatory. §78093.2(a)(2) places the infrastructure duty on
the **chancellor's office**. A hard district mandate to use one platform is
defensible via §78093.2(d)(2) (allocation conditioned on demonstrated
implementation) but it **is** an ask — name it rather than let it be found.

### 🟡 3f. (i) — removing "registered… and in good standing" is Tier 2

Striking *"A separate examination shall be conducted for each course"* and
*"only to a student who is registered at the college and in good standing"* is
substantive policy, not conformity. There is a decent Article 9 argument for the
registration half — §78093.2(b)(1) speaks of **"all incoming students,"** i.e.
before enrollment — but *"in good standing"* has no Article 9 hook at all.

**Recommendation: split.** Keep the incoming-student argument if you want it;
move "good standing" to the Tier 2 action.

### 🟡 3g. New grading (l) needs its §55023 companion

*"…students shall be offered a 'credit' option"* directs colleges to award a
symbol that **§55023 must authorize**. Your November package paired them
deliberately. Filed alone, the regulation requires a grade the grading
regulation may not recognize.

**Recommendation: this is Tier 2, item 7, and it should travel with §55023** —
which is also the reason it widens the rulemaking to a second section and was
recommended for a separate action.

## 4. ⚠️ One defect in the file itself

**In (c), the word "only" was deleted without a tracked change.**

Adopted: *"Credit may be awarded for prior experience or prior learning **only**
for individually identified courses…"*
Your file: *"…or prior learning  for individually identified courses…"* — the word
is gone and replaced by a plain space run carrying no `w:del`.

The nearby *", and only"* **is** properly tracked; this one is not. So a reviewer
who rejects every change does not get the adopted section back, and a reviewer
reading the redline sees no sign that a restriction was removed. The change is
substantive and matches your intent — it should simply be **visible**.

**Fix:** in Word with track changes on, retype "only" and delete it again.

*I verified this by reading the file's XML directly. Two other apparent
mismatches — in (b) and (l) — turned out to be artifacts of my own check
mis-handling Word's paragraph-mark insertions, not problems in your document. The
adopted (l) is deleted properly and deliberately; see §2b for why I would still
restore it.*

> the California Career Passport Program (added by Stats. 2025, Ch. 9, Sec. 13
> (AB 123)). The November 2025 proposal's citation to § 88792(b)(1) was a

for Prior Learning Initiative duty defined there:

> Reference: Sections 70901, 70902, 75013, 78093, 78093.1, 78093.2, and 88782,
> Education Code.

⚠️ Reference only, **not Authority** — §75013 confers no rulemaking power.
