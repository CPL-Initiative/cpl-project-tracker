# The clauses were not wrong. They were early.

> **Sam, 2026-08-26:** *"Here's an older 55050 revision I was working on before the
> newest revision was completed… I would have preferred to use my revisions, but the
> process didn't allow it and the CO went with the current revisions. Note that my
> revision references AB 123, which is the same as SB 135."*

---

## 1. ⭐ The argument, in one paragraph

The **November 2025 Regulatory Action Proposal** (Sam Lee, Executive Advisor;
Erin Larson, Staff Lead; 5C workgroup) proposed a §55050 whose own rationale
says, in writing, that several clauses were drafted *"to align with pending 2025
trailer bill language"* and *"Align to CPL trailer bill (pending)."*

Those clauses did not survive into the **2026-08-12 final text**.

**The bill is no longer pending.** AB 123's CPL provisions were enacted as
**SB 135 → Ed. Code Article 9 (§78093–78093.2), effective 2026-07-13.** The
stated reason for holding that language has expired, and in several cases the
Legislature has now made the same requirement *mandatory*.

This is the strongest posture available for the CO conversation, and it is not an
argument about preference: **the record already says why those clauses were
deferred, and the condition they were deferred on has been met.**

⚠️ The renumbering in the final text **is** Sam's — (m)→(a), (a)→(b), (d)→(c),
(h)→(d), (j)→(e), (b)→(f), (k)→(g), (c)→(h), (e)→(i), (g)→(j), (i)→(k), (f)→(l),
(l)→(m), (n) deleted. So this is not a competing structure. **It is the same
section with its substance restored.**

### Also missing from BOTH documents

**Reciprocity.** Neither the November draft nor the final text contains a clause
accepting another campus's transcribed CPL. **§78093.2(b)(2) now makes it
mandatory.** This is the one place where the amendment package must add something
genuinely new rather than restore — see
[`t5_55050_article9_amendments.md`](t5_55050_article9_amendments.md) §Gap 1, and
register row **#2**, whose *"no secondary review"* remains the Title 5 addition.

## 3. Where SB 135 came out NARROWER than AB 123

Sam's *"AB 123 … is the same as SB 135"* is right on the substance and worth two
footnotes, because both cut against clauses in his draft:

1. **AB 123 objective 3** required infrastructure that could *"identify CPL
   opportunities **and can facilitate the awarding** of credit."* **§78093.2(a)(2)
   keeps only "visible and accessible to the public."** The awarding half is gone,
   which weakens a *"utilize the infrastructure"* mandate slightly — though (d)(2)
   restores leverage by conditioning money on demonstrated implementation.
2. **AB 123 objective 2** named foreign credentials explicitly; **§78093.1(c) does
   not**. Item 10 above.

Neither is fatal. Both should be known before someone at the CO raises them.

## 4. Recommended package

**Restore, do not redraft.** Same section, same numbering, substance returned —
and for each clause, the citation that did not exist in November:

- **Tier 1 — now compelled by statute.** Items 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 plus reciprocity.
  Each cites Article 9. The ask is not *"we prefer this"*, it is *"Title 5 does not
  yet implement §78093.2."*
- **Tier 2 — still Title 5 policy, unchanged in strength.** Items 7 (CR symbol,
  with §55023), 8 (fees), 9 (program requirements), 11 (§55052/§55052.5). These
  are argued on their merits exactly as they were in November; Article 9 neither
  helps nor hurts them.

Keeping the tiers visibly separate is what makes Tier 1 credible. A package that
presents every restored clause as statutorily compelled invites a reader to test
the weakest one and discount the rest.

**And add §§78093–78093.2 to the NOTE's Authority and Reference.** The final text
cites neither.

⚠️ The November NOTE cites Reference **§88792(b)(1)**; the final text cites
**§88782**. Sam's own background section describes **§88782** (Career Passport).
One of the two is a typo and it should be settled before filing — not diagnosed
from here.

## 5. The separate, larger opening

Independent of the regulation, and worth raising in the same meeting:

**§78093.2(d)(2) conditions every allocation on *"the metrics described in
paragraph (1)"* — and paragraph (1) describes four goals, not metrics.** The
statute supplies none. The Chancellor's Office must define them.

The Implementation Funding model is the only worked, college-by-college
quantification of those four goals in existence, and it already covers (A) access
and (B) completion, with (D) satisfied by construction since MAP *is* the named
pilot project. **(C) advancing career attainment is the acknowledged gap**, which
Sam is addressing through CPL Projects and leadership on the CA Credential
Registry and Career Passport.

Offering the model as the (d)(1) metrics needs no statute and no change to
attendance accounting — which is more than register row #7 can say.
