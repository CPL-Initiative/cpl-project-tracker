## What I found instead of what we expected

**All 16 CPL revisions already have `blast_why` text.** There was nothing to
write from scratch. But **5 of the 16 carry text that is byte-identical to a
neighbour's**, along with an identical `blast_rank`:

| rows | shared rank | currently all say |
|---|---|---|
| **#6 · #13 · #16** | 5 | *"Ends the geographic lottery where the same credential earns credit at one college and nothing at the next…"* |
| **#9 · #10** | 3 | *"Removes the stigma-flag that triggers secondary review, and guarantees every award … lands on the transcript."* |

⚠️ **For a Chancellor's Office submission this is the thing to fix.** A reader
who meets the same justification three times cannot tell why three separate asks
exist — and the tied ranks make the ordering look arbitrary rather than reasoned.
Each of these rows genuinely does something different; only the prose collapsed.

⚠️ **The 4 dual-enrollment rows have no `blast_why` and should not get one.**
They are a marked SAMPLE written as neutral review prompts, never positions
("*A review would ask whether…*"). Advocacy text there would misrepresent them.

## The five drafts

Each is grounded in that row's own **Approach** and **Consideration** text — no
new statutory claims. Edit freely; the point is that they stop being interchangeable.

### #9 — Remove the requirement to flag CPL on the transcript
> The annotation is what invites the second look. While §55050 requires the
> record to be marked as prior-learning credit, a receiving college can spot it
> and re-adjudicate it — so the flag, not the credit, is what fails to travel.
> **This is the subtractive half of the transcript principle: stop marking it.**

### #10 — Transcribe all CPL, including waived / GE-area / elective units
> Credit that is awarded but never recorded leaves the student holding nothing
> they can show. Where a course requirement is waived rather than posted, the
> award can disappear from the transcript entirely — so the student carries the
> benefit only as long as they stay at that college.
> **This is the affirmative half of the same principle: record all of it.**

### #6 — Honor ASCCC statewide credit recommendations
> The recommendations exist and are already funded; what is missing is any duty
> to act on them. Today they bind only as a condition of a grant, so their force
> ends when the funding does. **Mirroring the existing ACE clause makes the
> state's own faculty recommendations durable rather than contingent.**

### #13 — Common Course Crosswalk via ASCCC Pathways to Credit
> This is the mechanism the other two asks depend on — without a crosswalk from
> prior-learning sources to statewide course equivalencies, there is nothing
> concrete for a college to honor. **Aligning it to AB 1111 / C-ID is what turns
> "honor the recommendation" into a specific course a registrar can post.**

### #16 — Honor MAP CPL recommendations (parallels the ACE clause)
> MAP already publishes the credit recommendations colleges consult, so this ask
> changes what is binding, not what exists. **It uses the exact regulatory
> template California already applies to ACE — the most precedented ask in the
> package, and the one hardest to object to on novelty grounds.**
