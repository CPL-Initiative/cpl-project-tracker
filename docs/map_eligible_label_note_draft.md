# Draft note to MAP — the word "Eligible"

**Status: DRAFT. Sam sends it; a session never does.** Authorized by his
decision-sheet verdict of 2026-09-15 (item 6, `yes`, on the proposal *"Raise it
with MAP as a labeling question — not a defect — and let them decide. I would
draft the note; you would send it."*).

House voice applies here — this is a college-facing communication, so it carries
no bold, no bullets and no glyphs, it concedes before it argues, it preserves
MAP's authority over the naming, and the ask sits last and small.

## The measurement behind it (re-verify before sending)

Measured 2026-09-15 against the published performance artifact:

- The dashboard's "Eligible" figure reconciles exactly to applied: statewide
  **220,371 units = `pa_u` 219,704 + `ppa_u` 666.5**; Alameda **84 = 78 + 6**.
- The API column we pull, also named `Eligible Credits`, carries the full JST
  parse: **1,410,529**.
- 91% of eligible *students* become applied; **15.6% of eligible *units*** do.

⚠️ Re-measure at send time. These are a snapshot, and the reconciliation is the
whole argument — a number that has drifted turns a labeling question into a
claim MAP can dismiss.

## The draft

> Subject: The word "Eligible" on the CPL Dashboard
>
> Pedro,
>
> Our figures and the dashboard's agree on the arithmetic. They differ on one
> word, and the naming is yours rather than ours, so I am raising it as a
> question.
>
> The dashboard reports an "Eligible" figure that reconciles exactly to the
> credits colleges have applied. Statewide it reads 220,371 units. Our applied
> measure reaches the same number once we add the two cohorts we track
> separately, 219,704 and 666.5. Alameda reads 84, and we get 78 plus 6. The
> match holds college by college.
>
> The API column we pull is also called Eligible Credits, and it carries
> something else: every credit recommendation parsed from the student's
> transcript, before a college decides which of them it can articulate.
> Statewide that column reads 1,410,529.
>
> Both numbers are right. They answer different questions — what a student
> arrived with, and what a college has applied — and the second is about a
> sixth of the first. A reader who opens the dashboard and the API in the same
> week meets one word and two quantities.
>
> This is not a data error, and we are not asking for either number to change.
> Our funding model now measures the applied rung, which is the rung the
> dashboard already shows, so the two stay consistent whatever you decide.
>
> If it is useful, we can send the college-by-college reconciliation we ran.
> You would then be able to judge whether the label is worth revisiting.
>
> Sam

## Why this was worth raising at all

Sam described the effective / eligible / applied naming as something the team
has "gone around and around over the years" on. One word carrying two
quantities 6.4 times apart, on two surfaces the same people read, is the most
likely source of that. It is also the one part of it we cannot fix ourselves.
