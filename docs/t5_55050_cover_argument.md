# The argument, and the call on Tier 2

> **Sam, 2026-08-26:** *"Go with your gut on Tier 1 & 2 and the argument:)"*

---

## 1. The call: file Tier 1 alone. Tier 2 follows separately.

**Not "include" and not "cut" — split.**

The two tiers carry **different burdens of proof**, and that is the whole reason:

| | Tier 1 | Tier 2 |
|---|---|---|
| the question a reader asks | *Does Article 9 say this?* | *Is this good policy?* |
| how it is answered | read the statute — five minutes | a debate |
| has it been had before? | no. Article 9 did not exist in November | **yes, in November, and these lost** |
| outcome if contested | you win | genuinely uncertain |

**Bundling makes Tier 1 inherit Tier 2's burden.** The moment the package also
asks for the grading symbol and the fee change, a reader can characterize the
whole filing as re-litigating a settled negotiation — and the natural move for
anyone who does not want to reopen it is to decline the package, not to separate
it for you.

That is an asymmetric trade of a near-certain win for a contested one. Don't take it.

⚠️ **The strongest counter-argument, stated fairly:** the rulemaking is open
*now*, and reopening §55050 later costs another full cycle — 5C, field vetting,
Consultation Council, two BOG reads. On the November timeline that is roughly six
months. Tier 2 was also already vetted through 5C, so it is not new to the field.
That is a real cost and it is why this is a judgment call rather than an obvious
one.

**It still loses**, for one reason: Tier 1 is not a request. It is a defect
report. Defect reports get fixed on their own schedule and do not queue behind
policy debates — but only while they are legible as defect reports.

### Where each Tier 2 item should actually go

- **Amendment 7 (no fee)** — carries **ASCCC Resolution 103.04**. Strongest of
  the four on its own merits. Best candidate for a near-term separate action.
- **Amendment 6 (CR symbol)** — needs the companion **§55023** amendment, so it
  widens the rulemaking to a second section. That is exactly the kind of scope
  growth that slows a filing. Separate.
- **Amendment 8 (course *or program requirement*)** — the broadest change in the
  whole package; it alters what CPL may attach to and touches three subdivisions.
  **Deserves its own action regardless.**
- **Amendment 9 (§55052 / §55052.5 consolidation)** — a different section
  entirely. It was always a separate item; keep it that way.

## 2. The argument

### Do not argue preference. Argue the gap.

*"I would have preferred my version"* is the argument that already lost, and it
invites the answer that already ended it: the process did not allow it.

**The argument that works is about the Chancellor's Office's own exposure, not
about the November draft:**

> **Title 5 §55050 is about to be filed without implementing the statute it
> operates under.**

Three things make it land, and all three are checkable rather than arguable.

**1. It is verifiable in one search.** The 2026-08-12 final text contains **zero**
references to §78093, Article 9, or SB 135, and **no** reciprocity clause. Its
NOTE cites neither. Anyone can confirm this in five minutes.

**2. Nobody is at fault, and say so first.** The final text's accessibility stamp
reads **6/19/26**; Article 9 took effect **7/13/26**. The drafting simply predates
the statute. Leading with this removes any suggestion the package is a criticism —
it is a timing fact, and it is the reason a fix is needed rather than a reason
anyone erred.

**3. The remedy is small and does not disturb the filing.** Same section, same
lettering, four amended subdivisions plus one appended and a NOTE update.
**Nothing re-letters (a)–(m), so the companion §55051 amendment is untouched.**
A package that can be adopted without re-opening anything else is a package that
can move.

### ⭐ And the sharpest point, which is about districts and not about us

**A regulation that omits a mandatory statutory duty does not make the duty go
away.** §78093.2(b)(1) — evaluate the prior learning documents of *all* incoming
students — and §78093.2(b)(2) — accept transcribed CPL from other campuses — bind
every campus **today**, by force of the Education Code.

So a district that follows the filed §55050 to the letter is still out of
compliance with the Ed. Code. **Title 5's job is to tell districts what the law
requires of them, and on these two duties it currently does not.**

That is the sentence to lead with in the room. It is not about the November draft,
it is not about MAP, and it is not a preference. It is the ordinary reason
regulations get conformed to new statutes.

### The record already makes half the case for you

The November 2025 Regulatory Action Proposal's own rationale says several clauses
were written *"to align with pending 2025 trailer bill language"* and *"Align to
CPL trailer bill (pending)."*

**That is the CO's own file recording why the substance was held, and naming the
condition.** The condition has been met. You are not asking anyone to revisit a
judgment — you are pointing out that a deferral has come due.

⚠️ **Use this second, not first.** Led with, it reads as *"my clauses should come
back."* Following the compliance gap, it reads as *"and the file already
anticipated this."* Same fact, different weight, entirely down to order.

### What not to say

- **Don't call it an error.** It is a sequencing artifact, and framing it as a
  mistake makes the fix about the people who drafted it.
- **Don't lead with MAP.** §78093.2(a)(2) and (d)(1)(D) name the infrastructure
  and the pilot project, so the hook is real and it will hold — but leading with
  it makes the package look like an initiative asking for its own codification.
  Let the statute introduce MAP.
- **Don't claim Tier 1 is unanimous.** Two clauses go beyond a restatement, and a
  careful reader will find them: *shall consider* in (c) sits on §78093.2(a)(3)'s
  *"may adopt"*, and the second sentence of the new (n) adds *no secondary review*
  where the statute says only *accept*. **Name both before they are found** — a
  package that flags its own two additions is trusted on the other seven clauses.

## 3. The NOTE citation — recommendation, with the caveat that matters

**Use §88782.** Two independent sources agree: the CO's own 2026-08-12 final text,
and the November proposal's *background* section, which describes §88782 as the
California Career Passport Program. The lone outlier is the NOTE line in the
November proposal itself, which is the likeliest place for a transposition.

⚠️ **This is a recommendation from agreement, not from reading the code.** This
session cannot reach `leginfo`. It takes someone thirty seconds with the Education
Code in front of them, and a citation in a filed regulation should be confirmed
that way rather than inferred. **Confirm; do not adopt on this reasoning alone.**

## 4. The order to raise things in

1. **The compliance gap.** Two mandatory campus duties, absent from the section.
2. **The timing fact.** 6/19 drafting, 7/13 statute. Nobody erred.
3. **The remedy.** Five amendments, no re-lettering, §55051 untouched.
4. **The two additions**, named by you before anyone finds them.
5. **The record.** The rationale already said these were waiting on the bill.
6. **Separately — the metrics opening.** §78093.2(d)(2) conditions every
   allocation on *"the metrics described in paragraph (1)"*, and paragraph (1)
   describes four goals and supplies no metrics. Someone must define them. The
   Implementation Funding model already quantifies (A) access and (B) completion
   college by college, with (D) satisfied because MAP is the named pilot project.
   **This is a separate conversation from the regulation and should not be folded
   into it** — it needs no rulemaking at all, which is precisely its advantage.
