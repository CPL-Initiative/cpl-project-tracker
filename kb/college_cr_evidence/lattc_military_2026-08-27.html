How a recommendation is scored

Confidence is a ranking heuristic, not a probability, and it is built from three things you can see on every bullet — so you can disagree with the arithmetic rather than with a number.

✓ Peer precedent — 25%

A named California college has already attached this recommendation to a named course of their own. A fact, and the strongest thing in the mix.

⚙ Word fit — 55%

How well the recommendation's wording matches the course title. Each bullet names the word it matched on, so a weak match is obvious at a glance.

How widely it is held — 20%

How many California colleges' students already carry this recommendation. A recommendation on one exhibit at two colleges is a thinner bet than one on 31.

These are keyed to the credit recommendation, never to an MOS or rate. A service member can hold several MOSs, so the recommendation printed on the JST is the awardable unit — and each one lists the JST exhibits that carry it.
