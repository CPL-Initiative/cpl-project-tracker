How to read this

Every proposal rests on two separate signals. They are never blended into one score, because they answer different questions and one of them is a fact while the other is a judgment.

⚙ The recommendation exists

ACE publishes a credit recommendation in this subject, and California students already hold it. This is what makes an articulation possible. It is a proposal — a machine matched words, and faculty decide.

✓ A peer college has used it

A named California college attached that same recommendation to a named course of their own. This is a fact, not a suggestion — and it is the evidence LATTC can point at.

Every proposal shows the word it matched on, so a reviewer can dismiss a bad one in a second. Where nothing in the ACE vocabulary shares a subject word with the course title, the page says so rather than offering the closest thing it found.

These are keyed to the credit recommendation, not to an MOS or rate. A service member can hold several MOSs, so the recommendation printed on the JST is the awardable unit. The exhibits listed under each recommendation are the JST entries that carry it — that is how many different routes reach the same credit.
