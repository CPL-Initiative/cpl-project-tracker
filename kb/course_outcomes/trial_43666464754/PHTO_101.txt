Catalog Description
This course is a historical survey of the evolving nature of photography from the 1800’s to the present. This course examines the role and function of photography and its cultural history, including its relationship to the fine arts, sciences, social sciences, fashion, and mass media. Emphasizes the aesthetic and historical influences on photography, as well as the medium of photography’s effects upon society and culture and as a form of visual communication.

Course Content
Lecture Content

I. Early inventions of photography

A. Origins of photography 1839–1854

B. Photogenic drawings

C. Latent image 

D. Timeline of technical processes

II. Portraits for the masses 

A. Early photography studio 

B. Wet plate collodion 

C. Carte-de-visite and mass production

III. Objects and events 

A. Early recordings 1839-1854 

B. Documenting industrial development 

C. Photography and the sciences: Anthropology and medicine

D. Imaging war in Crimea, America, France, and Prussia 

E. Truth or propaganda

IV. Landscape 

A. As art and document

B. Expeditionary photography

1. Mapping

2. Preserving the wilderness

3. Travel photography

4. Stereography

C. Colonialism

D. Ethnographic studies and global shrinking

V. Art photography

A. Early photography as artistic expression 1850-1945 

B. Esthetics

1. Narrative

2. Allegorical

3. Genre images

C. Delivery 

1. Exhibitions 

2. Clubs and societies 

3. Publications

D. Photographic manipulations as artifice and art

VI. Photography in the Modern Age 1880-1918

A. Naturalism, Photo-Secession, and Pictorialism as art

B. Social documentary: Camera as weapon and social reform

C. Wartime documentary

D. Influences 

1. Science and technology

2. Revolutionary art and Dadaist movement

E. Photos in advertising, design, and journalism

VII. Culture and photography 1945-1975

A. Revolution: human rights in Latin America, Mexico, Africa, and Asia

B. Media influence in postwar America

C. Evolution of photography as an art form in popular culture

D. Influence of technological advances

E. Relationship of photography and politics

VIII. Convergences: 1975-present

A. Photography and social concerns

B. Neutral vision, politics, and new social documentary

C. Feminism and postmodernism 

D. Nature and the body politics

IX. Women and photography: 1850-present 

A. Revisionist history 

B. Photography as profession, pastime, and art

C. Women as photojournalists and documentarians

D. Contributions to the medium

X. Electronic imaging 

A. New tools, delivery, and opportunities

B. Digital images in art, society, medicine, science, news publications, print media, television, and the Internet

C. Questions of veracity, manipulation, ownership, and copyrights

Course Objectives
identify photographic processes using scientific and technical terms.
recognize some of the most significant photographs historically and their impact on society and culture.
identify how photography has functioned in art movements.
assess the role of photography as it has influenced personal, group, and global perceptions of identity.
compare, contrast, and evaluate traditional exhibitions of photography.
evaluate the role of the photographic image in architecture, design, advertising, journalism, and digital compositing.
demonstrate application of critical thinking practices in interpreting and evaluating photography’s historical and contemporary aesthetic and technical developments, practitioners and genres, and its relationship within broader social, cultural and visual arts contexts.
