Catalog Description

This course provides a foundational introduction to communication studies. Students will explore key communication theories, effective public speaking techniques—both informative and persuasive— and the principles of rhetoric. The course equips students to articulate ideas clearly across diverse contexts and audiences. Students will critically evaluate historical and contemporary speeches while practicing ethical communication to enhance awareness of diversity, equity, and inclusion. Per CalGETC standards, this course is taught in English.

Verbal And Nonverbal Communication

Course Objectives

understand and articulate the foundational concepts of communication studies.

identify and utilize effective language techniques in various communication settings.

understand and utilize Aristotle's proofs.

analyze and evaluate various historical and contemporary speeches using rhetorical principles.

organize and deliver informative and persuasive presentations tailored to diverse audiences.

use critical thinking skills to effectively organize material for a presentation.

create and utilize presentation aids.

improve effective use of voice and body to communicate a message and manage apprehension.

find, critically examine - for credibility, accuracy, and relevance- and use supporting materials from primary and secondary sources in presentations.

exhibit awareness of diversity, equity, and inclusion in communication practices.

recognize personal biases and prejudices when communicating with others.

evaluate effectiveness of verbal and nonverbal communication of self and others.

critically listen, evaluate, and critique information shared by others.

provide constructive criticism to peers while practicing critical listening.

Assignments
Sample Assignment(s)
Assignment Text

Presentations in front of a live audience (one to many)

Informative Speech Presentation: Prepare and deliver a speech on a topic of interest, incorporating researched evidence and following organizational guidelines.

Persuasive Speech: Develop and present a persuasive argument on a current issue, utilizing rhetorical techniques and audience analysis.

Formal Writing - Research Paper: Write a research paper analyzing a significant speech, applying rhetorical principles, and evaluating its effectiveness. Use appropriate research and source citations to support your claims. 

Informal Writing - Peer Critique Assignment: Provide written feedback on a peer's speech, focusing on strengths and areas for improvement based on course concepts.

In Class Activities: Mock job interviews, experimental communication skills practice, theory discussions and peer evaluations. 

Formal assignments will be objectively graded using an instructor-designed rubric specific to the assignment.
