Catalog Description
This course is an introduction to the French language, presenting students with the basic skills for vocabulary and grammar recognition and use, as well as stressing pronunciation, oral skills, reading, and writing at the elementary level. Using a communicative style, students practice French grammar and vocabulary, sentence structure, and oral skills [listening and speaking]. This course also includes an introduction to cultural aspects of the French-speaking world. Lecture: 5 hours per week.

Course Content
Lecture Content

Introductory material such as:

The numbers 0 to 60
The French alphabet
The French accents
Greetings and salutations in French; introducing oneself
Days of the week; months; providing dates

Grammar:

Indefinite articles (Articles indefinis) and the gender of nouns
Expressing actions with regular -er verbs
Definite articles (Les articles définis)
The verb être; Negation with ne...pas; après ne...pas
Number agreement in nouns
Qualificative adjectives (Les adjectifs qualificatifs)
The verb avoir
The prepositions à, et, de
Yes and No question formation
The verbs ending in -ir
Interrogative words (Les mots interrogatifs)
Adjective-Noun placement
Verbs ending in -re; the verb faire, the verb aller and the futur proche
Possessive adjectives (Les adjectifs possessifs)
The verbs prendre e boire, préférer
The imperative (L'impératif)
Partitive articles (Les articles partitifs)
The verbs vouloir, pouvoir, devoir
The passé composé tense
Demonstrative adjectives (Les adjectitifs démonstratifs)

Vocabulary:

In the classroom
Identifying and describing people, places and things
Talking about academic subjects, classes at the university
Describing personalities, clothing and colors
Locating people and objects
In the university (Chambres d'ètudiants)
The family and relatives
Identifying rooms in a house
Describing the weather
Talking about foods and drinks; expressing quatity for such; ordering at a restaurant
Telling time
Expressing desires, ability, necessity and obligation
Talking about present and past events

Culture:

Musicians (Profil des musiciens): Louise Attaque, Daniel Lanois, Serge Gainsbourg, Gorges Brasens, Carla Bruni, Amadou et Mariam, Khaled
The Latin Quarter: Quartier latin: Le quartier géneral des étudiants; Quebec
Haute couture; Paris; Café culture in France; Le marché aux puces de la Porte de Clignancourt
Montreal; Nouvelle-Orléans; Samuel de Champlain (1567-1635)
Morocco (Maroc); Giverny et Monet
La cuisine de France, Maroc et Martinique.
Les grandes occasions: la fête des Rois, Pâques, Noël

Assignment Text
Example of an assigned composition topic: Using the vocabulary and grammar from Chapitre 5, introduce your family members. Include a basic physical description, age, and describe their personality. Mention some of the likes and dislikes for your family members. Don't forget to mention pets, if you have them. Your paragraph should have about 10-12 complete sentences in French [about 100 words]. Be mindful of spelling, accents, subject-verb agreement, as well as gender and number agreement. [If you are uncomfortable describing your family, feel free to invent a family so that you can make use of the vocabulary and grammar presented in this chapter.]

Grading rubrics are used to assess students on such categories as grammar and vocabulary, writing skills, reading comprehension, listening and speaking skills, pronunciation, participation and collaboration, self reflection, personal value, cultural awareness, and lifelong learning.
Students are expected to successfully analyze, categorize, compare, differentiate, debate, critically examine, formulate, compose, justify, properly conclude, recommend, self-evaluate, and editorialize.
Cultural awareness: Students are expected to outstandingly recognize, predict, and react to all aspects of cultural diversity covered in this course; reflect on what they have learned; readily share comments, opinions, and personal thoughts on the cultural and/or literary components in this course; and demonstrate an appreciation for the cultural diversity presented by properly recognizing, differentiating, reporting, categorizing, sorting, analyzing, comparing and/or contrasting, predicting, and/or initiating information from texts covered in this course. Finally, students are expected to clearly show how the information learned is significant to them, including articulating their approach to lifelong learning in a clear, well developed, and in-depth fashion.
