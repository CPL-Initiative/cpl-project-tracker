Catalog Description

This course introduces students to visual art and architecture from prehistory to the medieval era with a focus on art from Europe, North Africa, and the near East. The course will further consider global interactions involving these regions.

This course is not open to students who have already successfully taken ART 103.

Course Objectives

Identify, examine, and assess representative works of art and architecture from prehistory to the medieval era employing appropriate art historical terminology.

Analyze works of art and architecture in terms of aesthetic, socio-political, religious, historical, technological, and cultural contexts in which they were created.

Develop critical thinking, information literacy, and problem solving through an engagement with art, architecture, artists, and patrons from prehistory to the medieval era.

Identify subject matter, iconography, materials, media, visual elements, and design as descriptive and expressive components of the visual language of an artwork that communicate the values of their artist and their historical, cultural, or social context.

Recognize and differentiate between the roles of artists, patrons, technology, geography, economy, social organization, culture, and worldview as contributing factors in the production of art in Prehistoric through Medieval periods and styles.

Using appropriate examples of various art media, describe the general character of various civilizations and cultures, while recognizing their unique differences.

Identify the functions of art in various civilizations and stylistic periods. Examine their social and political organization, religion, and philosophies.

Analyze the major period and stylistic changes in terms of their contemporary political, religious, economic, and technological developments.

Recognize the ways that technological innovation has affected how artists use tools, materials, and techniques to produce artworks, while distinguishing between various disciplines of art.

Discuss and evaluate topics associated with the origination of artworks and their attribution.

Recognize and analyze connections between artworks made during the prehistorical to medieval era, and artworks made in current times.

Recognize and discuss the roles that prehistoric to medieval artworks play in current global issues, such as repatriation.

Deconstruct the historiography and methods of display used to represent the artists and cultures in this course.

Assignments
Outside Assignments
Assignment Text

Essay

Compare and contrast traditions of Egyptian figure sculpture to those of the Greeks using examples of artworks from various periods of Greek and Egyptian civilization.
Summarize general differences between figure sculpture traditions
Identify differences in approach towards subject matter, materials, design, and styles of representation.
Describe functions of figure sculpture and their relationships to social organization, religion, and historical/ cultural context.
Describe the iconography and the symbolism of materials and design.
Outside Assignments
Assignment Text

Essay

Describe, analyze, and evaluate the discovery and early study of Minoan and Mycenaean civilization. Using specific artworks from a variety of media as examples, discuss issues involving provenance and attribution, while describing how understandings and theories of function and iconography have changed over time.

In-Class Assignments
Assignment Text

Writing Assignment

Using specific examples of artworks from a variety of artistic media, describe new types of art forms originating in Mesopotamia. Explain how technological and cultural innovation, along with new types of social organization influence the arts.

Student Learning Outcomes

ARTH C110, SLO 1: Applying art historical terminology and methods, identify, summarize, and discuss representative works of art, architecture and media from prehistory to the medieval era.

ARTH C1100, SLO 2 - Analyze artworks as visual languages expressing complex and diversified meanings and experiences dependent upon historical context, cultural values, aesthetics, technological advancements, and descriptions and interpretations produced by art historical investigative approaches.

ARTH C1100, SLO 3 - Develop critical thinking, information literacy and problem solving that will enable them to to explain the historical and social context of artworks from prehistory to the medieval era.
