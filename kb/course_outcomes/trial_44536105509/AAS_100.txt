Catalog Description
This course provides an introduction to the historical formation of Asian America as well as contemporary issues faced by the highly diverse communities that comprise it. As demanded by the interdisciplinary nature of the field of Asian American Studies, this course relies on a range of historical, literary, ethnographic, and visual texts and makes comparisons with other racialized groups in the United States. Topics include issues of race, class, gender, and sexuality, immigration, migration, citizenship, labor, racialization, exclusion, political activism and social movements, family, community-building, war, imperialism, sovereignty, colonialism, transnationalism, culture, and creative expressions.
Student Learning Outcomes
Outcome
Critically analyze the intersection of race and racism as they relate to class, gender, sexuality, religion, spirituality, national origin, immigration status, ability, sovereignty, language, and/or age in cultural productions produced by Asian/Asian American artists.
Analyze and articulate concepts such as race and racism, racialization, ethnicity, equity, ethno-centrism, eurocentrism, white supremacy, self- determination, liberation, decolonization, sovereignty, imperialism, settler colonialism, and anti-racism as analyzed in relation to the theoretical foundations of the history and development of Asian/Asian American Studies.
Apply theory and knowledge produced by Asian/Asian American communities to describe the critical events, histories, cultures, intellectual traditions, contributions, lived-experiences, and social struggles of those groups with a particular emphasis on agency and group-affirmation.
Critically review how struggle, resistance, racial and social justice, solidarity, and liberation, as experienced and enacted by Asian/Asian American are relevant to current and structural issues such as communal, national, international, and transnational politics as, for example, in immigration, reparations, settler-colonialism, multiculturalism, and language policies.
Describe and actively engage with anti-racist and anti-colonial issues and the practices and movements in Asian/Asian American communities to build a just and equitable society.
Examine the political dimensions during the The Asian American Movement (activism) included but not limited to anti-racism, anti-colonial, student activism, and political art.
Specific Course Objectives
Objective
Upon successful completion of the course, the student will be able to:
Identify major debates, key concepts, influential scholars/thinkers, and current issues in the field of Asian/Asian American Studies.
Survey the immigration, settlement, acculturation, and assimilation of Asian Americans in the U.S.
Examine the contributions of Asian/Asian Americans in American society and their changing roles in socio-political and economic arenas.
Analyze and discuss the Asian Americans and contemporary issues.
Evaluate issues taking an intersectional approach considering race, class, sexuality, and gender.
Analyze Asian American histories and contemporary issues within a global context i.e. consider local dynamics as part of the relationship between Asia and the United States as well as with the Americas.
Analyze race relations between Asian, Blacks, Mexicans and Whites in the U.S.
Define and deconstruct the "model minority myth."
Evaluate the Asian American Movement and activists that impacted Asian American civil rights in the U.S.
Examine and distinguish between the various racial terms used with the Pan-ethnic category "Asian."
Gender analyis on women in the Asian/Asian American community.
Identify and analyze the history and contemporary impact of colonialism, imperialism, and racism on the Asian/Asian American community.
Identify and anaylze the impacts of white racism (anti-Asian sentiment) on the Asian/Asian American community in the U.S.
Examine various artistic styles and eras of Asian/Asian American artists and their cultural signifigance.
Analyze the politicized art and poetic works from the Asian American Movement of the 1960-70s.
Define sexuality and examine issues as they pertain to Asian/Asian American communities.

Required Reading

I. Assigned textbooks related to the Asian/Asian American experiences in the U.S. II. Articles from journals and publications III. Onine sources 

 

Suggested Reading

Ronald T. Takaki’s Strangers from a Different Shore: A History of Asian Americans. Updated and revised ed. Boston: Little, Brown, 1998.

Zhou, Min and James V. Gatewood, ed. Contemporary Asian America: A Multidisciplinary Reader. Second ed. New York: NYU Press, 2007.

Required Writing

I. Critical response papers to the readings. II. Brief responses to questions related to primary source documents. III. Short essays that summarize and critique a variety of perspectives and topics related to Asian/Asian American Studies. IV. Reports on field assignments that enhance students understanding of Asian/Asian American Studies, such as Asian/Asian American art exhibits, film series, concerts and/or folk celebrations.

Critical Thinking

I. Assessments and interpretations of historical and contemporary culture of Asians/ Asian Americans in the U.S. II Essays that explain and interpret issues related to the historical development of Asian/ Asian American Studies as a field of study. III. Primary source document analysis IV. Analysis of racial formation, institutional discrimination and violence against  Asians in the U.S. 

Outside Assignments

I. Attendance at a seminar, film presentation and/or museums related to Asians/ Asian Americans experiences in the U.S. II Background readings to prepare for class discussions. III. Response papers IV. Preparation for in-class oral presentations.
