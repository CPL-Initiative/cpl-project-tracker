Catalog Description
This course is the first semester of Spanish. This elementary level course is a study of the Spanish language and Spanish-speaking cultures, with emphasis on the development of communicative skills and basic structures. The focus is on teaching elementary-level language acquisition in a cultural context through listening, speaking, reading and writing. Interacts with authentic language in context. No previous experience in Spanish is required.
Student Learning Outcomes
Outcome
Demonstrate comprehension of elementary-level spoken Spanish relating to daily activities described in the present tense.
Produce a brief narrative in the present tense describing people and activities, demonstrating a command of basic vocabulary and structures in the Spanish language.
Specific Course Objectives
Objective
Upon successful completion of the course, the student will be able to:
Interact in oral exchange at the elementary level and with appropriate pronunciation and intonation. Exchange greetings and salutations, respond to classroom directions, and ask and answer questions.
Communicate orally and in writing to describe a variety of real-life activities using idiomatic expressions and elementary-level structures in the present tense.
Describe personal events in the present and near future, describe ongoing events, tell time, recognize and use numbers, and use adjectives to describe objects and others.
Produce and respond to affirmative, negative and interrogative sentences in writing using appropriate punctuation, spelling and accent marks at the elementary level.
Address others using formal and informal pronouns in the singular and plural. Recognize the variety of pronouns that exist in Spanish and the regions of the Spanish-speaking world where they are used.
Collect and provide information in real-life settings at the elementary level. Communicate preferences, likes and dislikes using spoken and written language.
Master elementary-level grammatical constructions: gender and number agreement, subject-verb agreement, and conjugation of common regular and irregular verbs in the present tense. Distinguish between present tense and past tense verbs.
Demonstrate knowledge of the variety of cultures that exist within the Spanish-speaking world. Understand the unique cultural differences among Spanish-speaking countries. Understand how language and culture reflect values, belief systems, and behavior.

Cultural understanding and appreciation
Introduction to the Spanish-speaking cultures of the world through history, geography, art, music, dance, sports, literature, religion, politics, and cuisine.
Cultural appreciation for the diversity of the Spanish-speaking world. Appreciation for the Spanish language variations that exist globally, as well as locally in southern California and other border cities. Recognition of and value for all of the variations of Spanish spoken throughout the world.

Assignments
Students are expected to research projects and solutions as dictated by specific assignments. Students are expected to spend a minimum of three hours per unit per week in class and on outside assignments, prorated for short-term classes.
Required Reading

Material from the workbook and other assigned readings.

Suggested Reading

Short readings from publications found in the library or the internet. Short readings related to current, cultural, or historical events from community newspapers and other publications. Realia will be used such as restaurant menus, signs, labels, shopping directories, advertisements, classified ads, etc.

Required Writing

Writing assignments may include, but are not limited to: 1) completion of written exercises from textbooks and other assigned material, 2) short answers to questions using vocabulary presented in class, 3) short compositions using grammatical structures and vocabulary, as well as other language materials presented in class.

Critical Thinking

Students become aware and evaluate the intricacies of language rules before they proceed with constructing sentences that are semantically and grammatically correct. When reading or hearing material in the target language, students must first understand the information conveyed, then use individual and personal experiences to reflect on it, and finally produce an oral or written answer.

Outside Assignments

Cultural Activities: Additional work may be assigned in the form of attending campus and community events, concerts, plays, or special performances, which provide experiences in a Spanish-speaking environment, either as field trips or independent projects approved by the instructor.
