Catalog Description
Identity and values, such as the arts, beliefs, and social forms, as expressed in lifestyles. Regional and interdisciplinary approaches will be used to build a dynamic model of American culture and its impact on Americans and the world.
Student Learning Outcomes
Outcome
Critically examine the theories and factors that influence various interpretations of American cultural identity.
Analyze changes in American society and culture, as reflected in artistic styles and forms.
Critically examine the impact of American culture in contemporary global conversations.
Specific Course Objectives
Objective
Upon successful completion of the course, the student will be able to:
Identify theories from various academic disciplines - such as the social sciences, behavioral sciences, or humanities - and apply them to an analysis of American culture and identity.
Students will be able articulate a complex understanding of the factors that influence American cultural identity: including race, gender, sexuality, ability, economic status and citizenship.
Students will analyze histories and narratives of the American past to gain a critical understanding of current issues.
Students will critically engage literature and the arts to analyze the ways in which they reflect changing beliefs, social institutions and identity in America.
Students will investigate and engage in conversations regarding the impact of American values and cultural practices on global communities.
Through a close reading of diverse texts including personal and cultural materials, students will learn how to critically analyze how individuals, groups, and a wide variety of political, economic, and cultural institutions have interacted to shape and give meaning to American Culture and Identity.

Suggested Reading

Selected handouts and links listed on Canvas for this course. Based on professor preference, can include associated research relative to family/community history.

Required Writing

a. Two American culture reviews (5-7 pages each) from the following areas: American literature/ American/visual art/ American music b. Individual family history and individual assessment (3-5 pages) or conversations in American identity.

Critical Thinking

Students learn how to analyze and evaluate cultural texts including literature, film, art, etc. and demonstrate a basic understanding of cultural theory and aesthetics through art reviews, critical conversations, and reflections on self and community.

Students may examine personal cultural materials as a reflection of personal and family identities.

Outside Assignments

1. Reading assignments, writing assignments; 2. Reviews require outside research, reading, and reference compilation. 3. Time capsule report requires outside research
