Catalog Description
A survey course that develops musical listening skills through lectures, discussion, in-class listening to recorded music, and live concert attendance. Stylistic and structural elements, cultural roles of music and musicians, and contributions of technology in Western music are examined through representational works from the earliest notated music to the present.
Student Learning Outcomes
Outcome
Students can articulate two basic elements of style of each of the Baroque, Classic, Romantic, and 20th Century eras.
Specific Course Objectives
Objective
Upon successful completion of the course, the student will be able to:
Compare and contrast the literature of music, its styles, forms, vocabulary and other aspects that contribute to a basic knowledge of music;
Critically analyze the cultural and historic roles played by musicians in history and today, and the role that various technological changes have played in those roles;
Analyze musical pieces of different periods of Western history through listening;
Compare and contrast musical instruments, their sounds, and place in standard ensembles;
Identify through broad historical survey presented in class five or six composers representing the main style periods in Western music;
Apply analytical listening skills while attending a live music concert.

Content in Terms of Specific Body of Knowledge
Non-Western music: India, Japan, the Middle East.
Emphasis on the differences between this music and that of the West including the philosophy, techniques of musical development and instruments.
Medieval music
The monastic chants and organum and their influence upon the beginnings of  Western music.
Instruments and their use along the pilgrimage routes to create the secular song form and the dance.
The rise of the Notre Dame school in Paris.
Renaissance music
The flourishing of polyphony in the Mass, motet, chanson and madrigal under
such composers as Dufay, Josquin des Pres, Palestrina, Victoria, Weelkes and
Andrea Gabrielli.
Baroque music
Venetian music and the growth of the multiple choir and instrumentally accompanied sacred music under the Gabrielis and Monteverdi.
The concerto grosso under Vivaldi.
The Mass, Passion and cantata under J. S. Bach.
The opera and oratorio under Handel.
The suite under Lully.
The further development of the harpsichord and the organ.
 Viennese music
The development of the symphony and the quartet under Haydn, Mozart and
Beethoven.
The song form of Schubert.
The study of sections of the multi-movement forms: the sonata-allegro, theme and variations, minuet and trio and the rondo.
Romantic music
The miniature piano form of Chopin.
Berlioz and the development of the program symphony.
The tone poem of Tschaikowsky.
The music drama of Wagner and the opera of Verdi.
The impressions of Debussy.
20th century music and beyond
Schoenberg and serialism
Stravinsky and polytonality
Pitch, tone and  electronic entities of composers after 1945.
New directions

Assignments
Students are expected to research projects and solutions as dictated by specific assignments. Students are expected to spend a minimum of three hours per unit per week in class and on outside assignments, prorated for short-term classes.
Required Reading
Textbook chapters as assigned; possible additional readings as posted to Blackboard or assigned.
Suggested Reading
Program notes at concerts; reviews of concerts in local newspapers; internet articles and commentary.
Required Writing
1. Three to four concert reports, 3-5 pages in length. 2. Exams contain essay questions requiring the student to discuss historical, structural, stylistic and cultural elements of music.
Critical Thinking
1. Students are asked to analyze music they have not previously heard, and discuss structureal and stylistic similarties/differences to music studied in class. 2. Students are asked to integrate sonic experience with cultural and historical contexts in writing and discussion.
Outside Assignments
1. Must attend 4-6 live concerts during the semester and write reports on them. 2. Must listen to the assigned recordings of musical examples and be tested on them. 3. Must read the text.
