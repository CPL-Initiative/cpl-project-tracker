Catalog Description
A study of the American economic system as it affects the decision-making of the individual as income earner, taxpayer, and voter. Emphasis is on application of the analyses of supply and demand, productivity, wages and the labor force, the money and banking system, the role of government, and domestic and international economic issues.
Student Learning Outcomes
Outcome
The student will draw demand and supply curves, label them correctly and use the graphs to explain fluctuating prices of a commodity - for example gasoline.
The students will be able to explain the role of competition in the American economy.
Specific Course Objectives
Objective
Upon successful completion of the course, the student will be able to:
Illustrate understanding of the relevance of economics in their daily life by analyzing current events. They will clearly delineate the difference between fact and opinion and identify the assumptions upon which conclusions are based.
Identify the types of markets and illustrate how differences in the markets affect their production and consumption patterns.
Use economic theory to explore macroeconomic issues.
Evaluate the cost and benefits of global trade

Critical Thinking

Students learn to reason through difficult decision-making processes using marginal analysis. Students must identify the two to three most salient factors affecting the outcome. Further, students must identify the weight each factor has in their decision-making. The goal is for students to be able to adapt this economic decision-making process to events outside the classroom. As an example, student swill read a news story about an event such as the historically low precipitation in the Rockies and how that will affect agricultural markets. The additional critical thinking step asks students to identify which and how other markets are likely to be impacted.

Outside Assignments

Students are required to work on two to three problem sets that include selections from the end of chapters as well as instructor-designed questions. These questions are to be short form written, analytical, and/or graphical in nature. There will also be short written assignments in response to selected news events. Finally, there are online quizzes the students must take outside of class.
