Catalog Description
This is the introductory journalism course on reporting and newswriting. This course prepares students for future journalism classes and a journalism degree by providing foundational knowledge of journalism content creation. The course covers media law and ethics, AP Style, research, reporting, source cultivation and interviewing. It also introduces students to news and feature writing skills while continually emphasizing the need for verified, fact-based information. Lastly, it explores various job options that require these journalism skills.
Student Learning Outcomes
Outcome
Use and evaluate technologies that enhance the news writing process.
Write a basic, hard-news lead based on proper journalism style.
Explain the role of ethics in their daily work and describe the ethical dimensions of the media's role in society.
Specific Course Objectives
Objective
Upon successful completion of the course, the student will be able to:
Identify and define the principles of journalism.
Demonstrate proficiency in basic journalistic skills through interpretive and accurate news writing and reporting.
In written news accounts, demonstrate the ability to evaluate and organize information.
Distinguish factual statements from judgmental statements and knowledge from opinion.
Effectively evaluate news sources and identify productive newsgathering techniques.
Discover and evaluate outside sources for use in writing editorials for the print media.
Analyze and apply principles of press law and ethics.
Compare and contrast current journalistic theories, methods and techniques.
Use and evaluate technologies that enhance the news writing process.
Learn to write a basic, hard-news lead based on proper journalism Associated Press style.
Conduct research through public documents, web, sources, etc.
Learn effective interviewing techniques.
Learn to write news and feature leads in journalism style.
Learn to write in different journalism forms, including inverted pyramid, martini glass and kabob styles.
Write on deadline.
Write for multiple platforms, web, print, broadcast, PR, etc.

Required Writing
1. Five two-page homework writing assignments, including one editorial suitable for publication. 2. Two fully developed news stories suitable for publication (2-4 pages each). 3. In-class writing of 15-20 news leads and short articles (2-3 paragraphs each). 4. In-class writing of online news/feature stories
Critical Thinking

Research news stories. Conduct interviews and interview industry professionals.

Outside Assignments

Assigned homework and reading in text. Researching and interviewing for stories. Students will complete outside reading, writing and observation assignments and reviewing notes for exams.
