Catalog Description
This course is an overview of the Africana Studies discipline including its social and academic origins, goals and development. Emphasis is placed on providing students with an understanding of the fundamental areas of study within the field and of the interdisciplinary approach to studying the African experience in America and the world. Emphasis is placed on the cultural, theoretical and historical formation of the African diaspora with particular emphasis on the creation of 'Black' and 'Blackness' as cultural and political identities. This course is intended for all students interested in general knowledge of the Black experience.
Student Learning Outcomes
Outcome
Students will analyze and articulate foundational concepts within the Ethnic Studies discipline, including: race and racism, racialization, ethnicity, equity, ethno-centrism, eurocentrism, white supremacy, self-determination, liberation, decolonization, sovereignty, imperialism, settler colonialism, and anti-racism as analyzed in Africana Studies.
Students will apply theory and knowledge produced by African American communities to describe the critical events, histories, cultures, intellectual traditions, contributions, lived-experiences and social struggles of those groups with a particular emphasis on agency and group-affirmation.
Students will critically review how struggle, resistance, racial and social justice, solidarity, and liberation, as experienced and enacted by African Americans, are relevant to current and structural issues such as communal, national, international, and transnational politics as, for example, in migration, reparations, settler-colonialism, multiculturalism, language policies.
Specific Course Objectives
Objective
Upon successful completion of the course, the student will be able to:
Explain the origins and relevance of African American Studies as an academic discipline and analyze the ways in which its interdisciplinary scope has affected the development of topics and trends in the field;
Present a timeline to illustrate the experience of African Americans from the colonial period through the Civil War and Reconstruction, Feminist movements, World Wars, the Civil Rights movement, Black Power movement, the LGTBQ movement, and the struggles and accomplishments of the late twentieth and early twenty-first centuries;
Use historical and current data to assess contemporary issues related to African Americans in politics, economics, business, culture, and academia;
Analyze the history of African American artistic, musical, and literary expressions from its African origins to the present;
Evaluate contemporary academic, political, and intellectual challenges and possibilities facing African American Studies;
Discuss the historical and political conditions underlying African American relations with other ethnic American groups.

Assignments
Students are expected to research projects and solutions as dictated by specific assignments. Students are expected to spend a minimum of three hours per unit per week in class and on outside assignments, prorated for short-term classes.
Required Reading
The course requires a selection of multidisciplinary texts from sciences and humanities. Primary texts may be selected at the discretion of the instructors and may include historical, literary, sociological and anthropological texts among others. The Introduction to African American Studies course applies an interdisciplinary approach that provides students with the opportunity to study extensively the history and culture of African Americans and their national and global impact. Through critical thinking and the development of analytical skills students develop an appreciation for the contributions African Americans have made to the development of the U.S. and the current world.

Required Writing

1. Write responses to survey topics to analyze learning objectives. 2. Optional, one research paper, a minimum length of seven pages. 3. Develop essay assignments based on readings. 4. Write one to two page reactions papers examining comprehensive aspects of culture and history. 5. Write a summary of the major points on readings.

Critical Thinking
1. Develop critical thinking and problem solving skills. 2. Develop the ability to apply skills in other academic areas. 3. Demonstrate an understanding community planning, policy and programs development. 4. Acquire research skills and information literacy. 5. Demonstrate an understanding of African American history and culture. 6. Demonstrate understanding of a global perspective and an appreciation of diversity. 7. Develop effective writing and research skills. 8. Analyze historical and cultural periods of development. 9. Analyze the unique aspects of the religious and cultural heritage of the African Americans. 10. Assess the impact of the slave trade in the colonial period.
Outside Assignments

1. Attend field trips related to social, historical and cultural events that promote greater understanding. 2. Participate and assist in forums on topics related to contributions of African American culture and history. 3. Journal writing on key periods.
