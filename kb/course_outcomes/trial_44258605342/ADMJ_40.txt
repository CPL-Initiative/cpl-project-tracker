Catalog Description:
ADMJ 40 - Juvenile Procedures
3.00 Units
This course is an examination of the origin, development, and organization of the juvenile justice system as it evolved in the U.S. justice system. The course explores the theories that focus on juvenile law, courts and processes, and the constitutional protections extended to juveniles in the U.S. justice system. It also examines recent case law and alternate methods and programs to assist juveniles.
Strongly Recommended: ADMJ 50.

Measurable Objectives:
Upon completion of this course, the student should be able to:
critically analyze and describe the juvenile justice system and its context in the criminal justice system;
apply California laws pertaining to juvenile delinquency and dependency to case studies;
critically distinguish between delinquency, status offenses, and dependency;
critically analyze the distinctions between adult and juvenile justice systems;
critically evaluate constitutional protections extended to juveniles through judicial decisions;
critically appraise and analyze juvenile court dispositions.
Course Content:
History of the juvenile justice system
Theories of juvenile delinquency
Measuring juvenile delinquency - UCR and/or victimization surveys
Juvenile victims
Juvenile offenders
California laws related to juvenile delinquency and dependency to case studies
Law enforcement and juveniles
Constitutional protections afforded juveniles through judicial decisions
Pretrial services and diversion
Juvenile court procedures versus adult court procedures
Juvenile court dispositions
Juvenile corrections
Preventing delinquency and recidivism
Emerging trends in juvenile justice
