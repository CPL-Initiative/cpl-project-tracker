Catalog Description:
CHIN 1A - Beginning Chinese
5.00 Units
Introduction to the Chinese cultures of the world featuring the study and practice of the four language skills (listening, speaking, reading, and writing) of Mandarin Chinese. Following an immersion instruction format, the class is entirely taught in the target world language of the selected course.
Strongly Recommended: ENGL C1000 (Formerly ENGL 1)

Measurable Objectives:
Upon completion of this course, the student should be able to:
compare and contrast Chinese and American cultural practices in relation to speech, social attitudes, and values;
demonstrate an understanding and appreciation of the culture of Chinese-speaking people, including formative historical events and relevant geographical information;
demonstrate understanding of the basic principles of writing the simplified version of characters as practiced in mainland China;
demonstrate proficiency in using the Hanyu pinyin phonetic system to spell and pronounce Chinese words;
demonstrate an understanding of the four-tone system of Mandarin;
demonstrate the ability to comprehend a short conversation composed chiefly of the vocabulary and grammar studied;
apply the sentence patterns and grammar structures presented in the course to create new sentences in culturally appropriate ways both orally and in writing;
conduct a simple, culturally sensitive conversation in Mandarin on topics studied, using words and expressions in structurally correct sentences;
write a coherent and culturally appropriate paragraph of fifty characters or more on a topic studied and/or discussed in class;
make simple descriptions related to size, shape, and color orally and in writing.
Course Content:

Course Content (Lecture):



Introduction to the history and geography of Chinese-speaking countries and cities of the world
Social and cultural information necessary to be able to understand important differences between Chinese and American cultural practices
Basic principles of writing simplified characters practiced on mainland China
Introduction to the Hanyu pinyin system of spelling and pronouncing Chinese characters
Fundamentals of Mandarin phonetics, including the four tones
Functional vocabulary, grammar, and writing to allow students to:
identify themselves in the target language (name, age, nationality, place of birth, profession or occupation) orally and in writing
order food and drink in restaurants, buy food in shops and markets, read menus, and write shopping lists
greet others, make introductions, take leave, thank, and write simple culturally appropriate expressions of thanks
be able to express spatial relationships (far, near, right, left, etc.) orally and in writing
be able to ask for, read, understand, and write simple street directions
identify clock time, the date, and the weather, and be able to read and write them as well
count, understand, and write numbers 
use vocabulary related to weights and measures orally and in writing
make descriptions related to size, shape, and color orally and in writing
Syntax and grammar that allow students to:
identify correct word order in a Chinese sentence
read, enunciate, and write simple descriptive sentences (Wo lei./Ta hen mang)
read, enunciate, and write ascriptive sentences using the verbs “shì” and “xíng “
read, enunciate, and write verbs-of-action to describe habit or action (Ta kàn bào./Wo bùhé chà.)
read, enunciate, and write verbs-of-existence (zhù, zài, zhùzài) to indicate location of subject
use adverbs of time in proper sentence position orally and in writing
combine verbs-of-motion and verbs-of-action to describe motion, destination, and purpose (Wo qù shangdìan mai bi) orally and in writing
ask grammatically affirmative and negative questions orally and in writing
create sentences with “you” and its negative form “méiyou” to indicate possession or existence and their negative equivalents orally and in writing
use the verb “zài” to indicate the location of an event orally and in writing
use the verb “gei” to indicate favor or offering orally and in writing
use expressions of quantity and measure orally and in writing
use  “de” to indicate ownership, condition, or color orally and in writing
use the “co-verb” for the verb of motion “lái/qù” to describe movement orally and in writing

Course Content (Laboratory):

Activate lecture content using interactive audio and audiovisual programs on CDs, DVDs, CD ROMS, target language websites, etc., featuring culturally authentic and contextual guided speaking, reading, and writing activities such as cued repetition of native speech, dictations, cued oral responses, listening comprehension, and interactive realia (culturally authentic texts).
Organized laboratory activities including conversation groups.
Laboratory Assignment:  Hanyu Pinyin drill: 
Practice pronunciation of the symbols for 23 consonants, 6 single vowels, and 18 compound vowels
Practice intonation of the different tone marks 
Practice syllables formed by blending a consonant and a vowel or a compound vowel
Practice the exceptions to standard rules for Hanyu Pinyin.
Methods of Instruction:
Choral/individual repetition of model speech
Distance Education
Lecture/discussion and question/answer in target language
Re-creation of dialogues and improvisation
Small group activities leading to skits, dialogues, etc.
Assignments and Methods of Evaluating Student Progress:
1. Typical Assignments
Write a brief paragraph that includes biographical information, such as nationality, place of birth, birthday, and current residence
Practice writing the characters learned in a given class
Demonstrate greeting skills in a skit taking place in an airport, train station, etc.
Laboratory Assignment: Make a recording of the poem “Thoughts on a Still Night” by Li Bai.
2. Methods of Evaluating Student Progress
Tests, quizzes, and interviews to evaluate the four language skills and cultural sensitivity in relation to material presented
Student participation in class activities
Graded homework assignments
Recordings from the language laboratory to evaluate pronunciation skills
Final Examination
3. Student Learning Outcomes
Upon the completion of this course, the student should be able to:
Demonstrate orally, proficiency in the use of the grammatical structures presented and vocabulary assigned,
Demonstrate in writing, proficiency in the use of grammatical structures presented and vocabulary assigned
Demonstrate an appreciation of the culture, including formative historical events and relevant geographical information.
