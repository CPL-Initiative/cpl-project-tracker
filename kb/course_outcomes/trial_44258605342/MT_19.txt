Measurable Objectives:
Upon completion of this course, the student should be able to:
demonstrate advanced skills of tennis;
demonstrate increased level of physical conditioning from beginning of semester in a progressive sequence;
demonstrate an advanced level of play;
demonstrate the roles of leadership, team play, sportsmanship and other social values related to team competition.
demonstrate proper sprint mechanics while on the tennis court;
demonstrate in written form how to formulate and set a seasonal goal;
correctly spot teammates in the weight room to prevent injury and overuse while weight training;
demonstrate the difference between plyometric training and strength training;
demonstrate concentric training methods to maximize muscle fiber recruitment;
demonstrate eccentric training to strengthen tendons and ligaments to prevent overuse injuries.
Course Content:
Rules utilized for California Community College men's tennis competition.
Practice and performance of individual skills, such as the serve and advanced strokes.
Development, execution, and evaluation of singles and doubles strategies.
Physical conditioning specific to the demands of tennis.
Performance enhancing strategies (goalsetting, concentration techniques, etc.).
Intercollegiate competition.
Explanation and demonstration of the different tools that provide different training stimulus.
Explanation and demonstration of maximum load method- resulting in higher muscle fiber recruitment.
Explanation and demonstration of slow contraction velocity repetitions with full recovery (longer rest periods).

Assignments and Methods of Evaluating Student Progress:
1. Typical Assignments
Analyze the strengths and weaknesses of an opponent, and apply the proper strategy for successful match play.
Review video tape of your serve and evaluate your technique. Determine two components of your serve mechanics that you could practice to improve your serve performance.
Read the chapter on goal setting. Be prepared to discuss how to effectively set individual practice goals.
Participation in intercollegiate contests
Read the chapter about advanced singles strategy. Be prepared to demonstrate tactics from the back court and approaching the net.
2. Methods of Evaluating Student Progress
Improved performance
Class Participation
Teamwork
Final Examination
3. Student Learning Outcomes
Upon the completion of this course, the student should be able to:
Demonstrate improved fitness, and overall wellness, by participating in a consistent training program
Work within a group, or team, to solve problems
Demonstrate and explain the physiological reasons for performing a proper warm up.
