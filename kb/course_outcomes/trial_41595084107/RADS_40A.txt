Admin Outline for Radiation Safety 40A
Radiation Safety
Effective: Fall 2018
Catalog Description:
RADS 40A - Radiation Safety
2.00 Units
A course designed to provide basic radiation safety instruction. Includes identification of the sources of radiation and radioactive materials, the nature of ionization radiation, biological effects, and risk assessment.
2 Units Lecture

Student Learning Outcomes
Upon the completion of this course, the student should be able to:
Identify the basic principles of atomic energy, radioactivity, and decay.
Assess hazards associated with the use of ionizing radiation.
Determine biological effects and risks from radiation exposure.
Course Objectives:
Upon completion of this course, the student should be able to:
Identify the basic principles of atomic energy, radioactivity, and decay
Assess hazards associated with the use of ionizing radiation
Determine biological effects and risks from radiation exposure
Estimate dose and risks to individuals and populations
Define monitoring for the workplace
Identify sources of radiation, including artificial and natural sources

Typical Outside-of-Class Assignments
Other:
Problem solving
Using a decay equation, solve the following problem:
If a sample has 1000 atoms of Tritium (H-3) on 9/17/2017, how many H-3 atoms will there be on 9/17/2021?  The half-life of H-3 is 12.3 years.
What is the primary type of interaction of a 0.2 MeV photon in Lead?
How about a 2 MeV photon?
How about 9 MeV photons?
If 250,000 people are exposed to 5 rem of radiation;
How many person-rem would that be?
If there are 8 excess cancer fatalities for every 10,000 person-rem, how many cancers above the natural occurrence would you expect?
Assuming a 20% natural cancer mortality, how many natural cancer deaths?

Rationale for DE
Explain why this course should be offered in Distance Education mode.
This course is only offered every 2 years; there needs to be a way to offer the course when face to face instruction is not possible to ensure student completion of the program is not delayed. Online offerings will increase enrollment in the OSH program.
Explain how the decision was made to offer this course in a Distance Education mode.
The decision was made in consultation with the Dean of the STEM program (Nan Ho) and through student feedback.
