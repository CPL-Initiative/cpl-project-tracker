Catalog Description
This entry level course is designed to introduce students to American Sign Language (ASL) and fingerspelling as it is used within American Deaf culture. Instruction includes preparation for visual/gestural communication followed by intensive work on comprehension through receptive language skills, development of basic conversational skills, modeling of grammatical structures, and general information about American Deaf culture. ASL 110 is equivalent to two years of high school ASL. Students are required to attend at least one off-campus event.

Student Learning Outcomes
At the conclusion of this course, the student should be able to:
Students will utilize American Sign Language to express and receive information about a variety of topics at a beginner level.
Students will demonstrate accuracy and proficiency in the use of learned structures and vocabulary.
Students will differentiate the unique cultural aspects of American Deaf culture from the unique cultural aspects of other cultures.
Course Objectives
At the conclusion of this course, the student should be able to:
Integrate American Sign Language (ASL) vocabulary, grammar and culture to introduce self and others, exchange personal information, talk and ask about surroundings (spatial agreement), telling and asking where people live (signer's perspective), converse about family, express opinions, talk about past, present and future activities.
Identify and apply the phonology of ASL to each sign by using the five parameters (handshape, palm orientation, location, movement, and non-manual behaviors) accurately and appropriately. 
Identify and analyze similarities and differences in the American Deaf and hearing cultures.     
Comprehend and generate short story narratives and conversations utilizing all elements of ASL grammar and phonology accurately.
Utilize American Sign Language grammar correctly to communicate a variety of activities and real world experiences.
Course Content
Course Lecture Content
Introducing Oneself and Others Utilizing Grammatically Correct American Sign Language (ASL).  Introduction to ASL, Introductions to the American Deaf Community
Language Functions: asking for/giving names; confirming information; correcting information; negative head shake
Grammatical Functions: yes/no questions; wh-questions: 'what'; personal pronouns; spatial referencing; numbers: 1 - 10
ASL Manual Alphabet A - Z
Exchanging Personal Information:  Forming questions, Establishing hand dominance
Language Functions: asking if deaf or hearing; asking where learning sign language; responding to information
Grammar: agent marker; yes/no questions; wh-questions: 'where'; negation: 'not'
Talking About Surroundings:  Spatial agreement
Language Functions: asking/telling where; expressing wants; correcting information: "wave-NO"
Grammar: real-world orientation; non-manual behaviors-'cs', 'far-away'; numbers: 11-15
Telling Where You Live:  When to use fingerspelling
Language Functions: asking/telling where; asking/telling how person comes to class
Grammar: wh-questions: 'where', 'how'; real-world orientation; noun-verb pairs; numbers: 16 - 20
Talking About Your Family:  contrastive structure, forming negative statements
Language Functions: asking/telling about marital status; asking/telling if have children/siblings; telling how many
Grammar: possessive pronouns; yes/no questions; negative responses: 'no', 'not', 'none'; contrastive structure; numbers: 21-25
  Telling About Activities:  Time concepts, Keeping each other informed
Language Function: apologizing; giving reasons and opinions; suggesting activities
Grammar: time signs; wh-questions: 'to-do'; dual pronouns; numbers: 26-30, multiples of 5 (to 100)
American Deaf Culture: Identify and analyze similarities and differences in American hearing and Deaf cultures in a variety of social situations. 
Course Lab Content
