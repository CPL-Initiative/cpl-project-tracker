Catalog Description
This course will provide students with an introduction to disorders of communication, including classification, prevalence, assessment and remediation of speech, language, swallowing, and hearing disorders in children and adults. The role of the speech-language pathologist and audiologist in educational and medical settings will also be presented.

Student Learning Outcomes
At the conclusion of this course, the student should be able to:
Demonstrate knowledge of the basic terminology related to the field of communicative disorders.
Demonstrate knowledge of the basic principles of evaluating and treating individuals with communication disorders.
Course Objectives
At the conclusion of this course, the student should be able to:
Identify and describe the anatomy and physiology of the speech and voice mechanism.
Describe the roles, responsibilities, and work settings of speech-language pathologists.
Define and explain the basic terminology related to human communication disorders.
Identify and classify various types of communication disorders.
Discuss the basic principles of human speech, language, and hearing development across the life span.
Discuss professional aspects of speech-language pathology including professional ethics, legal guidelines, certification, and licensure requirements. 
Discuss the impact of communication disorders on peoples' lives. 
Describe and discuss the basic principles of assessment and remediation of various types of communication disorders.
Course Content
Course Lecture Content

I.  Overview of human communication
 A.  The role of culture and environment in communication
 B.  Definition and classification of communication disorders according to etiology and type
 C.  Prevalence of communication disorders
 D.  Historical perspective on communication disorders
 E.  Major provisions of federal legislation affecting people with communication disabilities
 F.  Team of professionals serving individuals with communication disabilities
      1. Credentials for speech-language pathologists and audiologists
      2. Related professionals, including educators, psychologists, social workers, therapists and medical specialists
 G. Professional organizations
     1.  American Speech-Language-Hearing Association (ASHA)
     2.  California Speech-Language-Hearing Association (CSHA)
     3.  Maintenance of ethical standards and the American Speech-Language-Hearing Association Code of Ethics

II.  Anatomy and physiology of the speech and voice mechanism
 A.  The respiratory system and its structural framework and musculature 
 B.  The pulmonary system 
 C.  The laryngeal system, musculature and function 
 D.  The articulatory/resonating system 
 E.  The speech production process

III.  Normal speech and language acquisition
 A.  Developmental stages of speech and language
 B.  Mastery of language form and English speech sounds
 C.  Major difficulties which may contribute to communication disorders
 D.  Changes in language experienced by adults

IV.  Assessment
 A.  The communication continuum of differences, dialects and disorders
 B.  Purposes and procedures involved in the assessment of communication disorders 
     1.  Referrals
     2.  Screening
     3.  Case history
     4.  Opening interview
     5.  Systematic observation
     6.  Hearing screening
     7.  Oral-facial examination
     8.  Formal testing 
   a.  Norm referenced instruments 
   b.  Criterion referenced instruments 
   c.  Validity and reliability
  9.  Speech/language sampling
  10.  Informal testing 
 C.  Goals of assessment
  1.  Determination if a communication problem exists
  2.  Identification of deficit areas and consistency of problem
  3.  Areas of strength
  4.  Severity of problem
  5.  Etiology - probable causes of the problem
  6.  Recommendations
  7.  Prognosis
 D.  Consolidation of findings
  1.  Closing interview
  2.  Report writing and development of the Individual Educational Plan for the educational setting

V. Treatment of communication disorders
 A.  Objectives of intervention
  1.  Generalization of improvement in the real-world environment
  2.  Automatic learning
  3.  Self-monitoring
  4.  Sensitivity to the personal and cultural characteristics of the client
 B.  Target selection building on client needs
 C.  Generalization
 D.  Trial therapy
 E.  Age appropriateness
 F.  Baseline data
 G.  Behavioral objectives: stimulus, response and reinforcement principles in changing behavior 
 H.  Incidental teaching approach
 I.  Collaborative model of intervention
 J.  Family and environmental involvement
 K.  Measuring effectiveness
  1.  Post therapy testing
  2.  Generalizing
 L.  Follow-up and maintenance

VI.  Counseling
 A.  Empathize
 B.  Be focused
 C.  Keep it simple
 D.  Strive for cultural sensitivity
 E.  Summarize
 F.  Listen carefully and repeat
 G.  Avoid irrelevant revelations
 H.  Be in charge

VII.  Childhood language impairments
 A.  Categorization and definition of language disorders including causes and characteristics
  1.  Mental retardation/developmental disabilities
  2.  Learning disabilities and attention deficit disorder
  3.  Specific language impairment
  4.  Autism and pervasive developmental disorder
  5.  Brain injury and aphasia
  6.  Early expressive language delay
  7.  Neglect and abuse
  8.  Disorders related to psychiatric conditions
  9.  Central auditory processing disorders
 B.  Assessment
  1.  Characteristics of common language tests
  2.  Language sampling
 C.  Intervention
  1.  Target selection and sequence of training
  2.  Good teaching behaviors
   a.  Modeling the desired behavior for the client
   b.  Types of cueing to elicit desired client behavior 
   c.  Appropriate reinforcement and/or corrective feedback.
   d.  Generalization of the learned behavior to the everyday environment of the client

VIII.  Acquired Adult Language Disorders
 A.  Aphasia
  1.  Accompanying deficits
  2.  Types of aphasia
  3.  Assessment
  4.  Intervention
 B.  Right Hemisphere Damage
  1.  Characteristics
  2.  Assessment
  3. Intervention
 C.  Traumatic Brain Injury (TBI)
  1.  Characteristics
  2.  Assessment
  3.  Intervention
 D. Cognitive Impairment/ Dementia
  1.  Assessment
  2.  Intervention

IX.  Fluency disorders
 A.  Characteristics of stuttered and fluent speech
 B.  Onset and development of stuttering
 C.  Theory and conceptualizations of stuttering
 D.  Stuttering treatment with children
 E.  Stuttering treatment with adults 

X.  Voice and Resonance disorders
 A.  Changes in voice production over the lifespan
 B.  Disorders of voice
  1.  Disorders of vocal pitch, loudness and quality
  2.  Non-phonatory vocal disorders which include consistent and episodic aphonia
  3.  Voice disorders associated with vocal misuse or abuse
   a.  Vocal nodules
   b.  Vocal polyps
   c.  Acute and chronic laryngitis
  4.  Voice disorders associated with medical or physical conditions: Parkinsonism, vocal fold paralysis, pseudobulbar palsy, Huntington's chorea, spastic dysarthria, laryngeal papillomas, laryngeal cancer
  5.  Voice disorders associated with psychological or stress conditions
  6. Voice evaluation and treatment
    a. Examination by an otolaryngologist is always the first treatment step
    b. Case history
    c. Perceptual evaluation of pitch, loudness and vocal quality
    d. Treatment of voice disorders associated with medical conditions
    e. Treatment of voice disorders associated with psychological or stress conditions

XI.  Speech Sound Disorders
 A.  Understanding speech sounds
  1.  Classification of consonants by place and manner
  2.  Classification of vowels by tongue height, lip roundness and tension
  3.  Distinctive Feature Analysis
  4.  Phonology and articulation
  5.  Articulation impairments are disorders of production
     a.  Substitutions
     b.  Omissions
     c.  Distortions
     d.  Additions
    3.  Phonemic error inventory
 B.  Intervention
  1.  Therapy approaches - single phoneme target
   a.  Perceptual or ear training
    1.  Identification of a target sound
    2.  Location of target sounds
    3.  Stimulation
    4.  Discrimination
   b.  Production training
   c.  Stabilization
   d.  Correction
  2.  Therapy approaches - phonological process targets
   a.  Stimulability
   b.  Auditory bombardment
  3.  Oral-motor therapy
  4.  Sensory-motor training
  5.  Minimal pairs
  6.  Generalization and maintenance

XII.  Cleft lip and palate
 A.  Embryological development of the face and palate
 B.  Description of how clefts of the lip and palate form
 C.  Clinical features of clefts
  1.  Clinical features of clefts of the lip
  2.  Unilateral cleft of the lip and palate
  3.  Clinical features of a bilateral cleft of the lip and palate
 D.  Etiologies of clefts
 E.  Incidence of clefts
 F.  The cleft palate team and management issues
  1.  Surgical management of clefts
  2.  Dental management of clefts
  3.  Audiological management of clefts
  4.  Psychological management of clefts
 G.  Communication problems associated with clefts
 H.  Treatment of voice, resonance and articulation disorders associated with clefts

XIII.  Motor speech disorders
 A.  Processes of motor speech and causes of neurogenic speech disorders
 B.  Types of cerebral palsy and dysarthrias
  1.  Spastic cerebral palsy
  2.  Athetoid cerebral palsy
  3.  Ataxic cerebral palsy
  4.  Flaccid dysarthria
  5.  Spastic dysarthria
  6.  Ataxic dysarthria
  7.  Hyperkinetic dysarthria
  8.  Hypokinetic dysarthria
  9.  Mixed dysarthria
 C.  Assessment techniques used with neurogenic motor speech disorders
 D.  Intervention principles for acquired motor speech disorders
 E.  Apraxia of speech
  1.  Etiology
  2.  Assessment
  3.  Intervention

XIV.  Disorders of swallowing
 A.  The swallowing process
  1.  Anticipatory phase
  2.  Oral phase
  3.  Pharyngeal phase
  4.  Esophageal phase
 B.  Disordered swallowing in each phase 
 C.  Causes and correlations of swallowing disorders
  1.  Pediatric dysphagia
  2.  Dysphagia in adults
 D.  Evaluation for swallowing
  1.  Screening for dysphagia 
  2.  Case history and background information regarding dysphagia
  3.  Clinical assessment
  4.  The swallowing examination
  5.  Instrumentation
 E.  Dysphagia intervention and treatment

XV.  Audiology and hearing loss
 A.  The fundamentals of sound
 B.  The anatomy of the ear
  1.  The outer ear
  2.  The middle ear
  3.  The inner ear
 C.  Types of hearing loss
  1.  Conductive hearing loss
  2.  Sensorineural hearing loss
  3.  Mixed hearing loss
 D.  Central auditory processing disorders
 E.  Degrees of hearing loss
 F.  Deafness and deaf culture
 G.  Audiological testing procedures
  1.  Testing infants and children
  2.  Testing adults
 H.  Aural habilitation/rehabilitation
  1.  Amplification
   a.  Personal hearing aids 
   b.  Cochlear implants
   c.  FM assistive listening devices
  2.  Auditory training
  3.  Aural/oral and manual communication methods

XVI. Augmentative and Alternative Communication 
  A. Definition of AAC
  B. Types of AAC
  C. Dispelling Myths regarding AAC 
  D. Assessment considerations
  E. Intervention 
  F. Advocacy 

Future of the profession
 A.  The need for speech-language pathologists and speech-language pathology assistants in the future
 B.  The importance of the role of research in the profession
