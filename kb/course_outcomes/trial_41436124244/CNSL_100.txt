Catalog Description
Integrates concepts of lifelong understanding pertaining to career choice, educational planning, and self inventory. Skills, values, and interest assessments are utilized. Emphasis is on applying psychological principles to values clarification, goal setting, and decision making. Students analyze social/cultural conditioning and explore successful strategies for living in a diverse society.

Course Content
Course Lecture Content

Students will analyze factors that impact student success, such as self identity and cultural awareness, academic habits, and the community college system and resources. Students will synthesize and integrate information on identity, values, interests, and skills in order to clarify their own personal journey in college.  Students will be able to distinguish between traditional and non-traditional career choices as well as contrast the differences between degree options and requirements in order to select a career best suited for their individual needs. 

1. Self Identity & Cultural Awareness 

Analyze theory and concepts of self identity and cultural awareness 
Acquire understanding of cultural diversity via tools of implicit bias 

2. Role of a Community College Student & Academic Habits 

Acquire an understanding of the role of community college students
Analyze theory and concepts of academic habits 
Acquire an understanding of how to navigate a college campus and access programs and resources 

3. Health & Wellness 

Acquire an understanding of how to navigate stressful situations and on campus resources for mental health support 
Acquire an understanding of the importance of sleep and selecting nutrient-dense foods

4. Career Development  

Analyze theory and concepts of career and life planning.

5. Personal Exploration

Acquire the concept of interest, skills, and values, as they relate to human growth and life-stage development.

6. Career Exploration 

Foster awareness of diversity and relate it to psychological, sociological, and physiological forces within the workplace.

Standards of Achievement
How will learning be assessed? (Multiple measures must be used)

Class discussion and small group process emphasize the ability to identify, assess, analyze, compare, and process a variety of information regarding the student’s individual career goals, options, skills, etc. 

Written assignments and research projects that identify the student’s abilities, assess, analyze, compare, and process a variety of information regarding the student’s individual skills, interests, personalities, and values as they relate to educational and career requirements. 

Examinations (Written essay and objective) require students to analyze, define, clarify, explain, and compare valid conclusions regarding the role of the community college student and success strategies based on reflection of identity and cultural diversity. 

Class activities and presentations, written assignments, and examinations (Written essay and Objective)

Written textbook assignments include analysis of personal learning style, knowledge of goal-setting techniques, learning the elements of critical thinking, self-motivation techniques, and utilizing personal and campus resources.
Learning the elements of effective group study techniques, choosing a group, and working with others on materials from the linked classes. Participation will require students to analyze class materials, decide on the most appropriate manner to learn the material, formulate plans to learn that material, and assess the effectiveness of the group techniques utilized to study.
Learning skills assignments that highlight the link with other college classes. Students will demonstrate competency in learning skills by utilizing them in the linked class. Skills to be learned include test preparation, techniques for taking objective and essay examinations, note-taking, time management, goal-setting, and textbook study.
Examination (written essay and objective) requires students to define, analyze and demonstrate their knowledge of the course material.
