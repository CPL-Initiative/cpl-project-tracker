Catalog Description
Overview of the SAC Pharmacy Technician training program. Definition of the roles and preview of the opportunities open to pharmacy technicians in various practice settings. Presentation of pharmaceutical dosage forms, the drug development process, and drug classification systems. Introduction to prescription labeling and to the law and ethics of pharmacy practice.

Student Learning Outcomes
At the conclusion of this course, the student should be able to:
Describe the duties and roles of the pharmacy technician in various practice settings.
Identify the skills needed to successfully complete the Pharmacy Technology program at SAC.
Summarize the license and certification process for pharmacy technicians.
Differentiate drugs classified as OTC, Rx, (federal legend) C1, C2, C3, C4, C5.
Identify the steps involved in new drug development.
Discuss the characteristics of common drug dosage forms.
Memorize the 100 common trade-generic equivalents and indications.
Interpret prescriptions, memorize prescription sig codes, and prepare prescription labels.
Course Objectives
At the conclusion of this course, the student should be able to:
Memorize the top 100 common trade-generic medication names.
Demonstrate an understanding of the characteristics of common dosage forms.
Explain all the steps involved in new drug development.
Understand the system of drug classification.
Demonstrate the ability to read prescriptions and prepare prescription labels.
Explain in depth which skills are needed to successfully complete the Pharmacy Technology program.
Analyze and discuss the duties and roles of the pharmacy technician in various practice settings.
Course Content
Course Lecture Content

A.  COURSE AND PROGRAM INTRODUCTION 
     1. Course content
     2. Expectations
     3. Class organization
     4. Introduction to the trade-generic equivalents list

B.  DEFINITION OF THE PHARMACY TECHNICIAN’S ROLE 
     1.  The responsibilities inherent in the job of a pharmacy technician in
           a. Inpatient
           b. Sterile products
           c. Home health
           d. Ambulatory care
           e. Specialty pharmacy settings
           f. How the role of the pharmacy technician has evolved
           g. The current and future role of the pharmacy technician in providing pharmaceutical care  
           h. Requirements to obtain a pharmacy technician license and certification
           i. Job opportunities available for pharmacy technicians
 
C.  PHARMACY TECHNICIAN TRAINING 
     1.  The certificate and degree options available at SAC, including course sequencing and recommended electives
     2.  How the SAC Pharmacy Technology courses relate to the Board of Pharmacy licensing requirements
     3.  ASHP outcome competencies for pharmacy technicians
     4.  Differences between the Pharmacy Technology curriculum and pharmacy school prerequisites 

D.  SKILLS AND CHARACTERISTICS 
     1. The types of knowledge, technical skills, physical abilities
     2. Personal characteristics necessary for a successful career as a pharmacy technician

E.  ETHICS 

The basic rights of the patient as they relate to medication delivery and administration
The ethics involved in confidentiality (HIPAA), access to health care, and right-to-life issues
Consequences of unethical and illegal activities, including drug diversion and substance abuse 


F.  INTRODUCTION TO OUTPATIENT PRESCRIPTIONS 
     1. The legal requirements for prescriptions, including what prescription orders must specify and who can legally prescribe medications. 
     2. Definition of common prescription abbreviations
     3. Preparation of prescription labels, using accepted standard format

G.  DRUG RESEARCH AND DEVELOPMENT 
     1. Definition of a drug; sources of new medications
     2. Phases of the research and development process for new drugs
     3. The FDA criteria for drug approval; FDA classification of Rx and OTC medications  
     4. DEA classification system for controlled substances

H.  PHARMACEUTICAL DOSAGE FORMS 
     1. Differences in characteristics and uses of various solid and liquid dosage forms
     2. Traditional and newer drug delivery systems and methods of administration
