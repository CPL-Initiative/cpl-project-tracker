NURS 1: Nursing Science and Practice Concepts I
9.5 Units / LEC-LAB

An introduction to basic nursing skills and assessment as well as nursing process. Development of decision-making framework using evidence-based practice and patient care concepts demonstrating sound clinical judgment skills at the micro (work unit) and macro (health care facility) systems. Topics include safety; professionalism; communication; culture; functional ability; perfusion; evidence; informatics and technology; fluid and electrolytes; thermoregulation; pain elimination; health promotion.



Note: Admission to the Nursing Program requires passing a background check, a complete physical exam including immunizations and drug screening.

 

Transferability:

Transferable to CSU only

Prerequisites, Corequisites & Advisories

Prerequisite: BIOL2 - Microbiology

Objectives
1. Outline the history of major microbiological discoveries and describe their contributions to world civilization.
2. State Koch's postulates and apply them to different types of pathogens and to new diseases.
3. Relate basic principles of chemistry and cell biology to structure and function of microbes.
4. Explain how the unity of basic cell processes contributes to difficulties in treating infectious disease.
5. Describe the principles and mechanisms of microbial genetics and coevolution and apply them to the problem of increasing drug resistance in microorganisms.
6. Describe viruses and their relation to cells.
7. Compare and evaluate the various mechanisms of control and prevention of microbial disease.
8. Discuss the mechanisms of pathogenicity in microbes.
9. Compare and contrast the epidemiology of community acquired and hospital acquired infections.
10. Describe the functions of the human immune system, its relations to disease, and how vaccination contributes to immunity.
11. Describe the etiology, epidemiology, treatment and prevention of a variety of important infectious diseases.
12. Safely and aseptically perform a variety of microbiological laboratory techniques.
13. Collect and analyze data.

Outcomes
1. Describe the anatomy, physiology and biochemistry of microorganisms and the consequential effects of various environmental factors upon them.
2. Know the causative organism of the more common human diseases and the physiological effect of the drugs most commonly used in the fight against these diseases.
3. Describe the principles and applications of genetic engineering and the role that microorganisms are playing in this process.

Prerequisite: BIOL6 - Human Anatomy

Objectives
1. Identify cellular structures and describe how they contribute to the diverse functions of different cell types (lab and lecture).
2. Identify diverse cell types, and explain how diverse cell types become organized into tissues with unique properties (lab and lecture).
3. Identify diverse tissue types, and explain how diverse tissues become organized into organs with unique properties (lab and lecture).
4. Identify the organs that comprise specific organ systems, and explain how each organ contributes to the function of the organ system as a whole (lab and lecture).
5. Recognize anatomical pathology and explain how alterations of structure at molecular, cellular, and histological levels can lead to organ and organ system pathology (lab and lecture).
6. Diagram the anatomy of afferent and efferent neural pathways, from receptor/effector organs to spinal cord and/or appropriate brain region (lab and lecture).
7. Explain the overall function of each organ system (lecture).
8. Explain the embryological basis for adult structure (lecture).
9. Compare and contrast the cardiovascular system with the lymphatic system, the nervous system with the endocrine system, and the integumentary with the digestive system (lecture).
10. Use anatomical terminology to indicate body regions and structures, and to identify and describe surface features (lab).
11. Identify bones and bony landmarks (lab).
12. Identify skeletal muscle organs and muscle features (lab).
13. Identify peripheral nerves and their receptors or targets (lab).
14. Identify the viscera of the body and be able to describe their relative positions (lab).

Outcomes
1.Describe key structural features of different human cell and major tissue types.
2. Identify and describe the anatomy of the systems of the human body.
3. Relate structure and function at the cellular through system levels of organization of human body systems.
4. Describe structural or anatomical changes that occur in disease, injury or aging of the human body systems.

Prerequisite: BIOL7 - Human Physiology

Objectives
1. Describe and distinguish various roles of major classes of biomolecules in living cells (lecture).
2. Describe key functional features of different types of human cells and how they communicate (lecture).
3. Identify key functions of major organ systems and the physiological mechanisms underlying their operation (lecture).
4. Describe examples of how organ systems of the body are integrated and regulated (lecture).
5. Relate normal metabolic and physiological processes to malfunctions that cause disorders of the major organ systems (lecture).
6. Describe how body systems work together to regulate the homeostasis of different variables of the extracellular fluid (lecture and lab).
7. Investigate cardiac function through hands-on activities (lab).
8. Investigate the functions of the kidneys through hands-on activities (lab).
9. Investigate neural function (including reflexes and sensory systems) through hands-on activities (lab).
10. Demonstrate an understanding of the scientific method, experimental design, and the philosophy and process of science (lab).
11. Analyze experimental data to demonstrate physiological principles (lab).
12. Apply the scientific method and philosophy of science by designing components of, and carrying out, physiological experiments (lab).

Outcomes
1. Illustrate how the integration and regulation of organ systems affects the maintenance of homeostasis in the human body.
2. Relate the key functions of major organ systems with the cellular and molecular mechanisms that enable these functions.
3. Analyze examples of disease processes and relate them to aberrations of normal physiological function.
4. Utilize the process of science to design and carry out physiological experiments, analyze resulting data, and relate results to physiological principles.

Student Learning Outcomes

Demonstrate basic nursing skills using proper techniques and measures to promote safe, quality patient-centered care.

Integrate knowledge within a concept based framework for application to patient care.

Demonstrate the nurse's role in supporting a patient's psychosocial needs, functional ability, pain, perfusion, fluid and electrolyte balance.

Apply knowldge of pharmacology, pathophysiology and nutrition, as well as established evidence, to the care of patients.

Apply selected concepts related to professionalism, including the nursing process, to the provision of safe, quality patient care.

Integrate fundamental concepts related to the provision of safe, quality, patient-centered care: health promotion, culture, evidence, informatics, professionalism, communication and safety.
