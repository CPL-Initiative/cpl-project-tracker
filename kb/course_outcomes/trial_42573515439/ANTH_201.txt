Topics & Scope:
The steps in the scientific process and the meaning of theory in science.
(Obj 1)

An introduction to anthropology and the anthropological perspective.

A. Cultural Anthropology

B. Linguistic Anthropology

C. Archaeology (prehistoric/historic)

D. Physical Anthropology (forensic, dental, paleopathology, paleoanthropology)

E. Holistic, comparative and field work approaches to cultural and biological variation

Development and history of evolutionary thinking:

A. Covers key historical figures whose concepts provide a foundation for evolutionary thought

B. Natural and artificial selection

C. Principles of scientific classification

Assignments:
Independent Work (minimum 2 examples):
Develop a critical essay on biocultural differences and similarities between Homo neandertalensis and Homo sapiens. Students should pay particular attention to tool technologies, symbolic behavior and anatomy.
(Obj 1, 10, 11, 12, 14, 15)
Independent Work (minimum 2 examples):
Consult a primate database (such as Primateinfonet) to research nonhuman primates. Students will list taxonomy, common name, environment, physical traits and endangered species status.
(Obj 2, 7, 8, 9)
Critical Thinking Assignments (minimum 2 examples):
After working in small groups, students create a list of bipedal skeletal characteristics.
(Obj 12)
Critical Thinking Assignments (minimum 2 examples):
After working in small groups, students create a list of quadrupedal (great ape) skeletal characteristics.
(Obj 7, 8, 9, 12)
Critical Thinking Assignments (minimum 2 examples):
After watching a chimpanzee video, students discuss behavior, environment, locomotion, social structure and tool use.
(Obj 7, 8, 9)
