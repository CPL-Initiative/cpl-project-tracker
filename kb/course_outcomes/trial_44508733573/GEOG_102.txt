Course Outline
GEOG 102 - Cultural Geography
Division: Science and Mathematics
Department: Geography
Description

Are you fascinated with the enormous diversity of culture, language, religion, economics, politics, urbanization, agriculture, and ethnicity around the world? Have you ever wondered how this developed? Are you concerned about human rights, social justice, climate change, and access to clean drinking water, healthcare, education, and resources? Using the tools of geography, this course will help you to understand how humans interact each other and how humans interact with the environment.

Student Learning Outcomes
Upon successful completion of this course, students should be able to:
Analyze geographic patterns of income, urbanization, resource consumption, population growth, and access to education, healthcare, drinking water, and food within wealthy (most developed), middle income (developing), and poor (least developed) countries.
Compare and contrast origin areas (cultural hearths) of major world religions and languages with modern distributions of major world religions and languages.
Assess human rights within selected countries and regions, including the rights of women, ethnic, and religious minorities, immigrants, aboriginal peoples, and LGBTQ+ populations.
Course Objectives
Compare and contrast environmental determinism, eco-feminism, and human ecology as modes of analyzing human-environment relationships.
Differentiate folk and popular culture.
Distinguish among formal, functional, and vernacular culture regions.
Analyze the general distribution of population across the surface of the Earth, emphasizing relationships between density, development, and economic activity.
Examine the dynamic origins of agriculture, including the relationships between early agriculture and the origins of cities.
Construct a general historical geography of the Industrial Revolution, including technology systems.
Categorize the world distribution of the major languages and religions.
Distinguish between political geography and geopolitics.
Differentiate among traditional patterns of urban structure, including North American cities, European Cities, Latin American Cities, Islamic Cities, and cities in the developing world.
Evaluate the unevenness of economic development across the globe.
Analyze the differential impact of climate change on wealthy and disadvantaged populations.
Evaluate the socioeconomic status and political representation of historically marginalized groups, including women, ethnic and religious minorities, immigrants, aboriginal peoples and LGBTQ+ populations.

Out of Class Assignments
Reading Assignments

Read a current article relevant to immigrants and refugees and be able to take a quiz on the article during the next class period.

Writing Assignments
In a 2-3 page paper, discuss the different types of population growth a state (country) experiences as it goes through the four stages of the Demographic Transition.
In a 1-2 page paper, define and discuss three major types of migration push and pull factors.
Within a 3-4 page paper, explain how supranational organizations like the European Union (EU) aim to balance economic growth and environmental protection, in light of member state and economic pressures (e.g. the British exit from the EU). 
Critical Thinking Assignments

Read a current article relevant to the HIV/AIDS pandemic, cholera, ebola, or other infectious diseases.  Be prepared to lead a class discussion on geographic strategies to mitigate the diffusion of infectious diseases, especially within the developing world.
