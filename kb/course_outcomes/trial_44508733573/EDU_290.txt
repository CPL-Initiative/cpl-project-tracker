Need for the Course

EDU 290 is an introductory course for those entering into the field of elementary education. It provides students with a foundation in the historical and philosophical trends of education in the United States.  The course will also introduce topics such as teacher preparation, educational theory, and general principles of teaching school-age children. This course is Associate Degree applicable and transfers to CSU and UC.

Goals for the Course
To acquaint students with the social and philosophical foundations of formal education  
To introduce the various theories and practices of teaching as well as the implications of current policy and reforms.
To prepare students to work with school age children using sound developmental principles
For students to evaluate the quality of their own educational experiences
To prepare students to participate in a field experience lab in an elementary school setting
Catalog/Schedule Description
This course examines the evolution of education in the United States with a view to the philosophical foundations, historical trends, government involvement, law, and pedagogical movements. This course will also explore teacher preparation, education theory, multiculturalism, school reforms, safe schools, and controversial matters such as student rights, unions, and funding.
Student Learning Outcomes:
Upon satisfactory completion of the course, students will be able to:
Students will identify the various philosophies of education and explain their application and impact on American education
Students will describe the emergence of educational education in history and describe its impact on teaching and learning
Students will identify the responsibilities of the general public, state and federal government, and local school to provide education, and the challenges with meeting those responsibilities.
Course Objectives
Upon satisfactory completion of the course, students will be able to:
Express an understanding of the teaching profession related to historical influences, philosophical trends, educational theory, and preparation of the teacher.
Identify and explain the central issues in history and development of American education.
Students will identify the principle laws and court decisions that guide current practice
Explain how schools are governed, influenced, and financed.
Identify the components of a quality educational program and environment.
Explain strategies and ethical responsibilities to support diversity, equality and antiracism within the classroom.
Describe personal motives and values for teaching.
Summarize and critique readings relevant to issues in education such as contemporary issues, safety, poverty, cultural context, culturally responsive teaching.
Describe and support the idea of relevant curriculum in a global context. Including knowledge of state standards and current educational system reforms.
Identify the procedural and practical aspects in the day to day life of an elementary educator.
Discuss and support through research relevant educational topics such as gender, equal opportunity, social class, race and school achievement, legal implications of teaching, school effectiveness, curriculum development, child guidance and classroom management, children with special circumstances, roles of teachers, diverse neeeds of students, professionalism etc.
Compare and contrast traditional and non-traditional methods of instruction and evaluate the benefits and limitations of each
