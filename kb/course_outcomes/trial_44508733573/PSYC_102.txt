Need for the Course

PSYC 102 satisfies a general education requirement in Social Sciences for both the UC and CSU systems.  This course also fulfills a requirement for the AA in Psychology and is an option for students wishing to earn an AA-T in Psychology.  No other course offered on this campus provides an application of psychological concepts to day-to-day concerns such as stress, coping, and adjustment to life circumstances.

Goals for the Course
To satisfy part of the requirements for obtaining an AA degree in Psychology and part of the requirements for the AA-T degree in Psychology.
To satisfy part of the transfer requirements at four-year colleges and universities.
To offer a class in psychology that provides students with a deeper understanding of contemporary issues in human adjustment.
To provide a class in psychology that emphasizes the connection between research, theory, and practical applications to everyday life.
To provide an understanding of, and tolerance for, individual and cultural differences as they relate to adjustment issues.
Catalog/Schedule Description
Examination of human capacity to change, adapt, and cope with a variety of circumstances over the life span. Special attention is paid to the sociocultural contexts of adjustment, the role of self understanding in adjustment, stress, adult development, careers, and family issues. A variety of theoretical perspectives will be used to review healthy and unhealthy behavior patterns as well as coping techniques for day-to-day concerns.
Student Learning Outcomes:
Upon satisfactory completion of the course, students will be able to:
Students will demonstrate a broad understanding of the dynamics of change and choice throughout the lifespan, and reflect on the efficacy of their own life choices.
Students will relate concepts and perspectives associated with personal and social well-being to their own lives.
Students will apply theories of human development to personal and social growth and change.
Students will apply methods and approaches to conducting psychological research, as well as hone their ability to read and evaluate research studies in the discipline.
Demonstrate skills necessary for college success, including planning, time management, and short/long term goal setting
Course Objectives
Upon satisfactory completion of the course, students will be able to:
Identify contemporary issues in adjustment.
Compare and evaluate research methods used in psychology to examine adjustment including case studies, interviews, survey methods, quasi-experiments and laboratory experiments and correlations.
Compare and evaluate ethical issues in adjustment research.
Differentiate and evaluate the major psychological perspectives associated with personality and adjustment including trait approaches, biological approaches, psychoanalytic approaches, learning approaches, cognitive approaches, and humanistic approaches.
Evaluate the major concepts associated with stress including sources of stress, types of stress, physical reaction to stress, psychological reactions to stress, and individual differences in stress response.
Evaluate the major concepts associated with coping including definitions of coping, effective coping strategies, ineffective coping strategies, cultural, individual, and personality influences on coping.
Evaluate the major issues associated with development of the self-concept and identity including self-esteem, self-attributions, moral development and values, identity development, and cultural influences on self-concept and identity formation.
Distinguish the major concepts related to social cognition and adjustment including attitudes and behavior, person perception, persuasion, conformity and obedience, resisting social influence, and intergroup relations.
Distinguish the major issues associated with interpersonal communication including verbal and nonverbal communication, listening skills, speaking skills, self-disclosure, barriers to effective communication and enhancing communication.
Evaluate the major issues associated with gender and adjustment including contemporary perspectives on gender roles, gender stereotypes, gender similiarities and differences, development of gender identity and sociocultural influences on gender concepts.
Differentiate and evaluate the major concepts in human sexuality that impact adjustment including human sexual response, sexual knowledge, attitudes and behavior, sexual harassment, rape, sexual assault, child sexual abuse, and sexually transmitted diseases.
Describe and compare the major issues associated with interpersonal relationships including attraction and friendship, intimate relationships, jealousy and loneliness, lifestyle variations, family violence, and cultural influences on relationships.
Distinguish and evaluate the major issues associated with adult development including the family life cycle, parenting, personality development in adulthood, aging, and death and dying.
Distinguish and evaluate the major issues associated with careers including career development, career exploration and planning, job satisfaction, work-related stress, changes in the workplace and the workforces, and leisure.
Describe and compare the major issues associated with abnormal behavior including definitions of abnormality, anxiety disorders, mood disorders, schizophrenia, personality disorders, substance-use disorders, biological and cultural influences on disorders, and legal issues and disorders.
Describe and compare the major concepts associated with the treatment of disorders including psychoanalytic therapies, humanistic therapies, behavior therapies, cognitive therapies, biological therapies, group therapies, community psychology, guidelines for seeking therapy, effectiveness of therapies, and sociocultural issues and therapy effectiveness.
Distinguish and evaluate the major issues associated with health including historical perspectives on health psychology, promoting healthy lifestyles, coping with illness, substance abuse and addiction, and cultural influences on health.
Design a research project, collect empirical data, analyze the data, and compare the data to contemporary research and theory in child and adolescent psychology.
Compose and analyze a multi-source research paper on an issue in child and adolescent psychology employing appropriate research techniques and documentation in APA format.

Methods of Evaluation
The methods and percentages are examples for guidance and are not intended to be strictly followed.

A written review of at least three research studies focusing on human adjustment in professional psychology journals and an analysis of these studies application to adjustment issues using APA format to document sources. (0-10%)
An original research project applying psychological methods and theory in the area of human adjustment, obtaining data via case studies, interviews, or questionnaires, analyzing, evaluating, and interpreting this information using APA format (0-50%)
Objective examinations (e.g. true-false, multiple choice, fill-weekly quizzes, group tasks, final examinations) (0-50%)
Essay tests including take-home questions and/or short-answer/definition questions as part of the in class exams (0-50%)
In-class small group and individual activities and exercises requiring students to discuss, apply, analyze, compare, contrast, or evaluate contemporary adjustment issues (0-15%)
Essay assignments (approximately 250-500 words in length) requiring students to discuss, apply, analyze, compare, contrast, or evaluate adjustment issues presented in magazines, newspapers, websites, or other sources using standard rules of punctuation, spelling, grammar, and involving incorporation of course terms and concepts, and interpretation of narrative data into coherent paragraphs which support a point (0-20%)
On-line discussion using Learning Management System forums (0-50%)
Participation (0-10%)
