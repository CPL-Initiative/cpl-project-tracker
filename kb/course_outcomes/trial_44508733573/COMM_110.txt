Need for the Course

Professionals in many fields, including speech communication, broadcasting, and theatre, rely on their voice as a major tool in their work. COMM/THART 110 will provide students majoring in these fields, as well as those who are non-native speakers of English, the knowledge and skills necessary to analyze and improve their effectiveness as communicators. This course is associate degree applicable and transfers to UC and CSU.

Goals for the Course
To provide students the knowledge and skills necessary to understand, appreciate and use the human voice.
To allow students the opportunity to improve their effectiveness as speakers and performers.
To provide a support course to other courses in both the speech communication and theatre arts programs.
Catalog/Schedule Description
Techniques in voice production, including theory and practice in developing vocal skills for performance including breathing, rate, pitch, articulation, and quality. Application of concepts for students preparing for careers in communication, broadcasting, and theatre as well as non-native speakers of English. This course is offered as COMM 110 and THART 110.
Student Learning Outcomes:
Upon satisfactory completion of the course, students will be able to:
demonstrate the ability to read an excerpt for a play, news copy or other text using proper voice and diction
Course Objectives
Upon satisfactory completion of the course, students will be able to:
demonstrate the ability to read an excerpt for a play, news copy or other text using proper voice and diction
compare and contrast the major dialects used in stage speech
demonstrate the ability to speak through and listen to accented speech
compare and contrast the various dialects and accen
demonstrate the ability to match nonverbal movements in synchronous motion with selected vocal dynam
distinguish and properly articulate different types of vowels and consonants
demonstrate proficient use of the International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA)
demonstrate pitch, rate, volume and timbre appropriate to the meaning of a text
describe the effects of pitch, rate and timbre on the perception of spoken messages
identify the basic structure, function and influences on human speech and language
describe the anatomy and physiology associated with voice production
analyze their own voice and use this knowledge to aid in improving vocal qualities

UDL and UDL Curricular Design Framework
Universal Design for Learning (UDL) and Diversity, Equity, Inclusion, Accessibility, and Anti-Racism (DEIAA) principles are intentionally embedded throughout the Course Outline of Record to support equitable learning and facilitate DEIAA review through the following elements:
Course Title: Titles are written clearly to reflect content using inclusive language that supports understanding for all students and avoids "gatekeeping" terminology.
Catalog Description: Descriptions use accessible, asset-based language that validates student experiences and engages diverse learners.
Objectives and Course Learning Outcomes: Objectives and outcomes are stated clearly to advance equitable results and include the critical analysis of diversity and equity within the discipline.
Content: Curriculum is intentionally designed to accommodate different learning needs and reflect inclusive, non-monocultural perspectives.
Outside of Class Assignments: Assignments offer diverse ways to demonstrate mastery and reduce barriers to provide equitable, engaging practice aligned with course goals.
Methods of Instruction: Instruction utilizes an inclusive design framework featuring multi-modal delivery and active participation responsive to a broad range of cultural perspectives.
Methods of Evaluation: Evaluation utilizes transparent assessments—both formative and summative—prioritizing clear communication and iterative feedback to eliminate achievement gaps.
Digital Accessibility (WCAG & Section 508): All digital components, including documents, media, and third-party tools, are designed to meet current WCAG (Level AA) and Section 508 standards. This ensures that technology serves as a bridge to content rather than a barrier to entry for students using assistive technologies.
Distance Education: Course design ensures full digital accessibility and equivalent engagement across all modalities through Regular and Substantive Interaction (RSI).
Regular and Substantive Interaction (RSI): Scheduled, instructor-led interactions support student persistence by initiating frequent, meaningful feedback and building a sense of belonging.
Representative Textbooks & OER: Selection prioritizes zero-cost materials and Open Educational Resources (OER) to eliminate financial barriers that disproportionately affect minoritized students.
Instructional Materials: Materials incorporate authors and scholars from diverse backgrounds to ensure representative perspectives and a globally relevant curriculum.
Anti-Racist Curriculum Audit: Course content and disciplinary histories are critically examined to challenge systemic biases and integrate the contributions of historically excluded BIPOC scholars.
