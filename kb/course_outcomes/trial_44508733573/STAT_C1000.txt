Course Outline
STAT C1000 - Introduction to Statistics
Division: Science and Mathematics
Department: Mathematics
Description

This course is an introduction to statistical thinking and processes, including methods and concepts for discovery and decision-making using data.  Topics include descriptive statistics; probability and sampling distributions; statistical inference; correlation and linear regression; analysis of variance, chi-squared, and t-tests; and application of technology for statistical analysis including the interpretation of the relevance of the statistical findings. Students apply methods and processes to applications using data from a broad range of disciplines.

Student Learning Outcomes
Upon successful completion of this course, students should be able to:
Describe and summarize data of samples and populations.
Correctly apply the addition or multiplication rules of a probability experiment.
Correctly evaluate probability from a binomial or normal distribution.
Correctly define and conduct a hypothesis test.
Course Objectives
Part 1 - Common Course Numbering Objectives:
Assess how data were collected and recognize how data collection affects what conclusions can be drawn from the data.
Identify appropriate graphs and summary statistics for variables and relationships between them and correctly interpret information from graphs and summary statistics.
Describe and apply probability concepts and distributions.
Demonstrate an understanding of, and ability to use, basic ideas of statistical processes, including hypothesis tests and confidence interval estimation.
Identify appropriate statistical techniques and use technology-based statistical analysis to describe, interpret, and communicate results.
Evaluate ethical issues in statistical practice.
Part 2 - Local Course Objectives:
Recognize the proper use of statistics and distinguish them from the abuse of statistics.
Distinguish among different scales of measurement and their implications.
Apply concepts of sample space and probability.
Apply proper rules of probability.
Identify and understand the methods of data collection while evaluating the quality and appropriateness of the data.
Describe, analyze, and summarize the data of samples and populations, using numerical and graphical summaries.
Calculate measures of central tendency and variation for a given data set.
Calculate the mean and variance of a discrete distribution.
Calculate probabilities using normal and t-distributions.
Construct and interpret confidence intervals.
Compare and contrast different kinds of probability distributions, including the binomial, the uniform and the normal, and select the correct distribution in applications.
Run various tests of hypotheses on various types of sample statistics, while appropriately using the Central Limit Theorem.
Apply techniques of linear correlation and regression of bivariate relations.
Determine and interpret levels of statistical significance including p-values.
Identify the basic concept of hypothesis testing including Type I and II errors.
Select the appropriate technique for testing a hypothesis and interpret the result.
Formulate hypothesis tests involving samples from one and two populations.
Use linear regression and ANOVA analysis for estimation and inference, and interpret the associated statistics.
Interpret the output of a technology-based statistical analysis.
Compare and contrast applications from business, psychology, sociology, life science, health science, education and other disciplines.
Correctly use statistical technology (software such as Excel or MINITAB) to calculate and display descriptive statistics of samples and populations.

Out of Class Assignments
Reading Assignments

Read the study adapted from the article “Ages of Oscar-winning Best Actors and Actresses” by Richard Brown and Gretchen Davis in Mathematics Teacher magazine. Pay particular attention to the birth dates and gender of the winners and the dates of the award. Explore the data using appropriate statistics and graphs. Determine whether there are significant differences between the ages of the Best Actresses and the ages of the Best Actors by using appropriate hypothesis tests. Describe the methods used and the conclusions reached.

Writing Assignments

Locate a professional journal with an article that uses a statistical analysis of an experiment. Describe and comment on the design of the experiment. Identify one particular issue and determine whether the result was found to be statistically significant. Determine whether that same result has practical significance. Support your response in a typed one-page paper.

Critical Thinking Assignments

Under a given assumption, if the probability of a particular observed event is extremely small, researchers conclude that the assumption is probably not correct. Consider graduation rates, where a simple random sample is conducted of 1500 community college students who are seeking associate degrees and includes 790 who earned associate’s degrees within three years. Use a 0.01 significance level to test the claim that most community college students earn associate’s degrees within three years.
