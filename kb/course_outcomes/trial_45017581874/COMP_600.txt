Catalog Description
Introduction to Networks (ITN) is the first course in the Cisco Certified Network Associate (CCNA) program and introduces the architectures, models, protocols, and networking elements that connect users, devices, applications and data through the internet and across modern computer networks – including Internet Protocol (IP) addressing and Ethernet fundamentals. By the end of the course, students can build simple local area networks (LANs) that integrate IP addressing schemes, foundational network security, and perform basic configurations for routers and switches. The course helps students develop workforce readiness skills and builds a foundation for success in networking-related careers and certificate programs.

Goals
Course Goals
Goals Text
The course aims to provide students with a foundational understanding of network operations, including building simple LANs, integrating IP addressing schemes, implementing basic network security, and configuring routers and switches. Emphasizing critical thinking, problem-solving, and collaboration, the course prepares students for continued computer network training through the Cisco Networking Academy, ultimately leading to the CCNA certification exam.
Legacy Data
Provide instruction in the theory of operation and design of networks and intranetworks. Students will learn how data travels over a network; how routers, switches and other devices operate; how to design a network; how network topologies and protocols work. Students will be able to explain the functions of the OSI model layers and configure IP addressing. Students will be able to cut and install various types of cables and adhere to basic electricity and safety procedures. This course will prepare the student to take certification exams like the CCNA Certification Exam (Cisco Certified Network Associate). Students will learn to show a spirit of cooperation and teamwork by completing assigned group tasks. Completion of these tasks will enhance the students’ reading, writing, communication and mathematical competencies. Interpretation of technical terms used in networking and telecommunications technology, both verbal and written is covered. Students who successfully complete this course will be prepared to move on to the next phase of networking training, which can include the Cisco Networking Academy in preparation to take the CCNA Certification Exam.

Legacy Data
Upon successful completion of this course, students will demonstrate through theory and practical application, problem solving, critical thinking, written and oral communication, mathematical ability and the following skills and knowledge: 1. Describe the seven layers of the OSI model. 2. Describe the functions of the seven layers of the OSI model and TCP/IP model. 3. Describe how data travels up and down the OSI model and TCP/IP model. 4. Describe the OSI and TCP/IP model layer devices. 5. Use Binary Mathematics. 6. Use hexadecimal numbering system. 7. Convert binary to decimal. 8. Describe the function and use MAC addresses. 9. Describe and create IP addresses. 10. Configure IP addresses and convert the addresses to binary. 11. Describe IP Class ranges: A, B, C, etc. 12. Describe the difference between a network address and a broadcast address. 13. Describe TCP/IP, including IPv4 and IPv6 addressing and show how subnetting is accomplished. 14. Configure subnet IP addresses and describe subnet masking. 15. Demonstrate an understanding of LAN topologies (Star, Bus, Ring, Mesh and Hierarchical). 16. Demonstrate a knowledge of basic electricity and safety precautions. 17. Describe cable configurations and standards. 18. Demonstrate network cable configurations. 19. Design a basic network. 20. Configure Wiring Closet hardware and premise equipment. 21. Setup horizontal and vertical cable. 22. Terminate and test cable.
Assignments
Reading Assignments
Optional Text
Assignments

Assigned texts, student lab source files, white papers, instructor-written materials, technical manuals, instructor selected URLs, and other publications related to design and implementation of networks. For example, students may conduct research using a web search engine to learn about the non-profit organizations that are responsible for establishing international standards for the internet and the development of internet technologies.

Writing Assignments
Optional Text
Assignments

Preparing text for projects, maintaining a journal for recording laboratory assignments and projects, group reports, and performing mathematic calculations. For example, working alone or as a team, students work with IPv4 subnets and determine network and host information based on a given IP address and subnet mask.

Appropriate Outside Assignments
Optional Text
Assignments

The use of resources to supplement assigned course materials, including interactive network simulation tools to build practical skills; practice certification exams and guides to test for understanding; vendor documentation, technical blogs, and user forums to troubleshoot and resolve misconfigurations and issues in lab assignments; video tutorials and other web resources for extended learning, and participation in discussion boards, chats, and user groups to build professional competence. An example assignment may include a targeted job search on the web to find the types of IT jobs and titles, the associated skills and certifications, and typical salary ranges.

Appropriate Assignments that Demonstrate Critical Thinking
Optional Text
Assignments

Assignments that demonstrate critical thinking involve analyzing and evaluating learning materials, and applying this analysis in classroom discussions, writing assignments, and laboratory activities. Students then select and use appropriate methods and materials to complete laboratory assignments.

For example, a work-based scenario presents the student with a typical computer network issue and challenges the student to use troubleshooting tools to identify the issue, establish a theory of probable cause, test the theory, establish a plan of action, implement the solution, verify functionality, and document findings and configuration changes.
