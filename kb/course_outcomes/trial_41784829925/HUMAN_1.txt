Old World Culture
HUMAN 1:

3.0 Units


Print

        

This course examines the intellectual and artistic achievements of Western culture from prehistory to the Renaissance focusing on major movements, key figures, religious developments, and enduring works across art, architecture, literature, drama, philosophy, and music. Students will analyze and interpret these works within their historical, cultural, and philosophical contexts, compare and contrast diverse expressions of the human experience, and develop and advance historical understanding of major civilizations and cultures through the study of the ideas and artistic practices that have shaped Western civilization over time.

Learning Outcomes
Upon satisfactory completion of this course, the student should be prepared to:
Think critically, by interpreting and evaluating the role that ancient, medieval, and humanistic worldviews play in the production of the arts, literature, religion, and philosophy within Western culture.
Think critically, by interpreting and evaluating the significance of the arts, literature, religion, and philosophy in giving form and meaning to the human experience in Western culture from prehistory to the Renaissance.
Demonstrate a knowledge of the major periods in Western culture from prehistory to the Renaissance, including the key ideas, figures, and artistic movements that define them.
