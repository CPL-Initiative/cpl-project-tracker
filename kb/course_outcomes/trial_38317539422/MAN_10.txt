Course Description
Prerequisite: None
Course Credit Recommendation: Degree Credit


A basic operations course to prepare individuals for modern production environments that use industry 4.0 controls, automation, and process. Appropriate for individuals working in any occupation in advanced manufacturing.

Before entering the course, students should be able to demonstrate the following skills:

Course Objectives:
Upon successful completion of the course, students should be able to demonstrate the following activities:

Describe the Concepts and terminology of Smart Manufacturing
Understand the machine operation safety guidelines.
Describe basic types of tools and fasteners.
Define dimensional measurement and explain its importance. Perform measurement conversion.
Describe the types of Engineering drawings used in Manufacturing.
Describe basic electrical control, pneumatic, robot operations and sensor system operations.
Monitor machinery using Human Machine Interface, Internet, and Ethernet
Student Learning Outcomes:
Upon successful completion of the course, students should be able to demonstrate the following skills:

Describe an employer's PPE responsibilities.
Describe how to interpret a third angle projection multiview drawing.
Define electrical power and give its units of measurement
Describe how to run a robot program.
Explain the advantages and disadvantages of electronic sensors.
Course Content:

This course covers a wide range of advanced manufacturing topics including safety, basic hand tools, measurement, print reading, and the fundamentals of mechanical, fluid power, electrical, and automation.

Methods of Instruction:
Methods of instruction used to achieve student learning outcomes may include, but are not limited to, the following activities:

Facilitated discussions regarding Mechanical power transmission, material handling, sensors and other automation tools/systems and technologies.
Create and present lectures, demonstrations, video presentations, and lab exercises to explain and demonstrate industrial automation systems such as programmable logic control systems, motor controls, Ethernet Communications, and Human Machine interface technologies.
Present basic electrical, pneumatic and robotic operations, providing students with the opportunity to use concepts learned in class and lab to analyze real industrial automation scenarios.
Demonstrate the use of tools and technology to maintain and manage industrial automation
Guided Practice: provide step-by-step instructions on how to use PLCs, and event sequencing, sensors.
Lab Activities on how to start a machine.
Problem-based learning such as how to use force equations for fluid power systems.
Methods of Evaluation:
Students will be evaluated for progress in and/or mastery of student learning outcomes using methods of evaluation which may include, but are not limited to, the following activities:

Examination designed to provide objective evidence that students have attained the level of understanding expected in areas detailed in the student learning objectives such as industry 4.0 controls, automation and processes.
Individual, small group, or paired activities designed to allow students to demonstrate understanding of basic industrial automation concepts and tools.
Quizzes and in-class participation demonstrating proficiency in the subject matter.
A mid-term and final examination designed to assess student mastery of concepts, safety for industry, measurement and types of power systems commonly used in industrial automation technology.
Lab exercises designed to assess student capability to use technical tools, systems and resources to manage industrial automation tasks, documentation and safety requirements.
