Course Description
Prerequisite: None
Course Credit Recommendation: Degree Credit


Develops basic skills in listening, reading, speaking and writing. Emphasis on acquisition of vocabulary, structures and grammatical patterns necessary for comprehension of native spoken and written Arabic at the beginning level. Includes discussion of Arabic culture and daily life.

Course Objectives:
Upon successful completion of the course, students should be able to demonstrate the following activities:

Comprehend standard spoken Arabic related to the individual and his/her surroundings, likes and dislikes, daily routine, leisure activities and past experiences with a recognition vocabulary of 1000 words.

Apply reading strategies in order to understand and analyze 150- to 200-word short articles, dialogues, stories and other authentic literary and factual texts related to the self, family and surroundings, written in Arabic alphabet.
Use acquired elementary vocabulary and grammar to communicate orally about emotional and mental states; the individual; school and work; origin and family; plans and needs; sports and cultural events with an active vocabulary of approximately 500 words.
Use targeted beginning-level grammar and vocabulary to compose 100- to 150-word paragraphs of connected discourse on personal, familiar and cultural topics, using Arabic alphabet.
Identify and use the indicative mood of regular, irregular, and stem-changing verbs in the simple present, present progressive, near future and preterite when engaged in receptive and communicative activities.
Recognize and apply gender and number agreement rules with definite and indefinite articles; descriptive, possessive and demonstrative adjectives; subject, reflexive, direct and indirect object, double object, and relative pronouns when using both receptive and expressive skills.
Understand and use imperative, declarative, negative and interrogative simple and compound sentences in oral and written activities.
Identify verbal and non-verbal differences between Arabic-speaking cultures and their own as related to greetings, leave-taking, and levels of formality.
Identify verbal and non-verbal differences between Arabic-speaking cultures and their own as related to education, family, daily life, traditions, holidays, and artistic expression.

Course Content:

Lectures, class practice and laboratories will be dedicated to the logical presentation of the topics listed below.  The structural and grammatical patterns necessary for effective communication at the beginning level will be introduced.

Vocabulary  Topics - Introduction of vocabulary necessary for effective communication (reading, writing, listening and speaking) about the individual, his/her family and immediate surroundings. Topics to facilitate such communication include, but are not limited to the following.  They will be presented as they relate to the framework of both Arabic-speaking cultures and students' native cultures. Oneself: Greetings, introductions, leave-taking; basic descriptions: Physical appearance, personality, colors, physical and emotional/mental states, clothing, numbers, prices, description of objects, comparing people, things:

Surroundings: School (the classroom, objects, classes, commands), work, places (countries, languages),weather, seasons, telling time, home (the house, rooms, furniture, the neighborhood), location
Personal information: Origin, nationality, age, professions, telephone number, address, days of the week, months, years, dates, birthdays, asking and answering questions, family and relatives
Abilities, plans, obligations: Immediate obligations, duties, desires (present and future), preferences, likes, dislikes, plans, abilities, wants, needs
Daily routine: Activities at home, activities at school and in the classroom, activities at work
Leisure activities: Sports, favorite activities, cultural events, shopping
Experiences: reporting past events and facts, asking and answering questions about past experiences
Presentation of topics such as, but not limited to, the following:

Grammatical Topics - Introduction of the structural and grammatical patterns necessary for effective communication (reading, writing, listening and speaking) about the individual and their family and immediate surroundings.  Topics to facilitate such communication include, but are not limited to the following:

Structure: simple and compound sentence structure (imperative, declarative, negative, interrogative)
Number: singular and plural forms of nouns, subject and object pronouns, definite and indefinite articles, possessive adjectives, demonstrative adjectives, reflexive pronouns; number agreement between nouns and adjectives
Gender: masculine and feminine forms of nouns, adjectives, definite and indefinite articles, demonstrative adjectives, and subject and object pronouns, gender agreement between nouns and adjectives
Person: agreement between person and verb
Tenses and verb constructions:  present, present progressive, periphrastic future, infinitives, conjugated verbs plus infinitive, preterite.

Cultural Topics –  Presentation of topics which help students reflect on and evaluate the similarities and differences between their culture and those of the Arabic-speaking world.

The role of age and gender in interpersonal relationships and language exchange
Geography and topography and their influence on language and culture
National origins and backgrounds
Holidays, traditions, customs and religious beliefs as seen through music, literature, art and film
Historical sites
Familial roles and responsibilities
Differences and similarities between the daily life and leisure activities of the students' native culture and Arabic culture (academic life, housing, shopping, foods, sports, parties, home life, city vs. country life, etc.)
People of Arabic culture who have made important cultural, technological, and historical contributions to their own country and the world.
Dialectical variations
Verbal and non-verbal language as a means of conveying attitudes, feelings and emotions
Forms of address (formal/informal)

Methods of Instruction:
Methods of instruction used to achieve student learning outcomes may include, but are not limited to, the following activities:

Presenting lectures and discussions in Arabic illustrated with visual and audio effects in order to increase students’ ability to comprehend the spoken language; reinforce targeted cultural, semantic, syntactical and morphological items; and guide students in the application of listening, reading, speaking and writing strategies when communicating about themselves, their family and immediate surroundings.

Example: The instructor shows pictures of famous people and uses descriptive adjectives and the verbs "Said" and "Pasha" to describe their personal characteristics and clothing.  Following this model, students pair up and describe five famous people.  Afterwards, students share one description with the rest of the class members, either orally, on the whiteboard or on the Discussion Board

Showing/playing videos, music, interactive DVD’s, and selections recorded from TV, radio or the Internet in order to help students improve their ability to comprehend communication about familiar topics when Arabic is spoken at native speed; provide them with virtual access to Arabic-speaking communities as they compare of those of the students; cultures and their products; stimulate discussion about life, culture and history within these communities; and encourage the expression of opinions at the intermediate level.

Example: Before showing any cultural video, the instructor previews it by asking questions to activate prior knowledge of the topic.  Next, students watch the video and answer follow-up questions either orally or in writing.  This is followed by whole-class comparison of Arabic culture to the students' own cultures.

Presenting reading activities, which may include cultural selections and anecdotes, narratives, authentic poems and stories, magazines, newspapers, children's literature, Web articles, comic books, grammar, etc., that guide students in the application of reading strategies, help them draw logical conclusions from their reading materials and lead them in a sensitive comparison and contrast between the culture, history, current events and daily life of Arabic-speaking communities and their own.

Example: Instructor presents and reviews the day’s reading strategy. Students are then instructed to find and skim a cultural website that is dedicated to some celebrations in the Arabic world. They then share the traditional elements and activities of the celebration by posting them on the Discussion Board or by writing them on the whiteboard


Creating and assigning pair and small group communicative activities such as interviews, post-reading activities, dialogues, picture descriptions, games, skits, student creation of PowerPoint presentations, short group compositions, problem-solving puzzles, and other oral/aural communicative activities in order to help students practice specific speaking strategies and skills in the context of the target culture, vocabulary and grammar.

Example: Students are asked to post a blog to Canvas in which they describe their weekly routine.

Developing and assigning writing activities, which may include creative in-class  sentences, short descriptions, paragraphs, compositions, written exercises from workbook and textbook and close activities in order to aid students in mastering intermediate-level syntax, semantics and morphology, and to help them in writing well-structured compositions.

Example: In groups, students choose a famous figure (an actor, a college president, a politician, a music star, a professional athlete, etc.) and create interview questions and answers for that person in Arabic. Volunteers from each group act out the interview without identifying the famous person. The class guesses who the person is


Conducting individual conferences in order to advise students on their needs and progress.

Example: Before the submission of Early Alert forms, students are asked to schedule individual conferences with the instructor to review their grades, talk about their needs and receive advice


Instructing and aiding students with computer-based language programs that reinforce targeted vocabulary and grammatical structures

Example: Students are asked to go to the lab to use Rosetta Stone to review clothing vocabulary in Arabic. They then write a paragraph of 100 words about the clothes that they would like to buy for the celebration of their upcoming birthday


Developing and assigning online tasks such as written and oral discussion board postings; viewing of online PowerPoint presentations; completion of functionally-oriented website-specific vocabulary, cultural, listening, viewing, reading and writing tasks; interactive vocabulary, grammar and culture games; web quests; and textbook companion website interactive activities in order to reinforce targeted cultural, semantic, syntactical and morphological items; provide an opportunity for the practice of listening, reading and writing strategies and skills; present students with up-to-date information on and virtual access to Arabic-speaking countries, communities and cultures; encourage consideration of and respect for cultural differences as well as an awareness of similarities; and stimulate and guide oral and written interactive communication.

Example: Students are asked to post a blog to Canvas in which they describe their day-to-day weekly routine.

Methods of Evaluation:
Students will be evaluated for progress in and/or mastery of student learning outcomes using methods of evaluation which may include, but are not limited to, the following activities:

Oral reports/presentations/performances designed to evaluate students’ ability to communicate about familiar topics and activities.
Participation in class discussions and activities designed to evaluate competency in and understanding of assigned material.
Written assignments designed to assess progress in the ability to create simple notes, messages and paragraphs about familiar topics and activities.
Listening, reading and essay-type quizzes/examinations designed to evaluate student ability to understand and draw logical conclusions from conversations and authentic cultural material on targeted topics related to the individual, family and immediate surroundings.
Student portfolios, workbook and textbook assignments designed to evaluate student mastery of specific vocabulary, grammatical and cultural topics.
Class and individual projects designed to assess student ability to make thoughtful comparisons between the target culture and their own.
Final examination designed to evaluate student learning outcomes and readiness for Arabic 2.
