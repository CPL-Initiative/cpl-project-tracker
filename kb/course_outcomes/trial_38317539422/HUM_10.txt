Course Description
Prerequisite: None
Course Credit Recommendation: Degree Credit


An introduction to the methods of the academic study of religion and analysis of the category of "religion." Provides a nonsectarian survey and analysis of the concepts, practices, and historical and cultural influences of major world religious traditions, which may include indigenous religions, such as Native American, African, Afro-Caribbean, and Oceanic religions; Asian-origin religions, such as Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism, Sikhism, Confucianism, Taoism, and Shinto; Middle Eastern-origin religions, such as Judaism, Christianity, Islam, and Zoroastrianism; and New Religious Movements, such as atheism, Scientology, Neopaganism, and New Age. Students may not receive credit for both HUM-10 and HUM-10H.

Course Objectives:
Upon successful completion of the course, students should be able to demonstrate the following activities:

Explain how the category of religion and its associated terminology are variously defined and also how those definitions may be contested.
Evaluate the various methodological approaches to religion, such as anthropological, sociological, historical, psychological, and philosophical.
Describe historical and contemporary varieties of religious experience, belief, and practice in at least 8 world religions across 3 world religious traditions.
Examine the relationship between historical and contemporary varieties of religious experience and the cultures in which they are found.
Discuss the role of primary religious texts in their religious traditions through their respective interpretive frameworks.
Examine the relationship between religious traditions and political and social issues that arise in their respective cultures.
Recognize the influence of Christian European colonization on the development of religious studies as an academic field.

Methods of Instruction:
Methods of instruction used to achieve student learning outcomes may include, but are not limited to, the following activities:

Presentation of lectures and discussions of topics, issues, and texts central to the analysis of religious traditions in order for students to understand the various forms of religious expression and how to study them.
Inviting guest writers/lecturers invited to class to discuss topics in their particular expertise in order to provide students with expert perspectives on topics related to world religions.
Facilitation of cooperative learning tasks and activities designed to create a collaborative construction of knowledge of course materials, such as through small group discussion and group projects.
Web-based activities to reinforce course content, such as through peer-to-peer discussion or to scaffold student assignments.
Showing films, videos, slides, distributing handouts, and/or using electronic or computer-based media in order to reinforce understanding of concepts related to course content.
Methods of Evaluation:
Students will be evaluated for progress in and/or mastery of student learning outcomes using methods of evaluation which may include, but are not limited to, the following activities:

Written assignments with a minimum of 3000 words (12 pages) that analyze primary and secondary texts about course content, designed to evaluate students’ understanding of key course concepts and how to apply them to diverse contexts.
Oral reports/presentations of the application of course concepts to various religious traditions, such as in leading a critical discussion on course content or analyzing a particular media using the methods and tools learned in the course.
Quizzes/examinations designed to assess students’ ability to recall, critically analyze, and apply key concepts.
In-class informal writing assignments, including journal entries, quizzes, peer review of student writing in order to ensure understanding of course concepts and to demonstrate required familiarity with the weekly material.
Sample Assignments:
Outside-of-Class Reading Assignments

Reading assignments will be drawn from primary and secondary texts, with emphasis placed on original texts drawn from a variety of religious traditions. Where possible, students should read some representative religious texts in their entirety (i.e., the Analects, the Tao Te Ching, the Bhagavad Gita, or selected books from within the Hebrew Scripture, the New Testament, or the Quran). 
Outside-of-Class Writing Assignments

All sections will include a requirement for written assignments to be completed outside of class, such as traditional research, analytical, and reflective essays, or alternative projects such as creating zines, podcasts, or websites that include written research, analytical, and reflective essays or scripts. All outside-of-class writing assignments will include an element of working with both primary and secondary sources.
Other Outside-of-Class Assignments

In addition to required out-of-class reading and writing assignments, assignments which require students to personally experience and observe different religious practices should be considered for inclusion among other assignments.
