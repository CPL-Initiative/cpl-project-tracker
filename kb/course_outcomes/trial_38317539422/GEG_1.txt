Course Description
Prerequisite: None
Course Credit Recommendation: Degree Credit


The interacting physical processes of air, water, land, and life that impact Earth’s surface. Topics include weather and climate, the water cycle, landforms and plate tectonics, and the characteristics of plant and animal life. Emphasis is on interrelationships among systems and processes and their resulting patterns and distributions. There is a heavy emphasis on maps as descriptive and analytical tools. Students may not receive credit for both GEG-1 and GEG-1H.

Methods of Instruction:
Methods of instruction used to achieve student learning outcomes may include, but are not limited to, the following activities:

Discussions and demonstrations in order to develop in-depth familiarity with terminology and complex understanding of facts, rules, principles, and mechanisms of the atmosphere, lithosphere, hydrosphere, and biosphere;
Discussions based on the reading of college-level primary texts that apply geographically based analyses and evaluations on elements of air, water, land and life;
Reading assignments involving both primary and secondary sources that discuss the scientific and social nexus of specific spheres and of the interrelationships between spheres, including current events;
Student presentations that highlight the spatial patterns of processes, as well as the underlying reasons and the future implications of patterns;
In-class and homework assignments in which students are directed to properly utilize geographic tools, such as maps, graphs, data, and images and are also directed to present information in ways that demonstrate to critical thinking and problem solving skills;
Field trips as a method to illustrate geographic processes and interactions between spheres;
Screening of DVDs or video clips that present scientific insights into physical processes and that offer multiple perspectives on contemporary issues;
Conferences in order to provide personal assessments of individual students’ strengths and weaknesses in oral presentations and of writing skills;
Guest lecturers in order to provide expert and personal insights into Earth processes.
Methods of Evaluation:
Students will be evaluated for progress in and/or mastery of student learning outcomes using methods of evaluation which may include, but are not limited to, the following activities:

Written in-class quizzes, tests, and examinations designed to demonstrate understanding of basic facts, rules, principles, and mechanisms of the atmosphere, lithosphere, hydrosphere, and biosphere.
Class discussions of course content which gauge individual student’s ability to utilize geographic tools appropriately, such as maps, graphs, data, and images.
Individual and group oral presentations which demonstrate understanding of basic physical processes and insights into the interrelationships between the four spheres.
Writing assignments in which students demonstrate geographic methods to analyze, evaluate, and explain spatial issues and problems.
Sample Assignments:
Outside-of-Class Reading Assignments

Read a current event article from a newspaper on a topic in Physical Geography.  Topics could include recent volcanic eruptions, earthquakes, hurricanes or climate change.  Write a critique of the article in which the article is summarized and an analysis of the geographic issue is presented in essay format.
Complete a multi-step group research project in which students create an annotated bibliography and in-class presentation on a contemporary or historic topic in Geography.  Examples of topics could include desertification, deforestation, urban heat islands and climate change.
Create a diagram of atmospheric circulation based on the textbook and class notes.  Diagram should include location and movement of global wind and pressure belts.
Outside-of-Class Writing Assignments

Write a 2 page description and analysis of Southern California’s seasonal precipitation pattern. What are the major atmospheric conditions controlling Southern California’s weather and climate? What role does the topography of Southern California play in the weather we experience? Discuss the positive and negative dimensions of Southern California’s seasonal precipitation patterns for people living in the area.
Other Outside-of-Class Assignments

Analyze your preparedness for a major earthquake that destroys sections of major highways and causes loss of electricity to most people in Southern California. Begin with general information about your household including pets. Take an inventory of food, water, medicine, medical supplies, and pet food. How many days could your family survive with your existing stock of supplies? What could you do in the future to be better prepared for an actual event?
