Catalog Course Description
This course is designed for those interested in learning to ride and handle horses. It includes basic equitation, proper seating and hands, tack identification and use, and basic care and grooming of horses.

Objectives
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:

Define and demonstrate the correct use of horse terminology.

Identify the most desirable horse for a particular purpose based on the criteria for use. This could include: work, pleasure, and sport.

List performance traits to be considered when selecting horses for a particular purpose.

Outline a plan for basic horse care, including feeding, parasite control, and stable management.

Identify and explain the function and maintenance of various tack.

Demonstrate the appropriate grooming technique for a horse. 

Demonstrate proper saddling and bridling techniques with an emphasis on safety.

Demonstrate the proper seat position in the saddle.

List and discuss safety rules and procedures for proper horse care and management.

Identify proper aids, control, and horse schooling techniques.

IDEAA:
IDEAA and UDL Review Statement:

This course affirms a strong commitment to diversity, equity, and inclusion as integral to both student success and the advancement of the equine industry. We recognize that students bring a wide range of cultural backgrounds, lived experiences, and perspectives that enrich the study of equine science and strengthen the learning environment. This course is designed to provide equitable access to both academic content and hands-on experiences, ensuring that all student regardless of race, ethnicity, gender identity, socioeconomic status, ability, or prior exposure to horses are supported in achieving their goals. By emphasizing inclusive teaching practices, ethical engagement, and collaborative learning, this course prepares students to enter the equine field as skilled professionals and culturally aware leaders who are equipped to contribute to a diverse and evolving industry.
