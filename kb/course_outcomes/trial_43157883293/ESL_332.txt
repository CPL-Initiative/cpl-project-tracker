Catalog Course Description
This course acts as an introduction to English and is the first of three levels offered. Language skills are expanded in communicative contexts and emphasis is placed on development of “social English.” This course may be offered in a distance education format.
Course Content
Lecture Content:

Language Skills



Listening
Understanding of simple words and phrases based on previously learned material.
Identifying main topics of conversation with familiar material.
Demonstrating understanding of simple, non-face-to-face speech in familiar contexts, such as routine announcements.
Demonstrating strategies to check for understanding (using simple words and phrases to ask for clarification).
Responding appropriately to short emergency warnings.
Speaking
Responding to simple questions about daily activities and basic needs.
Making statements in the present, past, and future tenses based on previously learned vocabulary.
Asking questions related to basic needs.
Communicating basic personal information using the phone.
Reading
Reading and understanding of short, simplified narrative paragraphs on familiar topics and with previously learned vocabulary and sentence patterns.
Identifying the sequence of simple narrative passages.
Expanding sight vocabulary related to daily needs in familiar contexts.
Using strategies such as predicting or phonics decoding to interpret new words in familiar contexts.
Writing
Writing simple sentences based on oral work or familiar material.
Writing of daily activities.
Writing a simple message.
Writing a series of related sentences based on personal experiences or previously learned material.
Writing cursive letters of the alphabet.
Language Forms
Sentence types
Simple statements:  affirmative and negative
Wh-questions and answers
Compound sentences with “and", "but”, and “or”
Complex sentences with "because", "when", "after", and "before"
Grammar
Simple present
Simple past with commonly used irregular verbs
Future—"going to", "will"
Modals—"can", "should", "might", "have to"
Comparatives and superlatives of adjectives and adverbs—introductory
Count and noncount nouns
Objectives
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:

Comprehend simple face-to-face conversations using previously learned material in pair and group oral and written contexts.

Orally produce simple statements and questions based on personal experiences or familiar material with 70% accuracy.

Interpret dialogues and short, adapted paragraphs drawn from previously learned vocabulary and structures in textbook assignments and on exams.

Use appropriate vocabulary related to day-to-day adult “survival” situations or life skills in areas such as housing, transportation, job safety, emergencies, and general health, both orally and in writing.

Respond appropriately to language that differentiates between past and present events, both orally and in writing.

Student Learning Outcomes
Broader than Objectives, SLOs are often comprised of what the student can do as a result of combining several course objectives.

Write Paragraph:  Write a paragraph of 5 to 7 sentences on a familiar topic.

Read and Interpret Passage:  Read and interpret a narrative or descriptive passage of at least 200 words.

Talk and Communicate Successfully:  Talk for 1 1/2 minutes or longer and communicate successfully. The student can discuss his or her daily schedule, a personal event, a class reading or another topic deemed appropriate by the instructor.

Outside-of-Class Assignments
Typical Assignment
Write and perform a dialogue for a job interview using target vocabulary and sentence structures.

Write a paragraph about your daily routine in the United States today and what your daily routine was in your native country.  You will need to use both the present and past tense to describe your activities.

IDEAA/UDL
IDEAA:
IDEAA and UDL Review Statement:

This course promotes equity and celebrates student diversity through course objectives, content, and pedagogical practice. This course aims to equip students of any level or ability with the English language skills that are needed to succeed in their school, career, and community. 

This course supplies students with the vocabulary necessary for daily living, adult “survival” situations or life skills in areas such as housing, transportation, job safety, emergencies, and general health. 

In this course, students enjoy OER resources, small class sizes, opportunities to engage in meaningful conversation with instructors and their peers, and access to campus resources such as Student Services, Financial Aid, and Counseling services.
