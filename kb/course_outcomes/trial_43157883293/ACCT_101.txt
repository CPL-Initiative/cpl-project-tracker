Catalog Course Description
This is an introductory accounting course covering the entire accounting cycle including transaction analysis, journalizing, posting, and preparation of financial statements. This course may be offered in a distance education format.

Objectives
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:

Identify and properly use basic accounting terminology.

State the fundamental accounting equation in the T-account format and label the debit, credit, plus and minus sides.

Analyze business transactions using the rules of debit and credit in a double-entry accounting system and record in the appropriate journal.

Post from the journal to the general ledger.

Prepare three basic financial statements: balance sheet, income statement, statement of owner’s equity.
Journalize and post adjusting, closing, and reversing entries.

Reconcile a bank statement and prepare and record journal entries for the reconciling items.

Prepare and record journal entries to establish and maintain a petty cash fund.

Calculate employee earnings, determine deductions from gross pay, and calculate net pay. Prepare journal entries to record payroll.

Analyze and record sales and purchase transactions of a merchandising business under both periodic and perpetual inventory systems.

Understand and properly use the sales, purchases, cash receipts and cash payments special journals.

IDEAA:
IDEAA and UDL Review Statement:

This course connects the goal of equity, diversity, and inclusion to accounting. Students will be exposed to resources that connect them to accounting and finance organizations serving diverse groups, like NABA (The National Association of Black Accountants). This course will emphasize the importance of diversity, equity, and inclusion through the utilization of examples of diverse businesses with unique perspectives, inclusive of people with various backgrounds and expertise. Furthermore, students will be invited to guest speaker events representing professionals in the field from various racial, socioeconomic, cultural, and experiential backgrounds.
