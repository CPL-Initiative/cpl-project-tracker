Catalog Course Description
This course is an introduction to microorganisms, including bacteria, viruses, protozoans, fungi, and helminths. Topics covered include the general properties, characteristics, and classification of microbes, microbial identification and control, genetics and biotechnology, physiology, metabolism, and ecology. Also discussed are immunity and the medical impact of microbial diseases. The lecture portion of this course may be offered in a distance education format.

Objectives
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:
List significant microbiologists and describe their notable scientific contributions.

Discuss methods of taxonomy.

Handle live microbes with safe aseptic technique skills.

Demonstrate understanding of the scientific method by performing controlled ecperiments and analyzing the results.

Inoculate sterile media with live microbes and analyze their subsequent growth.
Prepare wet mounts and smears of microbial samples.
Demonstrate a number of differential microbial stains, including the Gram stain, acid fast stain, endospore stain, and capsule stain.
Compare and contrast typical eukaryotic and prokaryotic cell structure and function.
Discuss atypical bacteria such as Rickettsia, Chlamydia, Mycobacterium and Mycoplasma.
Compare and contrast bacteria, viruses and prions.
Identify metabolic pathways and types of metabolism, including cellular respiration, fermentation, anaerobic respiration, and photosynthesis.
Compare different types of enzymes and enzyme regulatory mechanisms.
Recognize the different categories of organic compounds and discuss their role in living cells.
Discuss in detail the replication of bacterial DNA.
Contrast different types of mutations and their effects to the individual and the population.
List different types of genetic transfer in bacteria.
Discuss in detail transcription and translation in prokaryotic cells.
Describe the lytic and lysogenic cycles of viruses.
Describe the theory and practice of modern biotechology procedures, including gel electrophoresis, immunoblotting, polymerase chain reaction, recombination, and gene therapy.
Compare and contrast the significant features of specific and non-specific immunity.
Discuss the signs, symptoms, pathogenesis and treatment of certain microbial diseases.
Recognize typical epidemiological patterns.
Describe the role of microbes in the environment, medicine, and industry.
Student Learning Outcomes
Broader than Objectives, SLOs are often comprised of what the student can do as a result of combining several course objectives.

Aseptic Techniques:  Upon successful completion of the course, a student should be able to properly use aseptic techniques for the transfer and handling of microorganisms and instruments.


2. Methods:  Upon successful completion of the course, a student should be able to use appropriate microbiological media and analytical methods to identify a specific bacterial species.


3. Interpret:  Upon successful completion of the course, a student should be able to interpret a sequence of genetic code.


4. Scientific Skills: Upon successful completion of the course, students will be able to use scientific inquiry skills related to hypothesis, prediction, assumption, interpretation and evaluation.

Outside-of-Class Assignments
Typical Assignment
After selected videos are shown, students are asked to form discussion groups and analyze the information it contained and summarize in writing the key points made in the presentation.
Several assignments in laboratory involve microbial “unknowns”, the identity of which the student must deduce from the results of morphological and metabolic tests.  A written report is turned in of their findings.
Case studies often serve as a point of group discussions.  Students are asked to apply course information to solve or explain the case.
On most lecture and lab exams, there are essay questions which the students must answer in complete sentences.
Students are introduced to microbiological resources on the Internet through an assignment in which they visit various websites to find information on specific topics. The validity of Internet sources is discussed.

Microbiology promotes equity, diversity, and inclusion through discussion of the contributions in the field by scientists of different genders and ethnicities.
