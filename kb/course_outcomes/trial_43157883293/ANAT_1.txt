Catalog Course Description
This course offers a systematic hands-on approach to the anatomy of the human body. It covers the structural organization of the human body: gross and microscopic anatomy of the integumentary, skeletal, muscular, nervous, endocrine, cardiovascular, lymphatic, respiratory, digestive, urinary, and reproductive systems, from cellular to organ system levels of organization. Human cadaver prosections and/or mammalian dissections are used in conjunction with models and new technology. This course is intended for nursing, kinesiology, physical therapy, radiologic technology, respiratory therapy, dental hygiene, surgical technology, physical therapy, and other allied health related majors. It may be taken concurrently with Physiology 1. The lecture portion of this course may be offered in a distance education format.
Course Content
Lecture Content:

Lecture Course Content:

In this course, students will study the microscopic and gross anatomical structures of the major human organ systems. Emphasis will be placed on gross anatomy, however, the embryological origins of tissues and effects of aging and pathologies may also be discussed. Particular attention will be paid to the relationship between cell/tissue structure and function.

Introduction: Anatomy vs. Physiology, levels of structural organization, anatomical terminology and surface anatomy.
Cells: Plasma membrane structure and membrane transport, organelle structure and functions; mitosis/meiosis
Histology: Structure, location, and functions of the four main tissue types (epithelial, connective, muscular, and nervous) and structure/function of membranes.
Integumentary System: Major cell types, layers of skin; epidermal derivatives
Skeletal System:  Osseous tissue, formation of bone, fractures, and structure/function/location of joints.
Muscular System: Characteristics of muscle tissue; 3 muscle types; organization of skeletal muscle tissue; physiology of muscle tissue, kinesiology, and action/origin/insertion of 106 muscles.
Cardiovascular System: Structure and function of major blood cells, major structures and blood vessels of the heart, blood flow through the heart, types of vessels, and identification of certain arteries/veins.
Lymphatic System: Structure and function of major lymphatic vessels, tissues, and organs.
Digestive System: Structure and function of major organs of the GI tract, emphasis on histological differences between the major regions of the alimentary canal.
Respiratory System: Structure and function of the major components of the upper and lower respiratory tract, development of the  respiratory system
Urinary System: Gross anatomy of the urinary system, structure of the nephron, formation of urine, and development of the kidney.
Endocrine System: Location and function of major endocrine glands and their secretions, histology of select endocrine glands.
Reproductive System: Structure and function of major male and female reproductive organs, histology of select organs, spermatogenesis, oogenesis, and hormonal regulation of the female cycle.
Development: Major events in embryonic development from fertilization to birth, discussion of effects of pregnancy and childbirth on the mother, and complications of pregnancy/labor.
Nervous System: Nervous tissue, organization of the nervous system, structure and function of major brain/spinal cord regions/structures, specific nerves and their innervations, major pathways to and from the brain, special senses with emphasis on the ear and eye.
Comparison of normal versus diseased, injured, or age-related structural changes in the above organ systems.

 Lab Course Content:

Note: Student lab work consists of histology slides, models, x-rays, and other mammalian dissections including pig hearts/kidneys, sheep or cow eyes, and sheep brains. Cat dissections will include the muscular, digestive, respiratory, urinary, cardiovascular, and reproductive systems.

Introduction to the Lab and Microscopy: Lab safety, protocols, microscope parts, and protocol for usage of microscope.
Language of Anatomy: Anatomical terminology, directional terms, body cavities, and body planes.
Tissues: Microscopic identification of epithelial, connective, muscular, and nervous tissues, along with discussion of locations and functions.
Integumentary system: Identification of major skin structures, layers, and derivatives on models and slides.
Skeletal system: Identification of major bones and bony landmarks on bones and X-rays, observation of differences between osseous tissues under microscope and on models.
Muscular system: Identification of 100 muscles on models, cats, and prosections along with the actions, origins, and insertions of these muscles; histology of muscle tissue and structures under the microscope and on models.
Cardiovascular system: Identification of heart structures and blood vessels on models, cats, pig hearts, and prosections; identification of formed elements under the microscope; artery and vein histology.
Respiratory system: Identification of respiratory system structures on models, cats, and prosections.
Digestive system: Identification of major GI tract organs and structures on models, cats, and prosections; histology of select organs.
Urinary system: Identification of major urinary tract organs and structures on models, cats, pig kidneys, and prosections; histology of select organs.
Reproductive System: Identification of major male and female reproductive organs and structures on models, cats, and prosections; histology of select organs.
Endocrine System: Identification of major endocrine glands and their secretions; histology of select glands.
Nervous system: Identification of major structures/regions of the brain and spinal cord along with select functions; identification of select nerves and their innervations; histology of select nervous tissues/structures.
Special senses: Identification of major ear and eye structures; histology of the retina and cochlea.
Objectives
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:

Identify cellular structures and explain their respective functions.

Describe the basic tissues of the body and their location and outline their functions.

Identify and describe the major gross and microscopic anatomical components of the integumentary system.

Identify and describe the major gross and microscopic anatomical components of the skeletal system.

Identify and describe the major gross and microscopic anatomical components of the muscular system and outline their functional roles in body movements.

Identify and describe the major gross and microscopic anatomical components of the nervous system and outline their functional roles in communication, control, and integration.

Identify and describe the major gross and microscopic anatomical components of the eye and ear and explain their functional roles in vision, hearing and equilibrium. 

Identify and describe the major gross and microscopic anatomical components of the endocrine system. List the major hormones, their target organs and effects.

Identify and describe the major gross and microscopic anatomical components of the cardiovascular system and outline their transport roles.

Identify and describe the major gross and microscopic anatomical components of the lymphatic system.

Identify and describe the major gross and microscopic anatomical components of the respiratory system and explain their functional roles in breathing/ventilation.

Identify and describe the major gross and microscopic anatomical components of the digestive system and explain their functional roles in digestion and absorption.

Identify and describe the major gross and microscopic anatomical components of the urinary system and explain their functional roles.

Identify and describe the major gross and microscopic anatomical components of the reproductive system and explain their functional roles in reproduction and inheritance.

Describe the anatomical changes that occur in a limited number of disease states.

Outline the development of the major body systems, and the normal changes that occur with aging.

Student Learning Outcomes
Broader than Objectives, SLOs are often comprised of what the student can do as a result of combining several course objectives.

Identify gross and microscopic anatomy and explain interactions of the major organ systems of the body.

Interrelate structure and function at the various levels of organization in the human body.

Define and apply medical terminology associated with the study of human anatomy.

Apply an understanding of anatomy to describe changes that occur with disease, trauma, and aging.

Outside-of-Class Assignments
Typical Assignment
Study Guides: Students must use problem solving skills and critical thinking to answer, in writing, in-depth questions and specific aspects of anatomical systems, disease states, and physiological functions of select systems.
Short Essay Questions: During exams students are required to answer short essay questions that may ask them to describe basic processes of the human body and comparisons of structure and function of organs/tissues.
Cat Dissections: Students work in groups using surgical tools to dissect out skeletal muscles, vessels, and major organs. Tests student's communication, problem solving, and critical thinking skills.
