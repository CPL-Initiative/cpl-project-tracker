Catalog Course Description
This course covers the study of embryology and histology of oral structural formation, clinical recognition of normal oral structures, the physiological and structural functions of teeth and supporting tissues, and oral anatomy relative to proper dental hygiene procedures.

Objectives
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:

Identify the surface anatomy of the face and neck.

Apply general and specific anatomic terms to the orofacial region.

Explain the embryologic development of the fetus.

Recognize the unique histology of the oral region.

Discuss dental anatomy.

Explain morphology and function of teeth.

Define occlusion.

Explain tooth eruption.

Student Learning Outcomes
Broader than Objectives, SLOs are often comprised of what the student can do as a result of combining several course objectives.

Typical Assignment
Using the assigned text book and other reading and research materials, write a short essay to explain the etiological factors of tooth decay, how enamel, dentin and pulp react to plaque acids and their individual physiologic responses.  Also include two of your ideas on how best to deal with these in a clinical setting.
In tooth development there is a sequence of events.  Write an essay explaining the development of a tooth. Include 3 possible interruptions in this pattern and what may be presented clinically to the hygienist.  i.e. Ameliogenises Imperfecta, and Dentinogenesis Imperfecta

IDEAA and UDL Review Statement:

The Shasta College Dental Hygiene Program promotes the equality of all people regardless of race, color, creed, religion, national origin, gender, age, marital status, disability, public assistance status, veteran status, or sexual orientation. Promoting equality, the Dental Oral Biology course (DNTL 10) explores the morphologic differences found in the primary and permanent dentitions of indigenous populations to demonstrate predictive characteristics as related to ethnicity. Discussions will reveal that though racial characteristics exist, they are not diagnostic or predictive to assume the specific sex, skin or eye color of the host but may be considered as suggestive features in determining the racial origin of the individual.

Likewise, the Shasta College Dental Hygiene Program guarantees equal access to its program to insure its student body mirror California’s diverse population. The vision is to educate future dental hygienists who will return to their communities to provide care and increase access to oral health care in these vulnerable populations.
