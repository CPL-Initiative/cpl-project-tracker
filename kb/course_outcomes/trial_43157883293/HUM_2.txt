Catalog Course Description
This course is designed to explore the humanities by examining the expression of human values, ideas, concerns, and experience through the arts, literature, media and the social sciences. The reading of important works in the humanities, written analysis, and attendance at selected performances are major requirements of this course. This course may be offered in a distance education format.

Objectives
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:

Explain what the humanities are and how they relate to each other.

Analyze in a detailed way a portion of the expressive works of humankind.

Examine, comprehend, and identify major works in the humanities. Write and discuss those works in the context of the proper historical period and with an appreciation of other expressions from different humanities areas produced in the same environment.

Student Learning Outcomes
Broader than Objectives, SLOs are often comprised of what the student can do as a result of combining several course objectives.

Analyze themes, connections, historical context, and ideas in the various areas of the Humanities, including but not limited to: visual arts, music, literature, and philosophy.

Examine three major works in the humanities from different cultures, compare and contrast the ways in which these works express the human experience.

Analyze past cultural achievements in the context of present-day intellectual expressions.

Identify and contrast the differences between primary and secondary sources.

Typical Assignment
Listen to the John Williams composed score to the movie “E.T.” Identify 3 composers of the 19th and 20th Century who may have influenced this composition.
From your reading assignments, discuss the architectural influences of Greek and Roman cultures on the design of American Government buildings of the 20th Century.
Write a 3-page paper comparing and contrasting the character Homer Simpson with the character Willy Loman.
Attend a play. Write a 3-page paper discussing some of the unifying ideas (connections) between the story, characters, costumes, set and lighting designs, and music.

IDEAA and UDL Review Statement:

This course promotes equity through critical thinking as it focuses on scientifically responsible data collection and analysis, a key component of inclusive and equitable pedagogy. Course content is committed to including academic research, analysis, and reflections from diverse scholars in the field to center structurally marginalized voices in the study of the humanities and arts. Multiple perspectives (philosophical, theatre, art, and others) bring unique voices to the material. Additionally, by using personal experiences and each student’s individual background as a framework for discussion, we will address diverse views on various topics in the course. Adding each student’s viewpoint enriches the course material and currency of the information.
