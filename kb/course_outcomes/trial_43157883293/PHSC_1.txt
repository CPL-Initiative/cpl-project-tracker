Catalog Course Description
This course covers active learning, lecture, discussion, demonstration, and lab activities in selected theories of physics and chemistry, emphasizing the conceptual basis of these theories. The course is designed for non-science majors as part of their general education requirement in science. This course is not appropriate for students who have taken college-level physics or chemistry. This course may be offered in a distance education format.

Objectives
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:

Describe the scientific method.

Perform measurements of quantities such as length, mass, and volume.

Apply Newton’s Laws of motion to simple situations; distinguish between mass and weight; and explain how action and reaction forces are related to each other.

Use the definitions of momentum, work, kinetic energy, and gravitational potential energy and the relationship between net force, work, and kinetic energy to solve problems involving conservation of energy and momentum.

Relate impulse to momentum change and work to changes in mechanical energy.

Using Newton’s Law of Gravity, explain the meaning of an inverse square force law, describe how satellites are placed into orbit, and describe why astronauts feel weightless in an orbiting spacecraft.

Applying the definitions of pressure and density, explain why pressure increases with depth in a fluid and how this gives rise to buoyancy, and apply Archimedes’ principle.

Distinguish between heat, temperature and thermal energy as distinct concepts. Use specific heat to relate heat transfer to temperature change, describe the role of heat transfer in phase changes, and identify the mechanism(s) responsible for heat transfer in a given situation.

State the law describing forces between electric charges. Distinguish between voltage and current, apply the definitions of voltage, current and resistance, and apply these ideas to predict the behavior of simple series and parallel circuits.

Describe periodic wave motion, in terms of amplitude, period, wavelength, and frequency. Distinguish between transverse and longitudinal waves; describe reflection, refraction, and interference of waves. Distinguish between traveling and standing waves, and describe simple standing wave patterns in one dimension.

Describe the properties of sound, explain the Doppler effect, and solve simple problems involving sound.

Describe electromagnetic radiation, interpret the electromagnetic spectrum, and describe sources of light.

Describe how the color of an object depends on the color of the light used to illuminate it and describe how images are formed by mirrors and lenses.

Describe and interpret the characteristics of the atomic, molecular and ionic classes of matter and identify the class to which substances belong.

Describe the characteristics of the phases of matter and the connections between the properties using the particle model.

Classify substances as elements, compounds, or mixtures.

Identify processes as physical or chemical changes.

Explain the roles of protons, electrons, and neutrons in atomic structure and apply the definitions of atomic number and mass number. Describe the experimental evidence for the nuclear model of the atom. Describe and apply the electron energy level/shell model of electronic structure.

Explain the historical organization of elements in the periodic table according to patterns in chemical and physical properties and explain the modern organization of the periodic table according to electronic structure.

Draw Lewis dot structures of atoms, ions, and simple molecules.

Distinguish between ionic and covalent bonds and define and interpret electronegativity. Describe how polarity of bonds/molecules affects properties such as solubility and boiling point.

Define and apply the mole concept and convert between weight and moles for quantities of elements and compounds.

Use chemical equations to describe reactions, balance simple chemical equations, and relate amounts of reactants and products in terms of numbers of particles and mass.

Classify chemical reactions according to type and interpret simple reactions of the various types (precipitation, oxidation-reduction, acid-base/neutralization).

Describe and interpret energy changes during chemical reactions and identify factors that affect reaction rate. Identify and interpret factors that affect if a reaction will occur.

Interpret the basic concept of chemical equilibrium and apply this to simple situations including acids and bases (distinguishing between strong and weak acids and bases), and explain and interpret pH.

Identify some of the most common functional groups and name and draw structures for simple hydrocarbons, alcohols, and other organic compounds. Identify and interpret structural isomers.

Define radioactivity, describe types of radioactive decay, define and apply half-life, interpret the mass-energy relationship, and describe nuclear fission and fusion.

Outside-of-Class Assignments
Typical Assignment
Students are presented with a variety of explanations for the phenomenon of weightlessness as experienced by astronauts in an orbiting spacecraft. (This is one of the most widely misunderstood concepts in physics.) By comparing each with established theories and empirical evidence, they must eliminate the incorrect explanations and converge on the correct one.
Students are shown a number of demonstrations related to the phenomenon of boiling.  (For example, water boiling at room temperature, freezing water by boiling it, boiling water by cooling its container, a super-cold boiling liquid, etc.)  They must then write a description of the phenomenon, finding the common threads in all of the demonstrations, eventually arriving at a satisfactory definition for boiling.
Given certain rules, based on atomic structure, students must construct molecular models for various compounds. Many models can be constructed in more than one way. Students are asked to predict how the structure of the molecule affects certain physical properties, such as vapor pressure, boiling point, etc.
