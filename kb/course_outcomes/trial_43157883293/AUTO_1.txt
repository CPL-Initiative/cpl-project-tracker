Catalog Course Description
This course is designed to cover the basic theory of electricity and magnetism, as well as areas of operation, testing, and service of vehicle batteries, switches, relays, starters and starting systems, alternators, regulators, charging systems, and light circuits. The course includes electrical theory, repair procedures, and laboratory tasks. This course, along with the Introduction to Hybrid and Electric Vehicle Technology course, is designed to prepare students to become ASE certified in area A-6 and is required for Automotive majors. This course may be offered in a distance education format.

Objectives
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:
General Electrical Principles
Define current, voltage and resistance.
Define the two theories of current flow.
Define Ohm's Law.
Define semiconductors and diodes.
Define series and parallel circuits.
Perform troubleshooting on electrical circuits.
Perform troubleshooting with voltmeter, ohmmeter, and ammeter.

Explain and discuss voltage drop testing.


Battery Diagnosis and Service

Explain battery state-of-charge test; determine needed service.

Explain battery capacity (load, high-rate discharge) test; determine needed service.

Explain how to maintain or restore electronic memory functions.

Explain how to inspect, clean, fill, and replace a battery.

Discuss slow and fast battery charging.

Explain how to inspect and clean battery cables, connectors, clamps and hold-downs.

Discuss how to start a vehicle using jumper cables with a battery or auxiliary power supply.


Starting System Diagnosis and Repair

Discuss and explain starter current draw testing.

Discuss inspecting and testing starter relays and solenoids.

Explain how to remove and reinstall a starter.

Discuss starter bench testing.

Explain how to inspect, test, and replace switches.  

Evaluate a starter control circuit.


Charging System Diagnosis and Repair

Discuss and explain charging system diagnosis. 

Discuss drive belt replacement and inspection. 

Discuss voltage regulator testing and evaluation. 

Explain alternator replacement procedures.

Discuss undercharging, no-charge, and overcharge conditions.

Typical Assignment

Students will use online software and diagnose an electrical circuit. After analyzing the circuit students will select a result and the software will score their answer. Students can repeat the diagnosis until a full understanding is achieved.

IDEAA and UDL Review Statement:

This course provides opportunities for students to connect the content to their personal experiences and fosters an inclusive environment through its flexibility, inclusivity, and accessible format. The course encourages discussions within the online Canvas component where students can share their individual experiences and applications of electrical concepts. Instructors will promote equity through students’ and guest speakers' broad range of human experiences. Instructors select technical topics/readings that diverse students can relate to, and that help a variety of students feel seen and valued, as well as consider and value other student/human perspectives. Students struggling in the course will be assisted by advanced students to support student success.
