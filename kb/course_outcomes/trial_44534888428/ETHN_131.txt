Catalog Description

An interdisciplinary introduction to Native American cultures and societies from ancient origins to the present with a particular focus on the period from the 15th century to the present.  The course examines themes of identities, sovereignty, political developments, race, resistance, revitalization, ethnogenesis, assimilation, decolonization, and resilience of American Indian nations through American Indian sources, traditions, and theories.

Justification for this course
This course is intended to fulfill the CSU Area F, Ethnic Studies requirement.

Student Learning Outcomes
Learning Outcomes
Describe and compare the relationships and impact of Indian individuals, communities, nations, confederacies prior to and throughout the European colonial period by examining and applying skills learned in analyzing original, oral, primary, secondary, and tertiary sources.
Apply theory and knowledge produced by Native American communities to describe the critical events, histories, cultures, contributions, lived experiences, and struggles of those groups with a particular emphasis on agency and identity.
Analyze the intersection of race and racism as they relate to class, gender, sexuality, religion, tribal citizenship, sovereignty, language, and/or age in Native American communities in the context of federal legislation and policies.
Analyze and articulate concepts such as race and racism, racialization, ethnicity, equity, white supremacy, self-determination, decolonization, sovereignty, imperialism, settler colonialism, and anti-racism as analyzed in Native American studies.
