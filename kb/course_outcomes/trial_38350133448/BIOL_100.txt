CATALOG COURSE DESCRIPTION:
This course is an introduction to the basic principles of ecology. Emphasis is placed on the biological systems, plants, and animals of Southern California through lecture, laboratory, and field trips. Topics include the nature of the physical environment, terrestrial and aquatic ecosystems, evolution and biodiversity, species interactions, and human impacts on natural systems. This course is intended for students interested in environmental biology.

STUDENT LEARNING OBJECTIVES:
Upon successful completion of the course the student will be able to:
Upon successful completion of the course the student will be able to:
Describe the major biotic communities of San Diego County and identify key factors that influence local and other biotic communities.
Describe major taxonomic groups and explain evolutionary trends among them.
Identify dominant plants and animals from each major biotic community of San Diego County.
Explain the process of evolution by natural selection.
Define and explain major processes influencing populations, communities, and ecosystems.
Identify and describe the major types of symbioses and give examples of each.
Explain and diagram the trophic structure of typical ecosystems and the energy flow and nutrient recycling in each.
Describe impacts of human beings on natural ecosystems.
Analyze the major environmental problems facing human beings and natural systems today.
Use the scientific method and critical thinking to solve ecological/environmental problems.

B. Reading Assignments:
Reading assignments are required and may include, but are not limited to, the following:
Assigned texts related to natural history and environmental biology.
Laboratory manual.
Online resources related to local natural history and environmental biology.
Laboratory activity practice with scientific figures and tables.
C. Writing Assignments:
Writing assignments are required and may include, but are not limited to, the following:
Brief research summaries.
Reports describing visits to recommended natural history points of interest in Southern California.
Weekly laboratory reports.
D. Appropriate Outside Assignments:
Outside assignments may include, but are not limited to, the following:
Reading assignments.
Field trips to natural history points of interest in Southern California, including field observations of biomes, local ecosystems, food webs and biogeochemical cycles.
Biodiversity surveys.
Online and library research related to natural history and ecology.
Preparation for in-class oral reports.
E. Appropriate Assignments that Demonstrate Critical Thinking:
Critical thinking assignments are required and may include, but are not limited to, the following:
Analyses of local ecological issues.
Analyses of topographical maps and related data.
Laboratory activities related to measuring abiotic variables: size, volume, density, temperature, pH, and salinity.
Laboratory activities related to populations, sampling, data, and analyses.
Field work with transects and quadrats.
Laboratory work with plant distributions and random sampling.
Experimental examination of variables affecting transpiration rate in plant leaves and variables of photosynthesis.
