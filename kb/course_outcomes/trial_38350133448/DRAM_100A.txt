CATALOG COURSE DESCRIPTION:
This hands-on course focuses on beginning acting skills, scenic design and construction techniques, and theatrical production. Students participate in all aspects of theatrical production through a college theatre company. This course is designed for first-year dramatic arts majors.
REQUISITES:
Corequisite: DRAM 104A
Limitation on Enrollment: This course is not open to students with previous credit for DRAM 101

STUDENT LEARNING OBJECTIVES:
Upon successful completion of the course the student will be able to:
Upon successful completion of the course the student will be able to:
Describe the principles of the company concept in the study of dramatic art.
Identify and define the purpose of significant physical plant areas used in the Dramatic Arts Program.
Execute safe practices as related to the physical plant through practical application and testing.
Demonstrate knowledge of basic acting concepts and techniques through in-class performances, projects and assignments.
Demonstrate mastery of basic technical skills in stage scenery assignments.
Demonstrate a basic proficiency in selected phases of play production and use of production terminology.
Locate and safely use machinery and equipment necessary to the dramatic art program.
Apply basic scenery construction techniques as related to production.
Section II
1. COURSE OUTLINE AND SCOPE:
A. Course Content:
The following topics are included in the framework of the course but are not intended as limits on content. The order of presentation and relative emphasis will vary with each instructor.
Course Lecture Content
Introduction to the concept of a theatre company
Areas of assigned production responsibilities
The concept of collaboration
Trust and rapport between company members
Professional theatre etiquette
Orientation to the physical plant
The proscenium and black box stages
The scenery lab
The costuming lab
The lighting and audio control booths
Basic safety practices for the various labs and the theatre areas as well as audience safety during production
Fundamentals of acting
Scene selection
Character analysis and development
Character relationships
Defining a character’s objective and demonstrating it through performance choices
Demonstrating proper rehearsal process
Preparation for productions--basic principles of stage scenery 
Tool usage
Methods of execution, including flat and platform construction, alternative materials, stage hardware and rigging
Practicing shop safety
Understanding the fundamental requirements of the play
Design concept
Technical feasibility
Reading of simple architectural drawings.
Color theory
Basic production requirements and terminology
Job descriptions and responsibilities
Production deadlines and schedules
Chain of command
Basic theatre production construction practices
Tool usage (saws, sewing machines, lighting control boards, etc.)
Construction technique (scenery, costumes, props, etc.)
Lab and theatre safety
B. Reading Assignments:
Reading assignments are required and may include, but are not limited to, the following:
Assigned textbook related to production fundamentals.
Company handbook.
A variety of play scripts.
Handouts dealing with technical theatre and design.
C. Writing Assignments:
Writing assignments are required and may include, but are not limited to, the following:
Image concept.
Character analysis.
Reviews of theatrical productions.
D. Appropriate Outside Assignments:
Outside assignments may include, but are not limited to, the following:
Scenic design research.
Reading assignments.
Character research.
Line memorization.
Scene study.
E. Appropriate Assignments that Demonstrate Critical Thinking:
Critical thinking assignments are required and may include, but are not limited to, the following:
Solve problems related to theatre production.
Assess and refine stage design.
Assess and refine acting technique.
