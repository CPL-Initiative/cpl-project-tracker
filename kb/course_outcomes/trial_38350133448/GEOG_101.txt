CATALOG COURSE DESCRIPTION:
This course is a spatial study of the Earth’s dynamic physical systems and processes. Topics include the natural environment and nature-society interactions with an emphasis on scientific literacy and critical thinking. The course covers classification and analysis of spatial patterns of weather, climate, climate change and the enhanced greenhouse effect, the water cycle, landforms, and the distribution of living organisms. This course also addresses environmental issues in geography and sustainability. The course develops students’ spatial analysis skills using maps, Geographic Information Systems (GIS), the Global Positioning System (GPS), and remote sensing imagery. This course is intended for social science majors, sustainability majors, or anyone seeking an understanding of Earth’s physical systems and human impacts on the natural environment.

STUDENT LEARNING OBJECTIVES:
Upon successful completion of the course the student will be able to:
Upon successful completion of the course the student will be able to:
Synthesize knowledge of the Earth’s seasons to predict how this will affect environmental patterns and processes.
Synthesize spatial data and information by utilizing scientific inquiry and spatial analysis methods and tools.
Analyze and interpret spatial patterns of atmospheric, climatic, geomorphological, and biotic processes on Earth’s surface.
Analyze and evaluate the ways nature-society interactions, including the enhanced greenhouse effect and pollution, cause changes in the physical environment.
Apply spatial thinking skills to engage with local communities on climate change adaptation and mitigation, environmental justice, and sustainability.

B. Reading Assignments:
Reading assignments are required and may include, but are not limited to, the following:
Professional geographic periodicals, such as The Journal of Geography and The Annals of the Association of American Geographers.
Credible media and information sources, including internet resources, which arise during the duration of the course.
C. Writing Assignments:
Writing assignments are required and may include, but are not limited to, the following:
Term paper on a specific geographical topic such as global climate change.
Brief reports on articles published in various journals and periodicals.
Reviews of television programs or internet videos relating to geographic topics.
Reflections of human environmental impacts.
Create and maintain a blog or website related to topics in physical geography.
D. Appropriate Outside Assignments:
Outside assignments may include, but are not limited to, the following:
Instructor-led or self-guided field trips to specific areas physical geographic interest in San Diego County of physical geographic interest, or to local weather stations or other professional settings.
Participation in campus or community events related to topics in physical geography.
E. Appropriate Assignments that Demonstrate Critical Thinking:
Critical thinking assignments are required and may include, but are not limited to, the following:
Relate the application of geographic methods to the solution of geographic problems.
Compare and contrast the major physical components of one geographic region to those of other regions.
Develop and assess hypotheses about the distribution of various physical geographic phenomena.
Evaluate and discuss current periodicals and books in geography as to their relevance to course lectures.
