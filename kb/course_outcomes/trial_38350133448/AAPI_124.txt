CATALOG COURSE DESCRIPTION:
This course is an introduction to the interdisciplinary field of Asian American and Pacific Islander (AAPI) Studies.  The course explores the diverse histories and contemporary social, cultural, and political experiences of Asian Americans and Pacific Islanders. Emphasis is placed on how settler colonialism, imperialism, racial capitalism, globalization, war, American empire, citizenship, and various aspects of identity such as race, class, gender, and sexuality have shaped AAPI experiences and their social justice movements.  Students will explore the topics through the lens of equity, self-determination, decolonization, and anti-racism.  This course is intended for all students interested in Asian American and Pacific Islander Studies and Ethnic Studies.

STUDENT LEARNING OBJECTIVES:
Upon successful completion of the course the student will be able to:
Upon successful completion of the course the student will be able to:
Evaluate the impact of student and community activism on the creation of Ethnic Studies and Asian American studies.
Analyze and articulate common Ethnic Studies concepts and terms such as race and racism, racialization, ethnicity, equity, ethno-centrism, eurocentrism, white supremacy, self-determination, liberation, decolonization, sovereignty, imperialism, settler colonialism, and anti-racism in analyzing the experiences of Asian Americans and Pacific Islanders.
Apply theory and knowledge produced by AAPI communities to describe the histories, critical events, contributions, intellectual traditions, lived-experiences, and social struggles of Asian Americans and Pacific Islanders in the United States, emphasizing agency and group-affirmation.
Identify shared experiences that link Asian Americans and Pacific Islanders together, as well as the internal diversity (class, gender, sexuality, religion, spirituality, national origin, immigration status, ability, tribal citizenship, sovereignty, language, and/or age) that characterizes this diverse group.
Use the lens of anti-colonialism, anti-racism, and equity for Asian Americans and Pacific Islanders to critically analyze how (settler) colonialism, imperialism, militarism, and racial capitalism shape AAPI historical and contemporary experiences particularly their (im)migration and/or displacement and their social and political exclusion in the US.
Evaluate the impacts of federal, state, and local laws in relation to Asian immigrants, Asian Americans, and Pacific Islanders on Asian American and Pacific Islander communities, particularly with respect to family formation, ethnic community development, and identity.
Critically analyze the intersection of race and racism as they relate to class, gender, sexuality, religion, national origin, immigration status, sovereignty, language, and/or age in Asian American and Pacific Islander communities.
Critically review how struggle, resistance, racial and social justice, solidarity, and liberation, as experienced, enacted, and studied by Asian Americans and Pacific Islanders are relevant to current and structural issues such as anti-immigrant sentiment, anti-Asian violence, U.S. military industrial complex, environmental racism, and settler-colonialism.
Critically examine primary and/or secondary sources created by Asian American and Pacific Islander cultural producers–memoir, fiction, major motion picture, documentary, oral history(ies) and other lived experiences, or other art forms–to clarify how Asian Americans and Pacific Islanders challenge negative stereotypes and resist social and political marginalization in the US.
Describe and actively engage with anti-racist and anti-colonial issues, the practices and movements in AAPI communities, as well as other racial and ethnic coalitional groups for a just and equitable society.

B. Reading Assignments:
Reading assignments are required and may include, but are not limited to, the following:
Assigned textbooks focused on Asian American and Pacific Islander studies.
Primary and secondary sources including document collections, novels/fiction, memoirs/autobiographies, major motion pictures, documentaries, and oral histories.
Articles from scholarly journals such as: A. Amerasia Journal; B.Contemporary Pacific: A Journal of Island Affairs; C. Hulili: Multidisciplinary Research on Hawaiian Well-Being; D. Journal of Asian American Studies; E. MANOA: A Pacific Journal of International Writing; and, F.Journal of Southeast Asian American Education & Advancement (JSAAEA).
Articles from Asian American periodicals/magazines such as: A. Asian American Policy Review; B. Asian American Law Journal; C. Asian Pacific American Law Journal; D. AAPI nexus: Asian Americans and Pacific Islanders, Policy Practice and Community; and E. Hyphen: Asian America Unabridged.
Reports from AAPI serving organizations and institutions such as Stop AAPI Hate and Asian Americans Advancing Justice.
C. Writing Assignments:
Writing assignments are required and may include, but are not limited to, the following:
Expository essays critically analyzing how settler colonialism, imperialism, racial capitalism, globalization, war, American empire, citizenship, and various aspects of identity such as race, class, gender, and sexuality have shaped AAPI experiences and their social justice movements.
Brief descriptions and analyses of persons, places, institutions, and events of historical significance to Asian American and Pacific Islander studies.
Analytical reviews of a book, major motion picture, or novel dealing with the contemporary AAPI experience.
Research papers/argumentative essays on a specific topic in the AAPI experience, or an AAPI-related topic covered during the semester. Biographical essays synthesizing primary sources, secondary sources, and oral history focused on the AAPI experience.
Newspaper/ Magazines articles and op-eds about current issues facing AAPI communities
Community event proposals
D. Appropriate Outside Assignments:
Outside assignments may include, but are not limited to, the following:
Read, take notes, and study the assigned texts.
Prepare for objective exams, in-class essays, and class discussions related to the AAPI experience.
Research and prepare for in-class and on-line multimedia presentations.
Complete take-home essays and papers that critically analyze topics in AAPI studies.
Conduct oral history interview(s) to document and contextualize the lived experiences of AAPI community members.
Participate in / Conduct community-based participatory research
Create and maintain a blog, a newsletter, a student newspaper covering AAPI – related school/ community activities, events, and issues.
Organize AAPI – related panel discussions/ workshops/ events
Engage in community service-learning activities organized by the instructor and local AAPI-serving organizations to understand more about issues impacting AAPI communities and AAPI community activism.
E. Appropriate Assignments that Demonstrate Critical Thinking:
Critical thinking assignments are required and may include, but are not limited to, the following:
Expository essays comparing and contrasting the experiences of different indigenous Pacific Islanders of the Hawaiian Islands, Guam (Chamorro), and/or American Samoa in at least two of the following topics: settler colonialism, resistance, self-determination, U.S. imperialism, the U.S. military industrial complex, and citizenship.
In-person or on-line multi-media presentations describing and analyzing the impact of immigration policies and anti-Asian laws on Asian American communities.
Analyze primary sources documenting anti-Asian sentiment, violence, and rhetoric, including newspaper articles, images in pop culture magazines and other media, and political speeches, and write anti-racism op-ed articles.
Research papers and argumentative essays analyzing the long-term and short-term impacts of the U.S. militarism and militourism on Pacific Islands
Critical reflective essays evaluating Asian American and Pacific Islander engagement with their own intersectional identities, including generational differences, and the development of Hapa (mixed-race) and biracial/multiracial second-generation AAPI.
Critical autobiography detailing how the student’s life intersects wih AAPI history and/or how different forces shaping the experiences of Asian Americans and Pacific Islanders (such as US colonialism, imperialism, militarism, capitalism, and racism) might have also shaped student life and experience.
