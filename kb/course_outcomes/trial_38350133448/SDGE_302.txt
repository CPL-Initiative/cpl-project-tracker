CATALOG COURSE DESCRIPTION:
This course provides an orientation in the power distribution and line construction industry. Basic electrical principles and safety on the job are emphasized. Topics include basic mathematical computations, including trigonometry fundamentals, electron theory, and the fundamentals of magnetism. Students combine electrical theory with laboratory and practical applications in the course of study. This course is designed for students interested in the electric power trade and those pursuing
careers in the field, including utility, military, and commercial areas.

STUDENT LEARNING OBJECTIVES:
Upon successful completion of the course the student will be able to:
Upon successful completion of the course the student will be able to:
Demonstrate ability to manipulate mathematical computation using fractions, decimals, percent, algebra, and fundamentals of trigonometry.
Demonstrate the knowledge in using fundamental mechanics: weight, pressure, torque, power, and vectors.
Demonstrate the knowledge of electron theory as it applies to conductors, electrical potential, current, impedance, and simple circuits.
Describe the fundamentals of electricity.
Demonstrate the knowledge in applying the fundamentals of magnetism as it pertains to permanent and electromagnets, magnetic flux, and reluctance.

B. Reading Assignments:
Reading assignments are required and may include, but are not limited to, the following:
Assigned textbooks.
SDG&E Construction Standards.
SDG&E Electric Standard Practices.
Power delivery industry articles from internet sites, such as www.lineworker.com and www.tdworld.com.
Internet journals, such as www.linemantrainer.com.
State of California General Orders 95 & 128.
C. Writing Assignments:
Writing assignments are required and may include, but are not limited to, the following:
Complete assigned reports.
Summarize the electron theory and the molecular structure of matter.
Perform arithmetic calculations as assigned.
Written short essays on fundamental mechanics.
D. Appropriate Outside Assignments:
Outside assignments may include, but are not limited to, the following:
Research web sites, such as www.lineworker.com and linmenonline.com.
Prepare research reports on the magnetic circuit.
Research articles on electric current.
Tour potential work sites.
E. Appropriate Assignments that Demonstrate Critical Thinking:
Critical thinking assignments are required and may include, but are not limited to, the following:
Analyze Ohm's Law and how it relates to the electron theory.
Compare and contrast permanent magnets and electromagnets.
Identify and describe the molecular structure of matter, explaining ionization.
Analyze how weight, pressure, and force affect work output and energy.
