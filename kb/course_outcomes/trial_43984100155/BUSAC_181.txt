BUSAC 181 - Applied Accounting
Units: 3.0
Print

Description:

This beginning accounting course presents a practical approach, emphasizing small business applications. Topics include the accounting cycle for a sole proprietorship; journals and ledgers; financial statements; adjusting, closing, and reversing entries; bank reconciliation; petty cash; payroll; payroll taxes; sales and purchases; and cash receipts and cash payments. An introduction to the use of an accounting software program is also covered.

Student Learning Outcomes:

Create and maintain a basic set of accounting records involving the use of journals, ledgers, and financial statements, emphasizing small business applications.
Analyze, interpret, and accurately record the effects of a variety of business transactions in conformity to the accounting equation and the double-entry framework.
Prepare end-of-period financial statements, including income statements, statements of owner's equity, balance sheets, and statements of cash flows, by utilizing the accounting cycle and end-of-period adjustments.
Reconcile bank and depositor records using cash journals, bank statements, and other financial information.
Process petty cash payments and adjust for cash shortages and overages.
Distinguish between employer and employee payroll taxes when calculating payroll withholding and preparing payroll records.
Analyze, interpret, and accurately record the effects of a variety of business transactions in conformity to the accounting equation and the double-entry framework using manual processes and accounting software. (Laboratory)
Reconcile bank and depositor records using cash journals, bank statements, and other financial information.(Laboratory)
