Catalog Description
This interdisciplinary course is designed for students interested in meeting general education requirements in humanities. The course develops students' understanding and appreciation of humankind's cultural heritage from the Upper Paleolithic (ca. 40,000 BCE) to approximately 1400 CE.  A survey is made of the literature, philosophy, music, painting, architecture, and sculpture of both Western and non-Western civilizations.

Content
Course Lecture Content (Use outline format)
The description and characteristics of world civilizations and development of cultures from Upper Paleolithic to approximately 1400 CE, including but not limited to the cultures of Africa, Asia, Europe, and the Americas:
Upper Paleolithic
Neolithic
Ancient Middle East (Mesopotamia, Egypt), South Asia (India), East Asia (China, Japan, Southeast Asia), and the Americas
Classical Europe (Greece and Rome)
Medieval Europe, Middle East (Mesopotamia, Egypt), Near East (India), Far East (China, Japan, Southeast Asia), and the Americas
Religious variety from global polythesim to Western monotheism
Influence of developmental factors on civilization:
Geography
History
Economics
Social Structure (including issues of gender and gender preference)
Technology
Cross-cultural influences
Interdisciplinary perspective of cultural disciplines, including but not limited to interpretation, analysis, and criticism of the Arts:
Painting (e.g., cave art, on pottery, on architecture, etc.)
Sculpture (e.g., monumental, relief, freestanding, architectural, etc.)
Architecture (e.g., megalithic, monumental, ritual/religious, etc.)
Miscellaeous art forms (e.g., calligraphy, mosaic, reliquary, etc.)
Music (e.g., Greek tonal, Asian traditions, medieval forms, etc.)
Literature (e.g., oral traditions, poetry, drama, essays, etc.)
Interdisciplinary perspective of origins, forms, and content of some of the art, literature and philosophy of the various periods and cultures studied:
Religion (including but not limited to primal sacred traditions, mythologies, Native American traditions, Judaism, Christianity, Islam, Confucianism, Taoism, Hinduism, Buddhism, etc.)
Philosophies (including but not limited to ancient Greek, including the materialists, sophists, Socrates, Plato and Aristotle, etc.)
Art and Architecture (including but not limited to religious influences on temples, artistic expression, etc.)
Connections between world cultures and their art, literature, and philosophies/religions (including but not limited to examples of syncretism, etc.)
