## Rows rest on anchors

An anchor is two courses at one college: an Honors course and the base course whose code names the same subject and number once the Honors marker is struck, with a second signal at the course level (an Honors word in the title, or equal titles). An Honors M-ID gets a row to its base when every anchor lands in that base; anchors in two bases, or a doubtful anchor (an Honors number pair whose titles disagree), hold it. A mint also needs every member course anchored, one Honors M-ID per base, no CCN-coded course in the base, and no second base named by another derivation (`kb/_honors_anchors.py`; `d-honors-anchored-rows-s654`).

A row on an impure base is the base's own impurity, not this plan's fault. The anchors say where the row goes when the base is split, so the re-clustering lane can rewire it mechanically.
