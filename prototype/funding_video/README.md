# CPL Funding in Motion

A 100-second animated introduction to CPL funding for colleges: animated text, a score generated in the browser, the MAP logo's red arrow flying the whole piece and shooting down barriers to CPL between scenes, and a closing label, How CPL Funding Works, linked in the web player to the CPL funding page. It is an introduction (Sam, 2026-09-26: "a guide would be much longer and more detailed"); detailed guidance follows separately. One built page and one MP4 per scenario, from one source.

## Files

- `funding_in_motion.src.html`: THE source. The animation, the score and the arrow's flight live here once. Placeholders (`__CFG__`, `__LOGO__`, `__MAP__`, `__ARROW__`, the page chrome) are filled by `build.py`.
- `build.py`: holds `CONFIG`, one entry per scenario: the priorities scene, the appropriation's destinations (`split`), the example allocation (`ex`) and its target, the explainer link and the page chrome. `python3 prototype/funding_video/build.py` builds Scenario 1; `build.py s2` builds Scenario 2.
- `funding_in_motion.html`, `funding_in_motion_s2.html`: the built pages, every image inlined. `funding_in_motion_n1.html` and `funding_in_motion_n2.html` are the narrated drafts of Scenario 1 and Scenario 2 (below).
- `20260926_CPL_Funding_in_Motion_v4.mp4`, `20260926_CPL_Funding_in_Motion_Scenario_2_v7.mp4`: 1920×1080, 30 fps, 101 seconds, with music. Re-versioned 2026-10-01 (S313) for Sam's video round (below); Scenario 2 again on 2026-10-02 (S314) for his confirmation deadline, and as v7 the same day (S316) for the summed target (below). Scenario 1's v4 still reads "price per CPL FTES" on its target slide; its page reads "rate".
- `assets/map_wordmark.png`: the MAP wordmark, cut from Sam's logo file with the red arrow removed and the background made transparent. `assets/map_arrow.png`: the arrow.

**The voice.** `narrate.py` reads `narration_s1.json` with Kokoro-82M's Heart voice (`af_heart`, Apache-2.0), locally; the first run downloads the model (about 350 MB) from huggingface.co into `~/.cache/kokoro` (`KOKORO_DIR`). The spoken form departs from the written script where the voice needs it to: acronyms are unspaced (`CPL`, `EDD`, `FTES`) so each reads as one quick word, MAP is written `map` because capitals spell it out, and years are words because digits read as "two thousand". `phoneme_fixes` corrects what spelling cannot (unspaced `FTES` reads as the word "eftess", and each year takes a lighter stress on its twenties); every fix must fire, and `never` lists the readings Sam rejected in v2 ("stilted, especially when sounding out C-P-L... same with sounding out the year numbers"), so a run that still carries one fails. A local transcription (`faster-whisper`, model `small`) hears v3 as written, draft 4's two changed passages included, and each "CPL" takes 570 ms against v2's 702 ms.

**The cues.** Each scene in `narration_s1.json` lists its `cues`, one for each reveal a word names: `at` is where the reveal begins in the scene's film time (a count is cued where it lands, the moment its figure reads), and `word` is the phrase that names it. `cues.py` pins each cue to the onset of the phrase's first word and writes it into the layout as one of the scene's `anchors`. The onsets come from `narration_s1_words.json`, which `cues.py --listen` hears from the committed track and stamps with the track's SHA-256, the two models and the date. faster-whisper `small` transcribes each scene's clip as the record of what the voice said. A forced alignment of the scene's text against the same clip (wav2vec2-base-960h, CTC, a quantized ONNX model from huggingface.co) places each word, because whisper's own times ran 0.1 to 0.5 s early inside a sentence and about a second early on a clip's first word; a word after a pause starts where the voice resumes. No stretch of the picture plays faster than the 90-second introduction, so where two cues sit closer in the narration than in the picture, the earlier cue moves earlier: a reveal may lead its word and never trails it. Draft 4 pins 41 cues: 35 land on their word and 38 within a quarter second (draft 3: 33 of 39, and 37). Six lead their words: four by 0.26 s or less, the title's kicker by 0.76 s, and the Support kicker by 1.25 s ahead of "Every priority comes with recommended strategies", so the six strategies keep the introduction's pace. Four cues carry a `skip` and its reason. The picture shows the title's years and the funding kicker before the words that name them, about 3 and 3.7 s early. The funding line "set aside", pinned, would hurry the count, so the counter takes the cue and the line follows its words by 1.1 s. The Timing heading opens its scene: the words that name it also name the release dates, which take the cue.

**The clock.** The narration drives it: `narrate.py` lays each scene out as a lead-in (1.0 s; 1.4 s for the title), its clip, and air (0.8 s; 3.4 s at the close), per `layout` in the JSON, and writes the voice as one track on that timeline (`narration_s1.mp3`) with the layout beside it (`narration_s1_layout.json`, committed because the page and the MP4 are built from it). In the page, `ft()` maps the player's time to the film's through knots, each scene's start and each of its anchors, and stretches the picture evenly between two knots. A cued reveal lands on its word, and the reveals between two cues keep their order and spacing; `nt()` maps the other way for the chapters and the score. Each barrier keeps its own pace, 1.8 times the introduction's (`BAR`), around the moment the clock lands its hit, so a barrier beside a heading that types in with the voice still plays as a wink; the arrow's recoil and the score's shot and pop follow the barrier. The 90-second introductions have no layout, so their film time is the player's time. Compared frame by frame against the previous build on 2026-09-29, each renders all 2,701 frames the same, with the same 86 arrow keyframes and 1,293 score events; draft 4 rebuilt them byte for byte, and all 2,701 frames of each match in headless Chromium.

**The score.** Under narration it is a bed, 14 dB down, and dips 6 dB more under the voice (`duck()`), easing over 0.4 s at each edge. Its sections keep their tempo and arrive just ahead of the scenes they belong to, repeating more of their phrase where a scene runs longer; every rule that named a bar of the 90-second score (the crash into each section, the swell out of it, the bells from the middle of B) now names a position within its section. Measured against the previous source, the introductions' score keeps all 1,293 events with the same times, pitches and voices; four gains differ in the seventeenth decimal place, from floating-point rounding.

**Captions.** Each scene's text is cut at sentences, and a long sentence at its commas, into caption cues of about two lines, timed by phoneme count and snapped to the pauses the voice leaves; they write MAP where the voice reads "map". The page shows them over the stage with a Hide captions control; the MP4 carries them as a subtitle track a viewer can switch off. The closing scene says the voice is synthetic.

## Rendering

`render.sh [s2|n1]` re-renders an MP4, in about 5 minutes; the narrated cut took 23 on 2026-09-29, 14 of them for its three-minute score. It fetches the two fonts from npm, renders the score offline to WAV, captures every frame in headless Chromium, and encodes with ffmpeg (libx264 and aac). It writes the file the page's Download link names (`mp4` in `build.py`'s `CONFIG`), so a re-version takes its name there, once: the original date code and a version suffix. Playwright's bundled ffmpeg has neither codec; point `FFMPEG` at the `imageio-ffmpeg` wheel's binary:

    FFMPEG=$(python3 -c "import imageio_ffmpeg as f; print(f.get_ffmpeg_exe())") bash prototype/funding_video/render.sh
    FFMPEG=... bash prototype/funding_video/render.sh s2

The explainer links Scenario 1's guide by default and Scenario 2's when it shows Scenario 2 (`?scenario=Scenario%202`). Before production, change `BASE` in `build.py` to the public repo's address, rebuild and re-render.
